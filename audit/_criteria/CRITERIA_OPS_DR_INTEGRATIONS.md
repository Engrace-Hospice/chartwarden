**Usage rules for module auditors:**

- Each numbered item is a normative requirement or invariant extracted verbatim-in-substance from the source, with the source section cited. Where the source uses MUST/never/required language, the item is binding. Where the source documents an accepted risk or trade-off, the item records that acceptance — a module that "fixes" an accepted risk unilaterally is a deviation, not an improvement, unless the lead approves.
- Items tagged **OPEN-###** are explicitly deferred in the source documents. Do **not** flag a module as defective for not resolving an OPEN item; flag it only if it forecloses or contradicts the open decision.
- Exact numbers (hours, days, percentages, retry counts) are normative as written. A module that states a different number for the same control contradicts the criteria and must be cited.
- Cross-document discrepancies known at pack time are listed in §5 ("Known Cross-Document Discrepancies"). Where a module aligns with one of two conflicting source values, escalate to the audit lead rather than recording a module defect.

## 1. DR/BCP REQUIREMENTS (DR-###)

### 1.1 Scope, Authority, Governance

- **DR-001** (DR §1.1) The DR/BCP document satisfies HIPAA Security Rule §164.308(a)(7) Contingency Plan and is the source-of-truth for availability/recovery obligations to customer hospice agencies. Customer service-level commitments reference this document **by version**.
- **DR-002** (DR §1.2) In-scope: all WardenX-operated services (backend, workers, PostgreSQL, Valkey, MinIO, MLflow, observability); backups/replication/restoration; vendor outages (Anthropic, DoseSpot, Twilio, Postmark, Stripe, MAC portals, Medicaid portals, Xero, Gusto) where they impact customer operations; BCP guidance to agencies; HIPAA breach triggers.
- **DR-003** (DR §1.3) Out-of-scope: customer agencies' internal IT continuity; agencies' own paper-fallback execution (WardenX provides forms and guidance; agency executes); WardenX corporate office continuity; archival beyond compliance retention (Architecture §24).
- **DR-004** (DR §2.1) ICS-style roles: Incident Commander, Technical Lead, Communications Lead, Compliance Officer, per-agency Customer Liaison. At solo-dev phase all roles converge to one person — an **acknowledged SPOF**. Backup contact and contracted relief are **required before WardenX has more than 3 customer tenants**.
- **DR-005** (DR §2.1) Compliance Officer is interim (Fabrice); **contracted compliance counsel required before first customer**.
- **DR-006** (DR §2.1) Each customer agency designates one staff member as primary incident point of contact; their backup is identified in tenant settings.
- **DR-007** (DR §2.2) On-call coverage by phase: (a) Engrace-only: 24/7 single operator with pre-documented blackout periods covered by **automated read-only degraded mode prepared in advance**; (b) 2–3 tenants: contracted on-call relief covering 8 PM–8 AM minimum; (c) 4+ tenants or first enterprise contract: formal rotation with ≥2 engineers.
- **DR-008** (DR §2.3) Severity classification is mandatory at declaration; reclassification allowed. Exact targets:
  - **SEV-1** (patient care impacted; data loss; PHI exposure; total outage): IC engaged within **5 min**, Tech Lead within **15 min**; initial status within **15 min**; updates every **30 min** until resolved.
  - **SEV-2** (significant degradation; partial outage; single critical feature down; e.g., DoseSpot outage, errors >5%): IC within **15 min**; initial status within **30 min**; updates every **1 hr**.
  - **SEV-3** (minor degradation, workarounds available): IC within **1 hr** business hours or **8 AM next day** off-hours; updates every **4 hr**.
  - **SEV-4** (no customer impact): next business day; internal comms only.
- **DR-009** (DR §2.4) Authority matrix is binding: kill-switch activation (IC for SEV-1; IC or Tech Lead SEV-2; Tech Lead SEV-3/4); failover to DR site (IC only, SEV-1/2 only); customer notification ownership; regulator notification by Compliance Officer (after IC + counsel review for SEV-1); restore-from-backup requires IC approval at SEV-1 (Tech Lead executes); forensic firm engagement requires IC + counsel.

### 1.2 Recovery Objectives (RTO/RPO) — Global and Per-Tier

- **DR-010** (DR §3.1) Five service tiers determine RTO/RPO and recovery sequencing. Tier assignments of named services are normative:
  - **Tier 1 — Critical patient care: RTO 1 hour / RPO 5 minutes.** Backend API (encounter save, eMAR, orders), PostgreSQL primary read+write, Valkey (session), Socket.IO (real-time alerts), **Module 18 EVV**, **Module 19 On-Call**.
  - **Tier 2 — Clinical operations: RTO 4 hours / RPO 15 minutes.** HearthNote Layer 1, HOPE Engine, IDG, Care Plan, Documents, FHIR Gateway **read**.
  - **Tier 3 — Revenue & Reporting: RTO 24 hours / RPO 1 hour.** Module 23 (Billing-Medicare), Module 24 (Billing-Medicaid), Module 25 (Remittance), Module 33 (Reporting), Module 30 (HQRP).
  - **Tier 4 — Augmenting & Optional: RTO 72 hours / RPO 24 hours.** HearthNote Layer 2 (LLM), DocLens, AI predictions, Telehealth, ML training.
  - **Tier 5 — Cold archival: RTO 1 week / RPO 24 hours.** Archived audit partitions, retired ML models, archived intake artifacts.
- **DR-011** (DR §3.2) Tier rationale invariants: eMAR is Tier 1 and billing Tier 3 (medication delay = safety event; 24-hour 837I delay = cash-flow event); HOPE documentation-at-visit is Tier 2 while CMS batch submission is Tier 3 (can wait 24 hours without regulatory consequence); telehealth is Tier 4 (reschedulable / phone fallback).
- **DR-012** (DR §3.3) Every incident records **actual** RTO and RPO achieved; post-incident review compares actuals to targets; trends drive tier adjustment, infrastructure investment, and procedure optimization.
- **DR-013** (DR §3.4) Customer-facing SLAs are deliberately looser than internal targets: Tier 1 = 2 hr RTO / 15 min RPO; Tier 2 = 8 hr / 1 hr; Tier 3 = 48 hr / 4 hr; Tier 4 = no SLA. Modules must not promise customers tighter than these.

### 1.3 Backup Architecture

- **DR-014** (DR §4.1.1) PostgreSQL backups via pgBackRest with **three repositories**: repo1 local (retention: **14 full / 7 diff**), repo2 backup VM (**30 full / 14 diff**), repo3 off-site S3-compatible (**90 full / 30 diff**). All repos **AES-256-CBC** encrypted with **distinct sealed keys per repo**; compression zstd level 3; process-max 4; bundling on.
- **DR-015** (DR §4.1.2) Backup schedule: **full Sunday 02:00 UTC**; **differential Wednesday 02:00 UTC**; **incremental daily 02:00 UTC except Sun/Wed** (retention 6 local / 13 backup VM / 29 off-site); **WAL archiving continuous** (every segment), retained **14 days local / 30 days backup VM / 90 days off-site**.
- **DR-016** (DR §4.1.2) Resulting recovery granularity: PITR to **any point within the last 14 days**; daily snapshots up to **90 days** off-site.
- **DR-017** (DR §4.1.3) `archive_timeout = 60s` forces WAL switch under low activity, **capping RPO at 60 seconds**.
- **DR-018** (DR §4.1.4) Every backup verified **within 1 hour of completion** (pgBackRest checksum verify + cron job parsing `info` output); any backup missing or invalid → **SEV-3 alert**; monthly full restore-to-sandbox drill (see DR-035).
- **DR-019** (DR §4.2.1) MinIO per-bucket strategy is normative:
  - `tenants/<org>/documents/` — versioning ON; real-time async replication to backup + off-site weekly.
  - `tenants/<org>/generated/` — versioning ON; backup weekly.
  - `tenants/<org>/intake/` — versioning OFF (ephemeral); **no replication; 30-day lifecycle**.
  - `tenants/<org>/claims/` — versioning ON; **Object Lock COMPLIANCE mode, 7 years**; real-time replication + off-site **daily**.
  - `tenants/<org>/cost-reports/` — versioning ON; **Object Lock COMPLIANCE, 7 years**; real-time replication + off-site daily.
  - `tenants/<org>/exports/` — versioning ON; backup weekly.
  - `tenants/<org>/recordings/` — versioning ON; lock per consent terms; backup daily.
  - `platform/form-templates/` — versioning ON; backup daily + off-site weekly.
  - `platform/ml-models/` — versioning ON; backup daily + off-site monthly.
- **DR-020** (DR §4.2.2–4.2.3) MinIO site replication primary↔backup (active-active, replicating delete/delete-marker/replica-metadata-sync), health verified daily. Off-site receives: **daily** incremental of `claims/` and `cost-reports/`; **weekly** full of `documents/`; **monthly** full of `platform/`.
- **DR-021** (DR §4.3) Valkey is **not the system of record** — all critical data is in PostgreSQL. Valkey holds sessions (loss → re-login), cache (loss → cold start), BullMQ queues (loss → possible job re-execution), Socket.IO pub/sub (ephemeral by design). Persistence: AOF `appendfsync everysec` (**worst case 1 sec loss on crash**); RDB save rules `900 1 / 300 10 / 60 10000`; **snapshot every 15 min copied to backup VM**; AOF rewrite daily in low-traffic window.
- **DR-022** (DR §4.3) **INVARIANT:** because BullMQ jobs may re-execute after Valkey loss, **all BullMQ consumers must be idempotent** — verified in the Module 1 test suite. Every module's jobs inherit this requirement.
- **DR-023** (DR §4.4) Application-state backup: container images in GHCR plus **monthly pull-and-archive snapshots to backup VM**; source in GitHub plus **weekly mirror to wardenx-backup-01**; build artifacts re-buildable (not separately backed up); SOPS-encrypted secrets in Git with **age keys held offline in two locations**; configs immutable per deploy, backed by Proxmox VM snapshots.
- **DR-024** (DR §4.5) Proxmox PBS VM backups: app/worker VMs **weekly, 4-week retention**; database VMs **daily snapshot (7 daily, 4 weekly)** in addition to pgBackRest; MinIO VM weekly/4 weeks; backup VM snapshot mirrored off-site **monthly, 3 months**; edge/obs VMs weekly/4 weeks. **VM-level backups are for fast bare-metal recovery; pgBackRest and MinIO replication are the authoritative data backups.**
- **DR-025** (DR §4.6) All backups encrypted at rest; **three keys per encryption boundary** (primary, backup, off-site) so one compromise doesn't compromise all; keys in SOPS-in-Git plus sealed envelope in fire safe; **annual key rotation** with old keys retained for re-decryption per retention matrix; key destruction follows the destruction-certification workflow (Architecture §24.2).
- **DR-026** (DR §4.7) Restore-verification cadence is normative: pgBackRest backup integrity **daily (automated)**; pgBackRest full restore-to-sandbox **monthly**; MinIO replication lag **continuous, must be < 1 min** with object counts matching; MinIO object restore **monthly — random sample of 100 objects, bit-identical to primary**; Proxmox VM restore **monthly — random VM boots cleanly on alternate node**; **off-site full PG restore quarterly, timed**; **off-site age-key SOPS decryption test quarterly**.

### 1.4 Replication Architecture

- **DR-027** (DR §5.1) PostgreSQL streaming replication primary→replica is **asynchronous** (`synchronous_standby_names = ''`); lag **<1 sec typical, <30 sec worst case** during write spikes; losing up to 30 sec of writes in a failover is an explicitly accepted cost.
- **DR-028** (DR §5.1) Replication lag alerting: **>60 sec → SEV-3; >5 min → SEV-2; >30 min → SEV-1 (replication broken)**. Replication slot age monitored (unconsumed slots fill WAL and crash the primary).
- **DR-029** (DR §5.2) MinIO replication RPO **typically <30 sec**, worst-case minutes during heavy upload; applications can be re-pointed to backup MinIO (write-through behavior validated in DR drill).
- **DR-030** (DR §5.3) Intentionally NOT replicated: Valkey (rebuilt on recovery), Prometheus/Loki/Tempo (observability loss acceptable), MLflow run metadata (rebuildable; model artifacts ARE backed up). Modules must not assume replication of these.

### 1.5 Failover Procedures and Time Targets

- **DR-031** (DR §6.1) PG primary→replica promotion triggers: primary unresponsive **>5 min** with no recovery sign; disk failure; corruption beyond easy recovery; network partition. **Target: 15 minutes from decision to running.** Procedure includes write halt, promotion, app reconfiguration, rolling backend restart, write verification, replica re-establishment.
- **DR-032** (DR §6.1.1) Application DB reconfiguration methods in preference order: (A) **PgBouncer host swap (preferred)** — config reload, app unaware; (B) DNS `db-primary.wardenx.internal` with **TTL 30 sec**; (C) config/env reload with rolling restart.
- **DR-033** (DR §6.1.2) Fresh replica rebuild: **~2–4 hours** typical; system operational but **without failover redundancy** until complete.
- **DR-034** (DR §6.2) Valkey failure recovery target **10 minutes**; rebuild from snapshot on backup VM; active users forced to re-login (accepted); **data loss expectation up to 15 min** (snapshot age + AOF replay). Single Valkey instance is an accepted risk (state is ephemeral by design); Sentinel/Cluster is a post-Phase-1 improvement (see OPEN-010).
- **DR-035** (DR §6.3) MinIO failover target **20 minutes**; reconfigure apps to backup MinIO with rolling restart. **Data loss expectation: up to 1 hour for documents (Tier 2); near-zero for claims/cost-reports** (real-time replication).
- **DR-036** (DR §6.4) Backend instance loss: load balancer (Pangolin) auto-removes on health-check failure — no manual action for client traffic; SEV-3 alert; manual intervention **<5 min**; replacement VM from template **~10 min**. **Data loss: none — backend is stateless.** (Modules must keep backend stateless to preserve this property.)
- **DR-037** (DR §6.5) Edge (Pangolin) failure target **30 minutes**; restore config from Git, Let's Encrypt certs from sealed backup; edge DNS records at **TTL 60 sec**. Single edge VM is accepted risk for Engrace-only phase; **two edge VMs active-passive required before multi-tenant launch** (see OPEN-011).
- **DR-038** (DR §6.6) Network/VLAN failure target **15–60 minutes** depending on cause; while network is broken, **all clinical operations go to paper** per DR §11.

### 1.6 Recovery Procedures

- **DR-039** (DR §7.1) Point-in-Time Recovery (corruption, destructive query, ransomware): target **1–4 hours**. Mandatory steps: stop all writes; take a **forensic full backup of the corrupted state before restoring over it** (tag `pre-recovery-forensic`); restore `--type=time` to a timestamp just before corruption; rebuild replication (new timeline); **notify customers of any data loss between corruption point and recovery point**.
- **DR-040** (DR §7.2) Object recovery order: (1) object **versioning** (enabled on all PHI buckets) is first line of defense; (2) restore/mirror from backup MinIO; (3) off-site restore if both affected (which segmentation should prevent).
- **DR-041** (DR §7.3) VM bare-metal restore from PBS: **15–30 min per VM**.
- **DR-042** (DR §7.4) Full application-stack rebuild (all VMs lost, data backups intact): **6–12 hours assuming hardware available**; bring-up in dependency order **PG → Valkey → MinIO → Backend → Workers → Edge**; secrets decrypted via off-site age keys; full smoke suite before traffic.
- **DR-043** (DR §7.5) Post-recovery verification checklist is mandatory before resuming customer traffic; all 17 checks must pass: `/ready` on all services; DB connections from all backends; **RLS isolation test (cross-tenant denial) passes**; audit event insert + read-back; **outbox dispatcher running, queue depth normal**; BullMQ workers connected, no stuck jobs; Socket.IO joins rooms; MinIO upload+download per tenant; DoseSpot health endpoint reachable; Anthropic test call; Twilio test SMS; **sample claim validation through Module 23 pipeline**; sample encounter save end-to-end including audit entry; **a recent backup completes (don't resume without proving new data is backed up)**; monitoring/alerting operational; no SEV-1/SEV-2 open; status page updated.

### 1.7 Backup Verification & Drills

- **DR-044** (DR §8.1) Continuous verification with alert thresholds: pgBackRest completion daily (failure → SEV-3, investigate same day); **WAL archiving backlog > 5 min → SEV-2**; **MinIO replication lag > 5 min → SEV-3, > 30 min → SEV-2**; **Valkey snapshot absent for 30 min → SEV-3**; PBS weekly job failure → SEV-3.
- **DR-045** (DR §8.2) **Monthly restore drill** — first Saturday of each month, 2-hour window: restore latest backup to sandbox; validation queries; **RLS smoke test from `packages/db/test/restore-verify.test.ts`**; checksum comparisons; results documented in `docs/dr-drills/<date>.md` using the prescribed template (timings, verification checkboxes, findings, actions); sandbox destroyed with destruction certificate.
- **DR-046** (DR §8.3) **Quarterly failover drill** — pre-announced 2-hour customer maintenance window (brief read-only mode expected): promote db-02, cut over, verify 30 min, cut back; document each step's timing vs RTO target; update DR doc with learnings.
- **DR-047** (DR §8.4) **Annual full DR exercise** — simulate regional disaster; restore from off-site to alternate hardware; full stack up; regression suite; compute realistic actual RTO; adjust if targets missed.
- **DR-048** (DR §8.5) 2026 drill calendar: monthly restore (first Sat each month); failover drills 2026-03-15 / 06-15 / 09-15 / 12-15; annual full DR exercise **2026-08-01** (see Discrepancy D-1 — Ops Runbook says March).

### 1.9 Regional Disaster & Catastrophic Scenarios

- **DR-055** (DR §10.1) Disaster categories: Local (VM/service), Cluster (Proxmox host), Site (all hosts at primary location, incl. hypervisor ransomware), Regional, Vendor.
- **DR-056** (DR §10.2) Site disaster phased plan: **Phase 1 Stabilize (0–2 h)** — SEV-1 declared, status page, **BCP activated (customers to paper)**, off-site backups verified, alternate infrastructure provisioned; **Phase 2 Restore (2–12 h)** — PG from off-site pgBackRest, MinIO from off-site replica, containers from GHCR, secrets via off-site age keys, dependency-order bring-up, smoke suite, DNS cutover (failover-critical records planned at **TTL 60 sec**); **Phase 3 Verify & Reopen (12–24 h)** — staged reopen, **Engrace verifies first**, then tenant groups; **Phase 4 Permanent (days–weeks)** — rebuild primary or promote alternate; re-establish full DR posture.
- **DR-057** (DR §10.4.1) Ransomware response invariants: **isolate affected hosts by disconnecting network — do NOT shut down** (forensic state preserved); engage cybersecurity counsel **within 1 hour**; **never pay ransom**; restore from known-clean backup point (**24–48 hour data loss expectation** depending on detection lag); preserve encrypted state for forensics; notify customers — **assume PHI exposure unless proven otherwise**; engage cyber-insurance carrier (Phase 2, see OPEN-012); notify FBI (IC3) and state attorneys general per state breach law.
- **DR-058** (DR §10.4.1) Architectural ransomware protections that modules must not undermine: backup encryption keys not on primary infrastructure; off-site backups not reachable from primary network; **MinIO Object Lock on claims/cost-reports — cannot be overwritten or deleted for 7 years**; RLS confines compromised tenant accounts; **audit log is INSERT-only via `wardenx_app` role — UPDATE/DELETE require `wardenx_admin` (separate credential)**; backup VM has its own credentials.
- **DR-059** (DR §10.4.2) Insider threat response: suspend account + invalidate sessions; forensic preservation of audit logs/session records/app logs; counsel engagement; scope documentation; HIPAA breach assessment; customer notification; control-failure review. Detection signals include **failed RLS denials** (attempted cross-tenant access) and abnormal export patterns.
- **DR-060** (DR §10.4.3) Anthropic extended outage = **SEV-2**. Procedure: activate **`external.anthropic_kill`** feature flag (disables all Anthropic-dependent features immediately); status banner "AI features temporarily unavailable. Clinical documentation and operations continue normally."; fallbacks: HearthNote Layer 2 → **Layer 1 only (deterministic narrative still works)**; DocLens → manual chart entry; AI copilot unavailable; predictions hidden in UI; outage duration tracked as Tier 4 actual-RTO. (See Discrepancy D-3 on Anthropic's tier in the Integration Catalog.)
- **DR-061** (DR §10.4.4) DoseSpot extended outage = **SEV-2**. Activate **`external.dosespot_kill`**; banner instructs paper prescription pads; controlled substances by phone to pharmacy per state DEA emergency rules; administered meds manually entered in eMAR; on restoration, **reconciliation workflow retroactively enters paper prescriptions**; immediate DoseSpot enterprise escalation.
- **DR-062** (DR §10.4.5) MAC portal outage (NGS J6) = **SEV-3**: generated 837I files **queue locally in Module 23**; billing-staff banner; auto-process on recovery; if outage **exceeds 7 days approaching NOE 5-day-window deadlines** for new admissions: escalate and document (NGS typically allows late filing in regional outages with documentation).
- **DR-063** (DR §10.4.6) Database corruption from application bug = **SEV-1**: identify introducing commit and **roll back application immediately**; scope corruption; decide forward-fix (small, isolated scope: corrective SQL + audit log of corrections) vs PITR (large/unclear scope: restore to pre-corruption point, reconcile interim work with customers); post-incident regression test added.

### 1.10 Business Continuity for Clinical Operations (Clinical Downtime)

- **DR-064** (DR §11.1) Degraded-mode behavior (status banner indicates mode). **Available:** patient lookup, encounter draft (**saved locally on mobile, syncs on recovery**), care plan view, eMAR view, medication list, document download. **Limited:** real-time alerts may be delayed; AI features (HearthNote enhancement, DocLens) may be disabled. **Unavailable:** new DoseSpot prescriptions (if DoseSpot affected); new claim submission. Clinical care continues normally; system catches up on restoration. Modules with clinical surfaces must conform to these availability classes.
- **DR-065** (DR §11.2) Full-outage paper fallback: WardenX provides each agency a **"BCP packet"** of pre-printed forms — paper encounter visit note (captures identical vitals to electronic form), paper MAR, order form, **patient information card (printed weekly for active census; more often for higher-acuity)**, emergency contact sheet, paper symptom assessment forms (**PPS/FAST/PAINAD/ESAS**). Patient care must continue.
- **DR-066** (DR §11.3) Re-entry workflow: **Module 8 Encounters provides "BCP re-entry mode"** that (a) marks the encounter `bcp_paper_origin = true`; (b) captures the original paper signature date and time; (c) the audit log shows the record originated on paper during an outage; (d) **late entry does NOT change the visit date** (back-dated to actual visit date).
- **DR-067** (DR §11.3) Re-entry must be **completed within 72 hours of system restoration**; the Field Operations role has a **"BCP Re-Entry Worklist"** view tracking outstanding paper records.
- **DR-068** (DR §11.4) Outage communications: care-team coordination falls back to phone/in-person/agency channels; agency's own emergency contact tree applies; agency designates one staff member to monitor the status page and relay.
- **DR-069** (DR §11.5) **CHC/GIP invariant:** Continuous Home Care and General Inpatient patients require continuous nursing presence regardless of EHR state; on-duty RN documents on paper continuously; **CHC/GIP eligibility and billing are NOT affected by EHR outage** — paper records satisfy the documentation requirement.
- **DR-070** (DR §11.6) Safety-critical data must remain available no matter what is down: patient name/address/location/primary diagnosis, active medication list, active orders, allergies, code status & advance directive summary, emergency contacts, designated DPOA agent — **all printed weekly via the "Weekly BCP Print" BullMQ job** for every active patient, delivered via email and held in printed patient binders. **In a total outage, the printed binder is the source of truth.** Any module owning one of these data classes must feed this job.

## 2. OPS RUNBOOK REQUIREMENTS (OPS-###)

### 2.1 Scope, Governance, On-Call

- **OPS-001** (OPS header) Scope dividing line: catastrophic scenarios (SEV-1 per DR §2.3) belong to DR_BCP; SEV-2/3/4 and planned work belong to the runbook. Module specs must route their failure modes accordingly.
- **OPS-002** (OPS §1.1) Procedures must be executable by **any qualified engineer**, not only the author (deliberate SPOF hedge). Conventions: commands are production unless marked DEV; times are operator-local unless explicitly UTC (§1.3).
- **OPS-003** (OPS §2.1) On-call model mirrors DR-007: Engrace-only = 24/7 single primary, no secondary (acknowledged SPOF); 2–3 tenants = primary 8 AM–8 PM + contracted relief 8 PM–8 AM; 4+ tenants = formal rotation, 2-engineer minimum.
- **OPS-004** (OPS §2.2, §14.1) On-call kit: working laptop with VPN **reachable within 5 minutes**; phone on-person with PagerDuty ringer override; internet with mobile-hotspot fallback; shift-start checklist (PD schedule confirm, bastion access tested, status-page admin tested, handoff acknowledged); shift-end handoff log (open incidents, watch items, customer commitments, vendor escalations).

### 2.3 Deployment Operations

- **OPS-011** (OPS §4.1) Pre-deploy checklist for **every** deploy: code merged to `main` (no branch deploys); all CI checks green (**lint, typecheck, unit, integration, RLS, e2e, SBOM**); container image **signed with cosign**; SBOM attached; migration files reviewed — **forward-only? two-phase pattern if breaking?**; new feature flags have defaults set and are documented in `docs/feature-flags/`; customer communication drafted for customer-facing changes; maintenance window if DB schema or breaking API change; **rollback plan documented in the PR — every PR has a "How to roll this back" line**; on-call briefed.
- **OPS-012** (OPS §4.2) Standard deploy is **blue-green**: verify CI artifact + cosign signature; pull to all app VMs; start green alongside blue; wait for green `/ready`; load-balancer ramp **5% (hold 60 s) → 25% → 50% → 100% at 90 s intervals (~5 minutes total)**, watching for error spikes; blue cut to 0% but **held running 1 hour as the rollback window** before teardown.
- **OPS-013** (OPS §4.2–4.3) Deploy watch: error rate, backend p95, new Sentry issues, customer-impacting alerts. Post-deploy within 15 min: `/health` and `/ready` 200 on all instances; **`/version` returns expected SHA**; sample API endpoint succeeds; no new Sentry issues above pre-deploy baseline; **latency p95 within ±20% of pre-deploy**; DB connections normal (no leak); BullMQ workers connected. Within 1 hour: no customer degradation reports; synthetics passing; expected audit-event volume; tenant smoke (Engrace login + encounter draft).
- **OPS-014** (OPS §4.4) Immediate-rollback triggers: **error rate >5% sustained >5 min**; **p99 latency >2× baseline sustained >5 min**; new SEV-1/SEV-2 traced to deploy; critical customer-discovered regression. **Rollback time target: <5 minutes from decision to fully rolled back.** Rollback incident report mandatory (what broke, how detected, why CI missed it).
- **OPS-015** (OPS §4.5) Hot-fix (SEV-1/2 bugs): branch from current production SHA; minimum change; **write/update a test that catches the bug**; CI; fast-track review by another engineer or **explicitly documented self-review** in the PR (bug, catching test, why correct, what could go wrong) with mandatory production hotfix log entry; deploy with **shortened ramp 5% → 50% → 100% over 3 minutes**; documented in the incident retrospective.
- **OPS-016** (OPS §4.6) Database migrations follow the **two-phase (expand/contract) pattern for breaking changes** (Architecture §25.12): **Phase N additive only** (adds columns/tables; **no DROP, no RENAME** of existing columns) with a **pre-migration incremental pgBackRest backup tagged `pre-migration-<sha>`**; Drizzle migrations apply via init container before backend rollout; new backend dual-writes old+new; **dual-write runs ≥1 week** to surface issues; backfill script if needed; **Phase N+1** switches reads to the new column/table (old still written); **Phase N+2 cleanup** removes the old column/table **only after confirming no code reads it** (grep + canary monitoring).
- **OPS-017** (OPS §4.7) Deploy cadence targets: routine backend deploys 1–3/week (daily acceptable); frontend anytime; **schema migrations at most weekly**, coordinated with backend deploys; **infrastructure changes only in maintenance windows**; security patches **within 72 hours of CVE publish for critical, 7 days for high, 30 days for medium**.

### 2.4 Database Operations

- **OPS-018** (OPS §5.1.1) Autovacuum on with workers 4, naptime 30s, vacuum threshold 50 / scale 0.1, analyze threshold 50 / scale 0.05; hot tables tightened: `audit_event`, `encounter`, `message` at scale_factor **0.05**; `outbox_event` at **0.02**.
- **OPS-019** (OPS §5.1.2–5.1.3) Weekly Sunday-window `VACUUM (ANALYZE)` on `outbox_event`, `session`, `idempotency_key`; monthly reindex of bloated indexes **>100 MB**, always **REINDEX … CONCURRENTLY**.
- **OPS-020** (OPS §5.2.1) Partitioned-table inventory is normative; modules owning these tables must conform: `audit_event` RANGE created_at, monthly, retention per Architecture §24 class; `claim` LIST provider_entity_id → RANGE created_at, quarterly, **7 years** (with `claim_line` co-partitioned); `encounter` LIST organization_id → RANGE created_at, quarterly, **per state retention** (`encounter_note` co-partitioned); `hope_assessment` annually, **6 years**; `message` monthly, **6 years**; `extraction_job` monthly, **30 days**; `feature_flag_evaluation` monthly, per flag kind.
- **OPS-021** (OPS §5.2.2) A **weekly BullMQ job `partition.rotate`** verifies next-3-months partitions exist for all partitioned tables and creates missing ones. New partitioned tables must be added to this job.
- **OPS-022** (OPS §5.2.3) Partition retention semantics: `operational` class — detach **13 months after creation**, export to Parquet, store in MinIO `platform/cold-storage/<table>/<year>/<month>.parquet`, drop the partition, **audit the destruction** (`audit.partition_archived`), restorable on demand. `legal_hold` class — **never detach; retention jobs skip**.
- **OPS-023** (OPS §5.3.1) PgBouncer is the connection layer: **transaction pooling mode**; `max_client_conn 200`; `default_pool_size 50`; `reserve_pool_size 5` / timeout 3; `server_idle_timeout 600`; `server_lifetime 3600`; auth scram-sha-256. Modules must be compatible with transaction pooling (no session-state reliance across transactions).
- **OPS-024** (OPS §5.3.2–5.3.3) Pool exhaustion indicator: `cl_waiting > 0` sustained → increase pool or find leak. Leak signature: `idle in transaction` older than 5 minutes; emergency `pg_terminate_backend`; long-term fix is the code path holding the transaction.
- **OPS-025** (OPS §5.4, §13.1.1) Slow-query workflow via `pg_stat_statements` (>1 min cumulative exec time threshold) and `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)`; query timeout limits prevent runaway queries.
- **OPS-026** (OPS §5.6) DDL rules: **CONCURRENTLY for index creation/drop on populated tables**; long-running ALTER TABLE only in maintenance windows with monitoring; **schema changes through migrations only — never ad-hoc on production**; two-phase pattern for breaking changes.

### 2.7 Secret & Credential Management

### 3.3 Cross-Cutting Patterns (apply to every integration unless its §6 section overrides)

- **INT-008** (INT §3.1) **Adapter package model:** each integration is wrapped in a `packages/<vendor>-client/` workspace exposing a typed interface (TypeBox), never the vendor SDK surface. The adapter owns auth/token lifecycle, request/response typing, retry/backoff, circuit breaker, rate-limit pacing, error normalization to `DegradedError`, and per-call telemetry. Vendor swap = new adapter behind the same interface. **Anti-pattern (hard fail): any module calling `fetch()` directly against a vendor URL.**
- **INT-009** (INT §3.2) **Circuit breaker on every external call** with configured timeout, retry count, backoff; states closed/open/half-open. On failure the adapter raises a typed **`DegradedError` carrying `{ integration, operation, retryable, customerMessage, correlationId }`**; raw fetch rejections never propagate to clients. Open-breaker events emit a System alert (Module 36 "System → integration failure") to the tenant admin and WardenX ops.
- **INT-010** (INT §3.3) **Retry policy:** transient failures (network, 429, 5xx, timeout) → exponential backoff with jitter, **capped retries (default 3)**; per-integration overrides only via the integration's §6 section.
- **INT-011** (INT §3.3) **Idempotency:** non-idempotent writes (claim submission, NewRx, Stripe charge) are protected by an idempotency key so a retry never double-acts. Native vendor idempotency keys used where supported (Stripe); otherwise the adapter **persists an attempt record keyed `(operation, businessKey)` before the call** and reconciles on retry.
- **INT-012** (INT §3.3) **Outbox pattern** (Architecture §25.13) for durable cross-perimeter dispatch: intent committed to the outbox **in the same DB transaction** as the business state change; a BullMQ worker drains the outbox and performs the external call with at-least-once delivery + idempotency. **Mandatory for: claim submission, iQIES submission, message-notification fan-out.**
- **INT-013** (INT §3.4) **Credential rules:** no credential in source or plain env files in production; secrets read from the secrets manager at boot and on rotation signal. Platform secrets never exposed to tenants. Per-tenant secrets keyed by `organization_id` and unreadable in another tenant's context (DoseSpot specifically: `packages/dosespot/credentials` per Architecture §8(4)). Per-ProviderEntity secrets (MAC submitter IDs, Medicaid trading-partner IDs, iQIES/MCReF credentials) scoped to the CCN-bearing ProviderEntity. **OAuth token isolation:** Xero/Gusto refresh tokens stored per-tenant; refresh failures must not cascade across tenants. Rotation cadence owned by Security Hardening; procedures by Ops Runbook §8.
- **INT-014** (INT §3.5, §5, §11) **BAA gate:** any PHI-bearing third-party integration requires an executed BAA before production PHI flows — **"Status must read EXECUTED" is a hard gate, not a tracking note**. CMS systems (MAC, HETS, MCReF, iQIES) use trading-partner agreements/enrollment, not BAAs. WardenX-operated local AI involves no third party — no BAA. The "no PHI by rule" channels (Twilio, Postmark, Web Push) still carry BAAs as defense-in-depth (except Web Push, whose browser push services are not BAA counterparties — which is exactly why no PHI may ever be in a push payload).
- **INT-015** (INT §3.6) **Sandbox/production separation:** every integration MUST have a non-production path used by CI and tenant-onboarding rehearsal; sandbox credentials are secrets and never co-mingled with production credentials; **environment is selected by config, never by a code branch**; **no production PHI in sandbox** — synthetic patients only; CI uses MSW mocks (web) and recorded fixtures (adapter integration tests), plus vendor sandboxes for contract tests.
- **INT-016** (INT §3.7, §7) **Webhook security baseline** for every inbound callback: signature verification with the vendor's scheme (reject unsigned/invalid); replay protection via timestamp tolerance + persisted event-ID dedup; source allowlisting where the vendor publishes stable egress ranges; **idempotent handlers (same event twice → one effect)**; webhook receipt audited; PHI-bearing webhook bodies (DoseSpot) processed in-perimeter and **never logged in the clear** (Pino redaction).
- **INT-017** (INT §3.8) **Observability per integration:** OpenTelemetry spans tagged `integration`, `operation`, `tenant_id`, `correlation_id`; Grafana per-integration dashboards (success rate, p50/p95/p99 latency, breaker state, 429 rate, retry rate, outbox queue depth); per-integration synthetic probe (Uptime Kuma + go-live health checks) verifying reachability and auth validity; cost-bearing integrations (Anthropic tokens, Twilio messages) feed Module 35 usage metering and the AI cost watch.
- **INT-018** (INT §3.9) **PHI minimization at the boundary:** PHI leaves the perimeter only to PHI-cleared, BAA-covered destinations and only minimum-necessary; **redaction-before-send for Anthropic** (AI Governance owns the redaction policy; this catalog owns the wire contract); **no-PHI-in-payload rule for Twilio/Postmark/Web Push enforced in the notification builder** — outbound notifications carry "You have a new message" + authenticated deep link, never clinical content.

## 6. MECHANICAL CHECKLIST FOR MODULE AUDITORS

Run every check against each module spec. Cite the criteria ID with each finding.

1. **Tier declaration.** Does the module declare its DR service tier(s), and do they match the named-service tier assignments in DR-010 and the integration tiers in INT-005? Any RTO/RPO number stated by the module must equal or be looser than the internal target for its tier — never tighter promises to customers than DR-013 SLAs.
2. **Downtime behavior defined.** Does the module define degraded-mode and full-outage behavior consistent with DR-064 (available / limited / unavailable classes)? Clinical-surface modules must state what remains readable and what queues.
3. **Paper fallback / BCP hooks.** If the module owns clinical documentation or any of the seven safety-critical data classes (DR-070), does it feed the Weekly BCP Print job and the BCP packet (DR-065)? Does Module 8 (or any module accepting late entries) implement BCP re-entry semantics: `bcp_paper_origin = true`, paper signature date/time capture, paper-origin audit, visit date unchanged, 72-hour re-entry window, re-entry worklist (DR-066, DR-067)?
4. **Offline/field behavior.** Mobile/field features must save drafts locally and sync on recovery (DR-064); CHC/GIP billing/eligibility must not depend on EHR availability (DR-069).
5. **Statelessness.** Does the module keep backend instances stateless (no instance-local durable state), preserving DR-036's zero-data-loss instance failover? Does it treat Valkey as ephemeral and PostgreSQL as the sole system of record (DR-021, DR-030)?
6. **Job idempotency.** Every BullMQ consumer the module defines must be idempotent (DR-022, INT-012). Does the spec state idempotency keys/strategy per job?
7. **Retry/DLQ semantics.** Do the module's jobs declare retry policy (default cap 3, exponential backoff with jitter per INT-010 unless an integration §6 override applies), failed-job/DLQ handling, and the document-before-clearing rule (OPS-035)? Queue-depth expectations consistent with the >1000-wait investigation threshold (OPS-034)?
8. **Outbox usage.** Cross-perimeter dispatch with durability requirements (claims, iQIES, notification fan-out, and anything analogous) must use the outbox pattern committed in the same transaction as business state (INT-012). Does the module instead call external services inline from request handlers? Flag it.
9. **Integration inventory conformance.** Does the module claim any external system not present in INT-005 (e.g., PECOS, direct Surescripts, EVV aggregators, direct pharmacy/HIE links — INT-007)? Any new integration must show the five-step addition of INT-002, not a bare claim.
10. **Protocol/direction/auth match.** For each claimed integration, do protocol, direction, auth method, and credential scope match INT-005 and the §6 contract (INT-019…INT-043)? Examples of hard fails: CMS webhook receivers (INT-044 — CMS acks are pull/file-based); a WardenX-held Surescripts credential (INT-020); platform-scoped DoseSpot credentials (must be per-tenant, INT-019); tenant-visible platform secrets (INT-013).
11. **Module-assignment match.** Is the module claiming an integration assigned to a different owning module in INT-005 without going through the catalog? (Consumption via the owning module's adapter interface is fine; direct ownership is not.)
12. **Adapter discipline.** All external calls through `packages/<vendor>-client` typed adapters; no direct `fetch()` to vendor URLs (INT-008). Circuit breaker + `DegradedError` with the five-field shape; raw rejections never reach clients (INT-009).
13. **Webhook security.** Any inbound callback the module handles must declare signature verification, timestamp+event-ID replay protection, idempotent handling, audit of receipt, and no clear-text logging of PHI bodies (INT-016); the event source must exist in the webhook catalog (INT-044).
14. **Idempotency keys for non-idempotent writes.** Claim submission → claim control number; NewRx → (prescriber, patient, rx_business_key); Stripe → native keys on all creates; notifications → `notification_delivery_id`; HL7 → message control ID / filler order ID; HOPE → HopeSubmission record; telehealth rooms → telehealth_visit_id; bulk FHIR → job ID (INT-011, INT-019…INT-043 per integration).
15. **Sandbox separation.** Does the module define a non-production path (vendor sandbox/fixtures/MSW), config-selected (never code-branch), with no production PHI in sandbox and no credential co-mingling (INT-015)?
16. **BAA gating.** PHI-bearing integration features must be gated on EXECUTED BAA status (INT-014); cloud AI specifically on the triple gate opt-in ∧ BAA+ZDR ∧ routing-allows-cloud, with per-tenant flags defaulting to false (INT-034). Modules shipping before their ADR/BAA gate closes (OPEN-003…OPEN-007) are non-conformant.
17. **No PHI in notification payloads.** Anything the module sends through Twilio/Postmark/Web Push must be deep-link-only, enforced in the notification builder (INT-018, INT-030…INT-033). AI calls must apply redaction-before-send (INT-034).
18. **AI degradation.** AI-consuming features must degrade through the fallback chain to a deterministic path and must never block patient care on AI availability (INT-034, INT-035, DR-060); respect `local_only` tenant semantics (INT-035).
19. **Kill switches.** External-dependency features must honor the kill-switch flags (`external.anthropic_kill`, `external.dosespot_kill`, and analogous per-integration switches) and define banner/fallback behavior consistent with DR-060/DR-061 and the INT-045 matrix.
20. **Migration approach.** Schema changes must be migrations-only (never ad-hoc), forward-only, and use the two-phase expand/contract pattern for anything breaking: additive first (no DROP/RENAME), pre-migration tagged backup, ≥1 week dual-write, read switch, cleanup only after verified no-reads (OPS-016, OPS-026). Index DDL on populated tables must be CONCURRENTLY (OPS-019, OPS-026). Schema migrations at most weekly, in maintenance windows when breaking (OPS-017, OPS-005).
21. **Partitioning & retention.** If the module owns a table in the OPS-020 inventory, do partition key/cadence/retention match exactly? New high-volume tables: are they added to `partition.rotate` (OPS-021)? Do retention/deletion jobs skip `legal_hold` and audit destruction (OPS-022, OPS-057)?
22. **Connection-pool compatibility.** No reliance on session state across transactions (PgBouncer transaction pooling, OPS-023); no long-held idle-in-transaction patterns (OPS-024); DB access via the prescribed roles only (OPS-070 — e.g., audit-log writes via `wardenx_app` INSERT-only, DR-058).
23. **Storage conformance.** Module object storage must use the per-tenant bucket layout and lifecycle/lock rules (DR-019, OPS-027, OPS-028, OPS-031); compliance artifacts (claims, cost reports) must land in Object-Locked buckets and must not be designed as mutable; presigned-URL sharing must be short-lived, prefix-scoped, consent-gated, view-audited (INT-043).
24. **Deploy compatibility.** Services must expose `/health`, `/ready`, and `/version`; support blue-green coexistence (two versions live during ramp) and the 1-hour blue retention window; new feature flags documented with defaults (OPS-011…OPS-013). Rollback plan line present in PRs (OPS-011).
25. **Alerting conformance.** Module-defined alerts must use the YAML schema with severity, threshold, duration, and an actionable runbook link, with expected false-positive rate <5% (OPS-043); severities must map to the DR-008 definitions; dashboards PHI-free (OPS-045).
26. **Audit events.** Does the module emit the audit events the criteria depend on: `ai.capability_invoked` with token counts (OPS-049), PHI export events (OPS-060), `security.rls_denied`, legal-hold and destruction events (OPS-022, OPS-057), break-glass if applicable (OPS-042)? Audit log integrity preserved across the module's DR behavior (DR-071).
27. **Secrets.** Every credential the module introduces must appear in the secret inventory with a rotation cadence (OPS-036), at the credential scope assigned by INT-005/INT-013; no secrets in source or plain env (INT-013); rotation procedure compatible with OPS-037 patterns (dual-valid or new-then-revoke).
28. **Capacity & cost.** Cost-bearing usage (AI tokens, SMS) must feed Module 35 metering and the cost-watch attributions (INT-017, OPS-048, OPS-049); module growth assumptions consistent with capacity thresholds (OPS-030, OPS-050).
29. **Tenant lifecycle.** Module behavior under tenant suspension (read-only compliance access, mutation block except admin export), closure (export + retention countdown), DSAR/portability (FHIR R4 bundle), and legal hold must match OPS-053…OPS-057.
30. **Severity & escalation mapping.** Module failure modes must map to the SEV definitions and named outage severities (DoseSpot SEV-2, MAC SEV-3, Anthropic SEV-2 per DR-060…DR-062); Tier-1/2 paths must not block documentation or medication administration on any external dependency (INT-045).
31. **Recovery participation.** If the module appears in the post-recovery checklist (Module 23 claim validation, encounter save, DoseSpot health, notification test paths — DR-043) or onboarding go-live gates (INT-047), its spec must support those checks (testable health/smoke endpoints, synthetic-safe operations).
32. **OPEN-item hygiene.** Where the module touches OPEN-001…OPEN-021, verify it (a) does not flag the deferral as resolved, (b) does not foreclose the pending decision, and (c) gates shipping on the gate actually closing. Do not record the deferral itself as a module defect.
33. **Discrepancy handling.** If a module conflicts with one source but matches the other on items D-1…D-10, route to the audit lead; do not unilaterally fail the module.

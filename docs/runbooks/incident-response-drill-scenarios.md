# Incident-response drill: scenarios and injects (CW-IR-01)

Three tabletop scenarios for the drill kit in [`incident-response-drill.md`](./incident-response-drill.md). The facilitator reads the
**card** column aloud, verbatim, and answers questions only from it. **Participants must not read this file before the drill.**
Every tenant, patient, operator and address below is invented; addresses are RFC 5737 documentation ranges and mail domains are
`example.org`. The cards describe hypothetical events; they are not claims about how the product behaves. "Expected actions" cite
the document a competent responder should reach for; "Probes" are the facilitator's follow-up questions.

Document short names: **DR** = [`CHARTWARDEN_DR_BCP_v0.1.md`](../architecture/CHARTWARDEN_DR_BCP_v0.1.md), **OPS** =
[`CHARTWARDEN_OPS_RUNBOOK_v0.1.md`](../architecture/CHARTWARDEN_OPS_RUNBOOK_v0.1.md).

Common to all three: the incident channel and the timeline document open at the first inject (OPS section 14.2); severity is
named with the DR section 2.3 table; roles are assigned out loud (DR section 2.1); regulator, customer and vendor contact is
**drafted, never sent**.

## Scenario 1: suspected PHI exposure (a document shows another patient's information)

**Learning objectives.** Recognise an exposure as SEV-1; contain without destroying evidence; scope it from the audit trail; run the
breach assessment as a business associate; notify the right covered entity through the right contact. **Roles:** IC, Technical
Lead, Communications Lead, Compliance Officer, counsel (played), customer liaison (played, Synthetic Hospice A and B).
**Runs about 90 minutes.**

| # | Offset | Card (read aloud) | Expected actions (document) | Probes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | T0 | "It is Tuesday, 16:40 local. The compliance officer of Synthetic Hospice A, a covered entity and your tenant, emails the support address: a family member of Patient P-A1 opened the visit summary the agency released to them today, and it describes a different patient, with another name and diagnosis. The agency has taken the document out of the family's view. There is no other detail." | Fix **discovery time** as T0 (or earlier, if the group says monitoring should have caught it) and write it down (DR section 9.3). Declare **SEV-1**: "PHI exposed in error" is a DR section 2.3 example. Name IC; open channel and timeline (OPS section 14.2). Do not reply to the tenant with any detail yet. | "Who may declare SEV-1 and who must be told in the first 15 minutes?" "What is the first thing you write down, and where?" |
| 1.1 | T+10 | "The Technical Lead looks at the document. The record names P-A1; the rendered content names P-A2. It was generated at 15:58. A release was deployed at 15:52. Two more documents generated between 15:55 and 16:20 look wrong in the same way. Nothing else is known." | A deploy is the prime suspect, which is an OPS section 4.4 rollback trigger ("new SEV-1 or SEV-2 incident traced to deploy"). The **real** rollback is `deploy/production/README.md`, "Rolling back" (OPS section 4.4, today's note), not the blue-green `lb-cli` commands. **Evidence first**: name what to keep before the rollback recreates containers (the three documents, the render job logs, the release's image digests). Name the containment lever and its approver (DR section 2.4). Place a legal hold on the three documents so retention jobs cannot touch them (OPS section 11.6). | "What stops *further* wrong documents in the next five minutes, before a rollback finishes?" (If no switch is named, record it.) "A rollback is forward-only on the schema: what if this release carried a contract migration?" (`deploy.sh` holds at exit 75 only for migrations the *deployed* image still has pending, so a manual redeploy of the previous image onto the advanced schema holds nothing; the answer is to read the migration ledger and the `-- compat:` headers first. `deploy/production/README.md`, "Rollback and migrations") |
| 1.2 | T+30 | "You query the audit trail. Document 1 was released to the family's portal account for P-A1 and viewed twice, once from 203.0.113.17. Document 2 went to a clinician inside the tenant and was viewed once. Document 3 was generated and never opened. All three patients belong to Synthetic Hospice A. No other tenant has documents in that window." | Scope from the audit routes: per-patient access history `GET /api/v1/audit/patients/{patientId}/access` and the event search `GET /api/v1/audit/events` (filters `eventTypeContains`, `from`, `to`); both are **org-scoped**, so a cross-tenant check is one query per tenant (a cross-tenant read leaves an `audit.elevated_access` meta-event). Results are handled by **ID only** in channels and drafts. Reliability of "who viewed" is stated with its limit (DR section 4.8.7, step 0, the document-access-log attribution caveat). | "How do you prove no *other* tenant was affected?" "Which screen shows you who viewed document 1, and how sure are you?" |
| 1.3 | T+45 | Counsel (played): "Is this a breach, whose is it, and when did the clock start? The executed BAA with Synthetic Hospice A requires notice within `<N>` days." *(The group reads the real figure from the executed BAA; this repo does not hold it.)* | ChartWarden is the **business associate**; the covered entity owns notice to patients, HHS and media unless the BAA delegates it (DR section 9.3). An impermissible disclosure is presumed a breach unless the four-factor assessment shows a low probability of compromise; none of the three exceptions fits an outside family member who opened and may have saved the document. Compliance Officer and counsel engaged within 60 minutes of T0 (OPS section 14.4). Notice to the covered entity is **drafted** (what happened, dates, who, what was exposed, what is being done) within the BAA deadline and never later than 60 days from discovery. | "What is the earliest date the clock could be said to have started?" "Who approves sending, per DR section 2.4?" "What does the notice *not* contain?" |
| 1.4 | T+60 | The customer liaison for Synthetic Hospice B phones: "We were on the same release. Are we affected?" | The caller is **not** the tenant's recorded breach-notification contact: confirm identity and answer through the contact recorded at provisioning (OPS section 18.1). Draft a status entry from the DR section 9.2.1 and 9.2.2 templates: **no tenant name, no patient detail, no PHI**; next update inside 30 minutes (SEV-1 cadence, DR section 2.3). | "Do you name Synthetic Hospice A anywhere public?" "What do you tell B in the next ten minutes that is true?" |
| 1.5 | T+80 | "The rollback finished and the render worker is verified. Synthetic Hospice B had no documents in the window. Synthetic Hospice A's compliance officer asks what happens next." | Stand-down criteria stated and met (OPS section 4.3 verification, audit volume back to normal). Evidence retained 6 years (DR section 9.3). **Post-incident review within 7 days** (DR section 9.2.4 template). Customer-facing resolution message from DR section 9.2.3. | "Who decides the incident is over?" "What goes in the post-incident review that is not in the notice?" |

**Traps.** Treating this as a tenant mistake before checking the deploy; rolling back before preserving evidence; posting the
tenant's name on the public status page; telling a caller who is not the recorded contact; starting the 60-day clock at the end
of the investigation instead of at discovery.

## Scenario 2: leaked backup key (the restore-secrets folder is exposed)

**Learning objectives.** Reason about what a leaked key opens (key plus ciphertext plus access); sequence a rotation so no backup
is orphaned; recognise the keys that cannot be rotated; decide the breach question when exposure cannot be ruled out.
**Roles:** IC, Technical Lead, Compliance Officer, counsel (played). **Runs about 120 minutes.** Reference: DR section 4.8.5 and the
"Leaked-key response" below it; OPS sections 8.1, 8.2.4, 8.3.1 and 8.3.3.

| # | Offset | Card (read aloud) | Expected actions (document) | Probes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0 | T0 | "It is Friday, 09:15 local. The operator who ran last week's off-site restore drill tells you the `restore-secrets/` folder they used (the escrowed key files `restore.sh` needs, DR section 4.8.6, kept together with the application keys a restore also needs) was synced to a personal cloud-storage account and shared by link for three days, to send a file to a contractor. The operator has already switched sharing off." | **Do not** copy, paste or "check" the key values anywhere, including the incident channel. Declare an incident; severity is **argued and recorded**: SEV-1 is justified because a decrypt capability over every tenant's history may be exposed (DR section 2.3, "PHI exposure"), and it can be lowered when the facts say so. Preserve the share link's access history from the personal account **before** it is cleaned up. | "What is in that folder? Who knows, without opening it?" "Does the operator being the same person as your IC change anything?" |
| 2.1 | T+15 | "The folder holds: the READ-ONLY off-site restore key's secret file, the off-site pgBackRest passphrase, the SSE-C key, `MESSAGING_ENCRYPTION_KEY` and `M34_TENANT_KEK`. It does **not** hold the WAL or backup S3 keys, or the local repository passphrase. The personal account's share log shows two views of the link from addresses 198.51.100.8 and 198.51.100.23; the operator recognises neither." | List what each secret opens, using DR section 4.8.5: the restore key reads the off-site bucket (it cannot write or delete, and the COMPLIANCE lock means nobody can delete locked versions); the passphrase opens the off-site pgBackRest repository (the whole database); the SSE-C key opens the object mirror (every document); `M34_TENANT_KEK` unwraps every tenant's DEK in any backup; `MESSAGING_ENCRYPTION_KEY` opens legacy-envelope message bodies. Conclusion the group must reach in words: **access to the bucket plus these files decrypts the full history**, and two unrecognised views mean exposure cannot be ruled out. | "Which single secret in this folder is the most dangerous, and why?" "Which of these does the plan say cannot be rotated?" |
| 2.2 | T+30 | "Your Technical Lead proposes to rotate everything now. The IC asks for the order." | Sequence per DR section 4.8.5, "Leaked-key response": (1) revoke the restore key at the provider and issue a replacement for responders (stops further reads); rotating the WAL and backup keys as a precaution is **staged** (new key in the container secret files first, then revoke the old), because revoking the WAL key makes `archive_command` fail and `pg_wal` grow; (2) update the **escrow** with the new material **before** anything is retired; (3) new off-site passphrase means a **new stanza**, and the old passphrase and stanza are **kept** until their backups expire; (4) a new SSE-C key covers new copies only; (5) rotate `M34_TENANT_KEK` per OPS section 8.3.3 (active key, retired list, re-wrap, prune last). | "What breaks if you drop the old passphrase today?" "Where is the stanza name set, and is it the same place for the database and the backup job?" (DR section 4.8.5: it is not; the compose file names it literally in `archive_command`. A finding if the group does not know.) |
| 2.3 | T+60 | "The Technical Lead reads the bucket's live configuration: Object Lock in COMPLIANCE mode for at least 100 days, a lifecycle that expires noncurrent versions and delete markers at 120 days and nothing else, 13 weekly full backups, and two full sets in the local repository. The provider's access log only goes back 7 days. Nobody has a record of the restore key being used by anyone but the operator. Also: `MESSAGING_ENCRYPTION_KEY` has no rotation procedure." | State the **exposure window per stream** (DR section 4.8.5, step 5): the pgBackRest history (the database) stays readable to a holder of the leaked passphrase and bucket access for about 7 months (about 91 days of retention plus 120 as a noncurrent version); the **object mirror has no expiry**, so every document stays readable under the leaked SSE-C key until someone purges it, which only a manual bucket-owner action can do after the lock lapses and for which no procedure exists. Nobody can delete locked versions in the first 100 days or more, not even the provider's root account. Rotation protects only new data; access control on the bucket is the control that matters now. A re-copy of an old object under a new SSE-C key writes a *new version*; the old one stays. `MESSAGING_ENCRYPTION_KEY` compromise is a **re-encryption project, not a rotation** (OPS section 8.3.3): record it as a **blocking finding**, with an owner. | "Can you delete the old backups to end the exposure, and when could anyone?" "What do you tell the covered entities about message bodies?" |
| 2.4 | T+80 | Counsel (played): "Can you show a low probability that PHI was compromised?" | Compliance Officer runs the **four factors in writing**: nature and extent (full database and documents of every tenant), the unauthorized person (unknown, two unrecognised views), whether PHI was actually acquired or viewed (not determinable; the provider log is shorter than the exposure), and mitigation (keys and access rotated, but not retroactively). Encryption is a safe harbor **only while the key is uncompromised**; here it is not, for every backup those keys open. The recorded owner of the conclusion is named; if "low probability" cannot be shown, covered-entity notices are drafted per DR section 9.3. Counsel and (if proposed) a forensic firm go through IC (DR section 2.4). | "Who decides, and what do they sign?" "Does 'we cannot tell' count as low probability?" |
| 2.5 | T+100 | "Rotation is staged. The IC asks how you know the backups still work." | Prove a restore opens with the **new** escrowed keys before retiring anything: isolated `restore.sh` plus `bun run backup:verify-restore` (DR section 4.8.6), never onto the running stack, recorded under `audit/restore-drill/`. Confirm archiving and the backup job are healthy (`archive_command` not failing, `pg_wal` not growing, `backup.stale` after 45 minutes without a snapshot) now, at T+1 hour and at T+1 day. Update the secret inventory's last-rotated date (OPS section 8.1). Policy follow-up: why escrowed keys were on a workstation at all. | "What do you check at T+1 hour and T+1 day?" "What stops this folder being synced again?" |

**Traps.** Pasting a key into chat to "compare"; dropping the old passphrase; rotating before updating escrow; believing rotation
shortens the exposure; assuming the S3 keys are safe because they were not in the folder (the restore key was); reaching for a
rotation of `MESSAGING_ENCRYPTION_KEY` that does not exist.

## Scenario 3: compromised operator account

**Learning objectives.** Contain an account when the only person who can contain it is that account; assume the coordination
channel is observed; hunt for persistence; scope PHI access per tenant; handle secrets the operator could read.
**Roles:** IC, Technical Lead, Communications Lead, Compliance Officer, counsel (played), the platform operator "Operator-1"
(played; the facilitator holds their answers). **Runs about 120 minutes.** Reference: DR sections 10.4.2, 14A.3, 9.1 and the
operator-compromise notes there; OPS sections 8.1, 8.5 and 18.3.

| # | Offset | Card (read aloud) | Expected actions (document) | Probes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.0 | T0 | "It is Sunday, 03:20 local. An alert reaches the platform security owner: `platform.bypass_invoked` events against Synthetic Hospice A and Synthetic Hospice B between 03:12 and 03:31, from 192.0.2.44, attributed to Operator-1's account with a browser the account has never used. Operator-1, reached by phone at 03:35, says they were asleep and have not touched a laptop since 22:00." | Declare **SEV-1** ("PHI exposure", DR section 2.3); IC engaged inside 5 minutes. Treat the account as hostile **and the incident channel as possibly observed**: move coordination to a channel the account cannot reach (DR section 9.1, out-of-band note). | "Which channel do you use, and can Operator-1's account read it?" "Who is IC if Operator-1 is the IC?" |
| 3.1 | T+10 | "Operator-1 is the only person holding a platform role. The sealed break-glass envelope is in the fire safe at the owner's office, an hour away." | Options and their cost, chosen by the IC with the approver named (DR section 2.4): (a) deactivate platform-wide with `POST /api/v1/platform/users/{id}/deactivate` (needs a platform-role session with a fresh `user.deactivate_platform` passkey step-up; the code has no self-target guard, so the **real Operator-1 can do it to their own account from a trusted device**, which is the fastest option unless their passkey is also compromised; it reaps every session and membership in every tenant and is audited `m2.user.deactivated`); (b) if (a) is not trustworthy or reachable, remove the platform role with the ticketed, audited CLI (`bun run db:platform-role revoke`, OPS section 16.5, owner connection) or use the **break-glass database credential** (OPS section 8.5: open the envelope, log the time and reason, rotate after, review within 24 hours; no hand-written SQL on the auth tables); (c) rotate `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET` and deploy (every session of every tenant is invalidated; customer-visible; OPS section 8.2.1); (d) suspend a tenant (OPS section 11.2; heavy). Close any open support sessions the account holds (`POST /api/v1/platform/support-sessions/{id}/close`). | "Which of these is fastest, and which is least harmful to patient care at 03:40 on a Sunday?" "Can Operator-1 do it themselves from a trusted device, and what would make you refuse?" "What does the plan say you do if there is no second operator?" (DR section 2.1: a recorded SPOF; a finding if unresolved.) |
| 3.2 | T+30 | "The audit sweep by actor, per tenant: in Synthetic Hospice A the account read 112 patient charts and requested one export; in Synthetic Hospice B it read only tenant settings and the user list. A platform role was granted at 03:25 to a user nobody recognises." | **Evidence preserved** (DR section 10.4.2, steps 1 and 2): export the account's `session` rows (address, user agent, times) **before** deactivation, because it deletes them, then `GET /api/v1/audit/events` filtered by `actorUserId` and the window, **one tenant at a time**, plus the DR section 14A.3 queries for bypass events, support sessions and break-glass events. **Persistence hunt**: new users, roles and memberships (the unknown platform role is deactivated through the same route), consent grants and support sessions, API tokens, alert webhooks and CI or deploy secrets, SSH keys, SOPS access. | "What would you look for that is *not* in the audit trail?" "How do you know you found every persistence mechanism?" |
| 3.3 | T+60 | "Operator-1's workstation could decrypt the environment file. The forensic image of the laptop is being discussed." | Treat **every secret the account could read** as exposed (OPS section 8.1 inventory, the SOPS age key in OPS section 8.4) and rotate by priority per OPS section 8.2.4: anything that grants access to data or to other secrets first, vendor keys next, the **non-rotatable** keys (OPS section 8.3.3) recorded as a blocking finding with an owner. Engage a forensic firm only through the IC with counsel (DR section 2.4) and image the laptop before reimaging. | "Which secret do you rotate first, and what does it break?" "What can you not rotate?" |
| 3.4 | T+75 | Counsel (played): "Which tenants must be told, what, and by when?" | Per tenant, written: Synthetic Hospice A (112 charts viewed by an unauthorized person) is a presumptive breach, so a covered-entity notice is drafted (DR section 9.3); Synthetic Hospice B (no PHI) is **not** a breach but is still a security incident the BAA requires the business associate to report (45 CFR 164.314(a)(2)(i)(C); check the BAA's wording). Notices go to the breach-notification contact recorded at provisioning (OPS section 18.1). Law-enforcement and insurer contact is a counsel and IC decision (DR section 10.4.1 steps 8 to 10), drafted only. | "What does B's notice say, and does it name A?" "Who may speak to the press, and what do they say?" |
| 3.5 | T+100 | "Containment holds and no further activity is seen. Operator-1 needs access back by Monday." | Re-enrol the operator on a **new** device and passkey only after the workstation is rebuilt; a second platform operator is named as an action (DR section 2.1); stand-down criteria stated; **post-incident review within 7 days**; DR and OPS sections that were wrong are corrected in a follow-up PR. | "What proves this account is clean before you restore its access?" |

**Traps.** Deactivating the account before exporting its session rows (deactivation deletes them); discussing containment in the channel the account can read; waiting for the envelope while the actor keeps reading;
suspending tenants first and breaching continuity of care; rotating secrets before preserving evidence; telling Synthetic Hospice B
nothing because "no PHI".

## Runbook corrections made while preparing this kit

These were found by walking the scenarios through the documents and are fixed in the same change. They are **material**, not editorial: DR/BCP section 14.3 calls for a version bump and a sign-off, so a pending row sits in DR/BCP section 14.4 and the owner and counsel confirm before the version moves:

- **DR section 9.3** (counsel has not yet reviewed the rewrite) cited the Privacy Rule and used "more than 500" for HHS; the rule is the Breach Notification Rule (45 CFR
  164.400 to 164.414), HHS notice is for **500 or more**, and the business associate's own duty (to the covered entity,
  164.410) was blurred with the covered entity's. Added the discovery rule, the three exceptions, the encryption safe harbor
  and the BAA-deadline caveat.
- **DR section 2.4** lists "activate kill switch" without saying which; the AI governance kill switch is per AI capability.
  Added the containment levers that exist for everything else.
- **DR section 9.1**: added what to do when the coordination channel itself may be observed.
- **DR section 10.4.1**: a stray vendor name in the ransom policy line.
- **DR section 10.4.2**: "disable the account; invalidate sessions" now names the platform route, its second-operator
  prerequisite, the fallbacks, the persistence hunt and the secrets the account could read.
- **DR section 4.8.5**: added **Leaked-key response**, including the Object Lock exposure window and the single-host stanza
  literal.
- **OPS section 4.4**: the blue-green `lb-cli` rollback names hosts the single-host stack does not have; added today's
  rollback path.
- **OPS section 8.2.4**: the six-line emergency rotation had no ordering, no backup-key branch and no answer for non-rotatable
  keys. **OPS section 8.3.1**: a passphrase on the command line (pgBackRest refuses secure options there) and `verify` with a
  `--type` it does not take; both corrected (not run against a live pgBackRest from this kit: confirm at the first drill).

## Still open (the drill should confirm each, and record it as a finding if it is still true)

1. One person holds IC, Technical Lead, Communications Lead and Compliance Officer, and is the only platform operator (DR
   section 2.1). No second operator, stand-in or contracted relief exists on paper.
2. `docs/architecture/vendor-escalation.md` (DR section 9.4) is not created; the roster in section 3 of the kit is the interim.
3. `MESSAGING_ENCRYPTION_KEY`, `TWO_FACTOR_ENCRYPTION_KEY` and `M24_CREDENTIAL_ENCRYPTION_KEY` have no rotation path (OPS
   section 8.3.3); an emergency has no procedure beyond the breach assessment.
4. `deploy/production/compose.backup.yml` hardcodes the stanza in `archive_command`, so a pgBackRest passphrase rotation on the
   single-host stack is a compose change, not a variable change. A code or deploy change, not a document one.
5. No switch stops PHI-bearing document generation without a rollback, tenant suspension or the AI kill switch.
6. The status page and incident mailbox in DR section 9.1 carry the legacy `wardenx` names; confirm what is live.
7. DR/BCP is bannered **TARGET, not built**; its VM-fleet commands (`ssh chartwarden-app-01`, `lb-cli`) name hosts that do not
   exist. Responders must be told which sections are operable today.
8. A cross-tenant audit sweep by actor is one query per tenant; no platform-wide actor search is documented.
9. Deactivating a user deletes their session rows, so session evidence must be exported first; no step in the code does that.
10. None of the items above has an owner or a tracking issue yet; the owner assigns them (the record template requires both for a
    finding).

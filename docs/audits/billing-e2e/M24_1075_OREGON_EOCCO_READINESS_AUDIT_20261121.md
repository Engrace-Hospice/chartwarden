## A. Executive summary

ChartWarden has a meaningful internal Medicaid claim-preparation foundation: provider-entity-scoped claim assembly, M5 coverage lookup, M6 hospice LOC periods, rate schedule lookup, claim state/validation records, credential configuration, correction/void modeling, and a fail-closed submit gate. It is **not close to a proven ordinary EOCCO hospice billing transaction**: no current source establishes the tenant’s EOCCO contract, the member’s service-specific payer responsibility, the provider’s EOCCO/837I/835 enrollments, or an approved electronic route. M24’s Oregon adapter currently creates a local JSON reference envelope, not an institutional 837I. M24 submissions are refused before a durable submission attempt or outbox event is created. This is intentional safety behavior and must remain in force until evidence-backed gates exist.

There are additional policy/claim-shape gaps even before transport: M24 derives a state/default plan from a trading-partner credential, has no persisted EOCCO MCO snapshot on assembled claims, has whole-day CHC math and no distinct in-home respite LOC, does not persist line end dates, and carries an Oregon FFS rate seed from FY2026. The public EOCCO manual reviewed is labeled 2025 (not 2026) and states hospice notice/CTI plus additional authorization after the initial six months; its current contractual effective date remains unconfirmed. M24 does not bind payer authorization evidence. Local `READY` therefore describes only the current local validation result, not payer authorization or sendability.

**Practical conclusion:** keep Oregon/EOCCO electronic Medicaid submission disabled. The first implementation lot should establish payer identity, responsibility, enrollment evidence and route qualification as distinct, dated, provider-entity-scoped facts. Only then should payer-specific claim policy, transport, remittance and controlled activation be scheduled.

## D. Architecture, security and route qualification

### Payer identity and enrollment

Current records can represent multiple organizations/provider entities and per-PE trading partner details. Credential mutation is guarded by permission, ABAC on the body’s provider entity, step-up, idempotency, and KMS-wrapped secret fields. This is a useful boundary. The credential record has a channel, optional MCO, endpoint/identifiers, active dates, and sandbox result, but no separate signed/approved evidence lifecycle for (a) OHA enrollment, (b) EOCCO contract/participation, (c) institutional 837I enrollment, (d) 835 ERA enrollment, (e) payer route certification, or (f) customer production authorization. Those states must not be inferred from a configured URL, successful synthetic test, or `activeFrom` credential.

Claim assembly currently sets `patientPayerId`, state and state-default plan but leaves MCO identity empty; the claim schema’s null MCO means FFS/Open Card. The readiness registry deliberately does not infer FFS when MCO is null and refuses both unresolved and managed-care Oregon routes. This prevents a ZIP-code/CCO-card shortcut from making a destination. Keep this guard until dated member/service responsibility and provider participation evidence are represented.

### Institutional path

Intended chain: M24 internal claim → qualified 837I document → tenant-selected Stedi/Claim.MD route → EOCCO/Moda payer → transport and payer acknowledgments → status → 835 ERA → M25 reconciliation. M24 currently stops before the first external submission: the Oregon adapter’s assembly is JSON, adapter metadata advertises an unsupported direct-MMIS/CCO channel, and the registry says format unverified / submission unavailable. Existing M23 has reusable X12/clearinghouse components and tenant routing patterns, but Medicare route behavior is not proof of Oregon Medicaid format, payer mapping, enrollment or EOCCO certification. M24 does not currently hand its internal line model through the M23 institutional renderer/tenant clearinghouse transport.

### Security and controls

Static review found tenant/provider entity scoping in credential schema and routes; KMS cipher columns; permission + ABAC + step-up for credential updates; organization scoping and `withOrgContext` in the M25 consumer; client cannot choose a submission destination; and refusal before attempt persistence. Those are positive design controls. This audit did not run RLS/live-role suites, inspect production secrets, exercise webhooks, or send vendor data. It therefore does not certify tenant isolation, encryption key handling, audit coverage or runtime authorization. Before activation, add evidence for immutable approval history, provider-entity-scoped enrollment, verified 835 account identity, inbound signature/replay handling, PHI-safe vendor/error logging, duplicate/retry behavior, and auditable manual overrides.

## E. End-to-end walkthrough and routing matrix

### Synthetic ordinary hospice claim trace

1. Operator selects patient, M6 admission and a service period; M24 reads M5 coverage and M6 LOC periods. Coverage payer identity/rank exists, but CCO/service responsibility is not proven by M5’s current representation.
2. Operator selects provider entity. M24 finds an active trading-partner credential, uses its `serviceState`, and selects the state-default plan; there is no dated EOCCO plan assignment inserted in claim MCO fields.
3. M24 builds LOC lines and state-seed prices. The builder’s LOC/date segments are local; CHC is charged in day units; line model persists one service date, not the returned segment end date. The rate seed is FY2026 and statewide.
4. Validation can reach local READY with seams explicitly unbound. EOCCO notice/PA, external enrollment, eligibility and payer route are not included as verified facts.
5. UI/route consults readiness. Oregon route is unavailable even if a gateway is configured. `POST .../submit` refuses at `write-service-drizzle.ts:2678–2684`, before submission row/outbox creation.
6. Therefore no 837I, clearinghouse response, payer claim number, 277CA/other acceptance, adjudication status, 835 ingestion or EOCCO M25 payment reconciliation occurs. This is the exact stop point.

# ADR-0079 — A request nobody is handling stays in front of the supervisors: a bounded watch on `failed_unreachable` requests, one supervisor attention alert (in-app always, a page to a verified endpoint when the policy is on, a visible ops notice when it is not), a stalled-escalation figure and watchdog, and fleet-wide ops-notice claims

- **Status:** **Accepted** (2026-10-19; decisions closed in the M36 gap-closure part B blueprint). **Provisional and OPEN for the clinical lead: who is paged (the supervisor roles), the reminder schedule and its cap, and that the supervisor alert uses the request class's platform floor.** **Provisional and OPEN for operations: the stall threshold, who receives the ops notices, and the choice of a real transport.** No clinical-lead sign-off is recorded. Nothing here turns anything on: the flags `messaging.escalation_timers`, `messaging.notification_policy` and `messaging.ack_requests` stay off by default.
- **Date:** 2026-10-19
- **Phase:** Messaging Hub Phase 4 (WP 4.11 follow-up, gap closure part B: G-3, G-4, G-5 of `docs/runbooks/messaging-escalation-fallback.md`).
- **Implements:** the gaps the WP 4.11 runbook recorded. **G-5** (a `failed_unreachable` request that nobody reassigns was watched by nothing after the moment it failed), **G-3** (supervisor alerts were in-app only and `route_unavailable` / `ladder_exhausted` raised no ops notice), **G-4** (no alert rule or stalled-worker check for the escalation queues; ops notices were per process and repeated hourly).
- **Extends:** [ADR-0071](ADR-0071-messaging-delivery-failure-handling.md) (decision 6, the `failed_unreachable` state), [ADR-0073](ADR-0073-messaging-notification-policy.md) (the platform floor and the verified-endpoint rule are reused, not copied), [ADR-0076](ADR-0076-messaging-unacknowledged-queue.md) decision 8 (the gauges and the threshold notice), [ADR-0078](ADR-0078-messaging-supervisor-reassign.md) (the supervisor's way out of a stuck request).
- **Amends:** ADR-0071 decision 6 ("`failed_unreachable` ... the deadline timer stops watching it") is **narrowed**: the timer still does not watch it, but a separate watch (decision 1) keeps it in front of the supervisors. ADR-0073 decision 1 ("the supervisors' `ladder_exhausted` / `failed_unreachable` alerts are not designated") is **superseded for the new supervisor alert only** (decision 3): the four assignee alerts and `DESIGNATED_EVENT_TYPES` are unchanged. ADR-0076's "per process, hourly" notice dedupe is replaced (decision 6).
- **Related:** [ADR-0010](ADR-0010-tenancy-rls-invariants.md), [ADR-0057](ADR-0057-escalation-ownership-m19-m36.md), [`docs/runbooks/messaging-escalation-fallback.md`](../runbooks/messaging-escalation-fallback.md).
- **Numbering note:** 0079 is the next free number after ADR-0078. The index's pre-existing irregularities (two ADR-0069 files, a gap before 0073) are unchanged.

## Context

Checked in the code, not assumed:

- The database guard of migration 1120/1126 allows `failed_unreachable` -> `acknowledged`, `resolved`, `pending`, `cancelled`, **not** `escalated`. Nothing can escalate such a request; only a person (acknowledge by the assignee, supervisor/party reassign) moves it. `listDue` and `isEscalationDue` therefore cover `pending` and `escalated` only, correctly.
- It becomes `failed_unreachable` when every out-of-band route to the assignee has terminally failed. It is loud **once** (the ops notice and the in-app alert of `ack-unreachable-notifications`), then silent: the unacknowledged queue and the gauges still show it, and nobody is told again.
- `ladder_exhausted` told the supervisors in-app once; `route_unavailable` told only the sender and the assignee; neither raised an ops notice. In production the router is the fail-closed deny-stub, so `route_unavailable` is the **normal** outcome of an overdue request until M19 binds a router.
- Every supervisor alert is seeded `channelsOverride: ["in_app"]`. The notification policy (ADR-0073) plans an out-of-band row only for the four **assignee** alerts (`DESIGNATED_EVENT_TYPES`), from the class floor and the recipient's **verified** endpoints, behind `messaging.notification_policy` and the event family's flag. The delivery-failure consumer counts only those four alert types as routes to the person who owes the response.
- **No vendor transport is configured in any environment.** A planned push/SMS row is settled `failed` / `transport_not_configured` by the dispatcher.
- The ops notices of ADR-0076 (`queue_overdue`, `latency_high`) are evaluated inside the per-minute `messaging.ack_escalation_sweep`, with a process-local `Map` for "once, then hourly": with several workers, or after a restart, the same breach notifies again at once. Being inside the sweep, they also cannot report that the sweep is not running.
- The platform has a Valkey connection (`REDIS_URL`) and `ioredis`; an ops notice sink (`platform-ops-notices.ts`: a log line plus the optional `OPS_ALERT_WEBHOOK_URL`). It has no Alertmanager rules and no vendor pager.

## Decisions

1. **A bounded watch on unreachable requests (G-5).** The per-minute sweep, after its escalation pass and for every active organisation whose `messaging.escalation_timers` flag is on, runs `createUnreachableWatch(...).watch(orgId)` (`ack-escalation/unreachable-watch.ts`). It reads the `failed_unreachable` requests whose deadline has passed (`AckTx.listUnreachableOverdue`, oldest first, at most 500) and, per request, raises a **stage** of the schedule below. It **writes nothing to the request** and sends nothing to the assignee's failing transport: the only effects are an ops notice and the supervisor attention alert (decision 2). A human moves the request (ADR-0078 supervisor reassign, a party's reassign, or the assignee's acknowledgment); a reassign returns it to `pending` and the deadline timer watches it again.
   - **Schedule (provisional, OPEN):** stage `k` opens at the sum of the first `k` unroutable backoffs and lasts the next one: at the deadline, +1 h, +3 h, +7 h, +15 h, +39 h, +63 h, +87 h; the stage windows are the unroutable backoff (1 h, 2 h, 4 h, 8 h, 16 h, 24 h, 24 h, 24 h). **Eight reminders at most** (`UNROUTED_MAX_RECORDS`), then nothing more is raised; the request stays on the queue and in the gauges. The schedule reuses `unroutedBackoffMs` so the two reminder families cannot drift apart.
   - **No hot loop:** a stage is raised once. The schedule is derived from the clock and the deadline, not stored, so a sweep that was down skips the stages it missed (it never replays them late) and the claim (decision 6) makes each stage fire once per fleet. The alert's correlation id is `ack-unwatched:<request>:<version>:<stage>`; a redelivery or a second worker (a flushed Valkey) converges on the same alert row (live suite 2). The version is in the id so a request that is reassigned and fails again starts a new schedule.
   - **Why not escalate it:** it would need a guard change (a new legal transition) and would send to a new tier user through routes that just failed for the previous one; a clinical decision, not made here. The router is still asked for `pending` / `escalated` requests exactly as before.
2. **One supervisor attention alert (G-3, G-5).** A new seeded in-app rule `m36.ack.supervisor_attention` (category `clinical_urgent`, severity floor `warn`, recipients the role set `clinical_supervisor` + `don`, the keys copied from `m36.medication_question.unrouted`; tenants re-aim it in the alert-rule admin; **not** pinned) is emitted by `raiseSupervisorAttention` (`ack-escalation/supervisor-attention.ts`) with a `reason` code: `ladder_exhausted`, `route_unavailable`, `failed_unreachable`, `unwatched`. The payload is allowlisted and fixed: `reason`, `requestId`, `ackClass`, one fixed sentence per reason, and a link to the queue (`/messaging/unacknowledged`, because a supervisor may not be a member of the conversation). No channel, message, patient or name. The supervisors are **removed** from the `ladder_exhausted` and `failed_unreachable` rules (they now go to the sender and the assignee only), so a supervisor gets one alert per event, not two. `ack-escalation-notifications` raises it on the **first** record of each unroutable type (`typeOccurrence === 1`), so `route_unavailable` now reaches the supervisors too; `ack-unreachable-notifications` raises it for `failed_unreachable`; the watch raises it for `unwatched`. The previous "later records go to the parties under the not-routed rule" conversion of the alert type is gone (the supervisors are no longer on that rule).
3. **Paging through the existing seam (G-3).** `m36.ack.supervisor_attention` is in a new platform constant `SUPERVISOR_PAGE_EVENT_TYPES`, which `designatedClassOf` accepts alongside `DESIGNATED_EVENT_TYPES`. With `messaging.notification_policy` and `messaging.ack_requests` on for the tenant (ADR-0073's flag rule, unchanged), the **class floor** (`important`/`urgent` = push, `critical` = push + SMS) is planned for each supervisor, **limited to their verified endpoints**, and quiet hours never delay it. The supervisor alert is deliberately **not** in `DESIGNATED_EVENT_TYPES`: that set is pinned to the four assignee alerts, drives the recipient pin, and is the only thing the delivery-failure machinery counts as "a route to the person who owes the response". A failed supervisor page therefore never escalates, never marks a request unreachable (live and unit tests).
4. **Degrade visibly, never silently (G-3).** `AlertEmitter.emit` now also returns `outOfBandPlanned` (the number of non-in-app deliveries it planned). When it is 0 for a supervisor attention alert (flags off, no verified endpoint, no supervisor resolved), `raiseSupervisorAttention` raises the ops notice `m36.ack.supervisor_unpaged` (ids, class, reason and the supervisor count). When a page **was** planned but the dispatcher failed it (every environment today: `transport_not_configured`), the delivery-failure consumer raises `m36.ack.supervisor_page_failed` (delivery id, channel). The in-app alert always stands. Event-level ops notices are added for `ladder_exhausted` and `route_unavailable` (first record) and `failed_unreachable_unwatched` (each stage); `m36.ack.failed_unreachable` is as before. All carry ids, counts and codes only.
5. **Alert rules and the stalled-worker check (G-4).**
   - **Seeded rule:** `m36.ack.supervisor_attention` (decision 2) is the rule for `ladder_exhausted`, `route_unavailable`, `failed_unreachable` and unwatched. It is seeded in code (`PLATFORM_DEFAULT_ALERT_RULES`, upserted at boot by `wireAlertRuleSeed`), the pattern every other `m36.ack.*` rule uses; there is **no migration** in this change.
   - **Gauge** `wardenx_m36_ack_escalation_stalled_seconds` (platform-wide maximum; 0 = none; `-1` = not measured), exposed with the ADR-0076 gauges. Per organisation it is the age past the deadline of the oldest `pending` / `escalated` request that the sweep **should already have handled**: overdue and not in its unroutable backoff (the exact predicate of `listDue`, now shared as `unroutedBackoffElapsed`), and only for a tenant whose `messaging.escalation_timers` flag is on (with it off nothing is stalled by design). A healthy worker clears such a request within about two sweep ticks. It covers both queues together: `messaging.ack_escalation_timer` jobs wake a request at its deadline and the per-minute `messaging.ack_escalation_sweep` finds it whether or not a job exists, so a request that stays unprocessed means neither is working for it. It cannot say which of the two is dead; the operator checks both (the runbook gives the order).
   - **Watchdog** (`bootstrap/ack-queue-watchdog.ts`): the same sample read on a plain 60 s timer in every backend process, independent of BullMQ, because the per-organisation notices run inside the sweep and cannot report that the sweep is dead. At or above 5 minutes (provisional, five ticks) it raises `m36.ack.escalation_stalled` (counts only), at most hourly while it lasts, re-armed on recovery. The scrape-side twin is the gauge: an operator alert on `> 300` plus an alert on `== -1`.
6. **Fleet-wide ops-notice claims (G-4).** `bootstrap/ops-notice-claims.ts`: `claim(key, ttl)` is `SET key NX PX ttl` on the platform Valkey; exactly one process holds a key until it expires or is released (a recovery releases it, so the next breach notifies at once). It replaces the process-local `Map` of `createAckQueueNoticeEvaluator` (`queue_overdue`, `latency_high`), and is used by the watch (per stage) and the watchdog. **Fail-open:** with no Valkey, or an error from it, the claim falls back to a process-local map, i.e. the previous behaviour; a duplicate notice is accepted over a silent breach. A Valkey flush can repeat a notice once.
7. **No new flag, no new table, no new migration, no new outbox event.** The watch and the watchdog run only where the existing flag and workers run; no event is added to the catalog and no consumer is added to `arch:outbox-bindings` (the existing consumers gained behaviour; `grep apps/backend/src/index.ts` shows `createUnreachableWatch`, `startAckQueueWatchdog` and the claims wired there). One optional field on a seam result (`outOfBandPlanned`) and one optional field on the delivery route state (`channel`).

## What this does NOT do (still needing a real transport or a decision)

- **It does not make a phone ring.** No push, SMS or voice vendor is configured anywhere; a planned page is settled `failed` / `transport_not_configured` and the platform says so (`supervisor_page_failed`). A supervisor is paged only after (a) an operations-chosen transport is configured, (b) the supervisor has a **verified** endpoint, (c) the tenant turns on `messaging.notification_policy` and `messaging.ack_requests`. Voice stays SOS-only. The OPEN go-live precondition (a named out-of-hours watcher) is **unchanged**: an ops notice is not a watcher.
- **Who is paged is not decided.** The audience is the role set `clinical_supervisor` + `don` (tenants may re-aim the rule). Whether the clinical lead should be paged, an on-call supervisor instead of a role, or a second level after N minutes, is a clinical decision (OPEN).
- **No automatic escalation of an unreachable request**, and no third reminder family beyond eight stages. After the last stage only the queue and the gauges show the request.
- **No sequential fallback** (push then SMS after N minutes unopened), as ADR-0073.
- **The ops notices go where the existing sink goes** (log line plus the optional `OPS_ALERT_WEBHOOK_URL`). Nobody is paged by them unless the operator routes that webhook; no Alertmanager rule is added (the operator writes them against the gauges, ops runbook section 16.8).
- **The watchdog's sample is platform-wide:** one organisation that cannot be read makes the check skip (the gauge reads `-1`), and it names no organisation.

## Alternatives considered

- **Re-arm the deadline timer for `failed_unreachable`.** Rejected: the timer's only action is an escalation the guard forbids from this state, so it would fire, find nothing to do, and need its own backoff; the watch is that backoff without a job.
- **A new history event type (`unwatched_reminder`) with an outbox event and consumer, mirroring `route_unavailable`.** Rejected for now: it needs a migration (enum), mirrors in `packages/db`, `packages/types`, the OpenAPI schema and the web wording, a catalog event and a binding, for a reminder whose durable dedupe is already given by the alert's unique correlation id plus the claim. Revisit if the timeline must show the reminders (it does not today).
- **Keep supervisors on the existing `ladder_exhausted` / `failed_unreachable` rules and designate those.** Rejected: the same alert also goes to the sender and the assignee, who would then be paged on routes that just failed, and those two alert types would have to join the set the delivery-failure machinery counts.
- **Add the supervisor alert to `DESIGNATED_EVENT_TYPES`.** Rejected: it is pinned to the four assignee alerts by a test, drives the rule pin (a tenant could no longer re-aim the audience) and the failure counting (decision 3).
- **A table of "last notified" per request for the notice dedupe.** Rejected (YAGNI): Valkey `SET NX PX` is the established short-lived-claim tool here, and losing it costs at most one duplicate notice.
- **Evaluate the stalled check inside the sweep.** Rejected: it cannot detect its own death (decision 5).

## Consequences

- With the flags off (every tenant today) nothing changes for requests; the supervisors still get the in-app attention alert once per event (instead of the previous in-app `ladder_exhausted` / `failed_unreachable` alert), `route_unavailable` now reaches them, and each is accompanied by an ops notice and, because no page is planned, `m36.ack.supervisor_unpaged`. **That is more ops notices than before, by design** (the gap was silence); an operator who does not want them leaves the escalation flag off.
- A tenant that turns the flags on and registers verified supervisor endpoints gets planned pages that fail until a transport exists; the ops sink says so per failed delivery (one notice per failed channel row, not deduplicated across supervisors).
- The supervisors' in-app inbox now holds `m36.ack.supervisor_attention` alerts; the web inbox renders them like other `clinical_urgent` alerts (fixed message, link to the queue).
- Tenants that had **re-aimed** the `ladder_exhausted` / `failed_unreachable` rule at other roles keep their tenant row (it shadows the platform rule) and so keep an in-app alert to those roles **in addition** to the new supervisor alert. They must re-aim `m36.ack.supervisor_attention` too (runbook).

# ADR-0096 — Messaging external partner share: one conversation, explicit, expiring, instantly revocable, read-only

- **Status:** **Proposed** (2026-10-23). Backend, the staff admin screen, the partner's read-only page and the staff invitation preview are built (see Implementation status), flag-off by default. Partner replies are a Proposed design only (D25), not built. Legal (BAA) review, clinical-lead sign-off on what may be shared, and compliance concurrence are NOT recorded; decisions D1 to D25 are the build team's defaults.
- **Date:** 2026-10-23
- **Phase:** Messaging Hub Phase 6, WP 6.6 (capability AW-10 "approved external partners").
- **Related:** [ADR-0087](ADR-0087-messaging-break-glass.md) and [ADR-0089](ADR-0089-messaging-export.md) (the grant, guard-trigger, step-up and flag patterns reused here), [ADR-0086](ADR-0086-messaging-retention-and-legal-hold.md), [ADR-0010](ADR-0010-tenancy-rls-invariants.md), the M2 auditor two-stage session (`apps/backend/src/modules/02-identity/auditor-enrolment.ts`, M2 spec 7.7), M31 portal engagement scope.

## Context

WP 6.6: "`external_share`: explicit, expiring, single-conversation scope for e.g. pharmacy/DME; partner identity via M31/M29 patterns; no PHI over out-of-band channels. Partner sees only shared conversations/resources; revocable instantly." Every other M36 reader is a workforce member (ADR-0010 tenancy plus channel membership). A partner is not a workforce member, has no `user` row, and must never gain one as a side effect (a user row would carry roles, a default organisation and the whole `messaging.read` surface). This is the largest new trust boundary in the module, so the default is as small as it can be: read one conversation, for a short time, through a surface that can only ever answer for that one share.

## Decision (proposed)

1. **D1: scope is exactly one conversation.** One `message_external_share` row names one `channel_id` (composite FK to `channel`). There is no multi-channel share, no patient scope, no "all my threads". Only `discipline`, `location` and `on_call` channels may be shared. `care_team` and `bereavement` (a patient subject, 42 CFR Part 2 exposure), `announcement` (a tenant-wide broadcast) and `dm` are refused with the same neutral 404 as an unknown channel. **Direct messages are deferred:** sharing a DM would hand a partner a conversation two people believed was private, so it needs every participant's consent, which the module has no way to record. **The creator must be able to view the channel by membership** (the same `canViewChannel` predicate the message routes use, WITHOUT the break-glass read grant): holding `messaging.share.manage` alone never reaches a conversation, and a non-member gets the same 404 as for an unknown channel. The check is in the service and, for the channel kind, repeated at every partner read (a channel cannot change kind, but the read is self-contained).
2. **D2: live, but only what was posted after the share, minus clinical content.** The partner sees messages in that channel with `created_at >= shared_from` (the database clock at creation, frozen by a guard trigger) that are: not deleted, not redacted (an approved redaction, ADR-0055), `body_kind` plain/markdown/rich (never `system`), and carry NO attachment, NO clinical link, NO structured entity reference (a patient or staff mention), no chart-promotion request, no acknowledgment request, and are not urgent or addressed to one recipient (`urgency <> 'routine'` or an `urgent_recipient_user_id`). A reply or quote is shown only when the message it points at is itself visible. The history before the share is never visible. *Why live and not a snapshot:* a snapshot freezes the first PHI-bearing answer and forces a second copy of message bodies outside the cipher; live-from-now keeps one encrypted store, one redaction path, one retention path, and makes revoke and expiry true cut-offs. *Why "after the share":* the people who wrote earlier messages did not know a partner would read them. *Why the exclusions:* attachments and clinical links are the highest-PHI content and the least reviewable, and a patient mention is how a message becomes patient-specific. "Explicitly marked shareable" content (a per-message opt-in) is deferred: nothing in this slice lets an attachment or clinical content reach a partner (open item 3). The exclusions are by structure, never by reading text, so a patient named in free text is NOT detected (known limit a).
3. **D3: expiry.** The share carries `expires_at`, default 7 days, at most 30 (`CHECK expires_at <= shared_from + 30 days` and `> shared_from`). A share cannot be extended: expiry is frozen; to continue, create a new share (a new `shared_from`, so the partner does not see the gap as continuous history either). A partner session never outlives its share.
4. **D4: revocation is instant because nothing is cached.** Every partner request resolves the session and the share in ONE SQL statement on the database clock: `share.revoked_at IS NULL AND share.expires_at > now() AND session.expires_at > now()`. No in-process or Valkey grant cache, no JWT, no "check at login". Revoking sets `revoked_at` (guard: once, by the acting user, forward only); the next partner request returns the uniform 404. Revoke never depends on the tenant flag and needs only the manage permission plus a fresh step-up.
5. **D5: partner identity (M31/M29-style, no user account).** A *partner access principal*, deliberately not a Better Auth session and not a `user`:
   - **Invitation (stage 0).** Creating a share returns, once, a 256-bit one-time invite token (`base64url`, 43 chars) and an 8-digit verification code. Only `sha256(token)` and an scrypt hash of the code are stored. The token is bound to its organisation in the redeem body (`organizationId` + `token`, the auditor-redeem shape) so the lookup runs under that tenant's RLS: a token presented against another tenant matches nothing.
   - **Enrolment stage.** Redeeming the token (single use, atomic `invite_redeemed_at` update) mints a partner session token (256-bit, stored as `sha256`, bearer in the `Authorization` header, so it is not ambient and needs no CSRF defence) in stage `enrolment`. That session reaches only `POST /partner/verify` and `GET /partner/shares/:id` (state). Everything else answers the uniform 404.
   - **Second factor, then active.** `POST /partner/verify` takes the 8-digit code, which the staff member who created the share must give the partner through a DIFFERENT channel than the link (a phone call). Five wrong attempts lock the share (`code_attempts`, a CHECK): only a staff REISSUE (D14) resets the counter, with a new invitation and a NEW code. On success the session moves to `active` (guard: forward only) AND its bearer is replaced by a fresh one (D13), and only then can it read.
   - *Departure from the auditor pattern, stated plainly:* auditors prove TOTP because they are `user` rows with an authenticator; a partner has none, so the second factor is the out-of-band code. It is weaker than TOTP (a code spoken once, valid for the share's life). TOTP/passkey for partners is an open item (4).
6. **D6: what a partner can post — nothing in this slice.** `access` is a column fixed to `'read'` by CHECK. A reply would need a message author kind other than `user`/`system`, a send path that bypasses channel membership, and clinical-lead review of what a pharmacy may write into the record; it is deferred (open item 2). A read-only partner also cannot mark anything read, react, or learn who else is a member.
7. **D7: every partner read is audited, fail closed.** Each successful read inserts one `message_external_share_read` row (share, session, count of messages returned) in the same transaction, before any byte is returned; if the insert fails the caller gets no messages. Both tables, plus the share, carry the 1107-style PHI audit triggers (the share audit row omits the invite and code hashes and the partner free text). Share creation and revocation also publish trail-only outbox events (`m36.external_share.created|revoked`, ids and instants only).
8. **D8: legal hold, redaction, retention.** **A message under an ACTIVE legal hold is NOT shown to a partner** (judged on the hold's live criteria in the same statement as the read, as the ADR-0089 export does, consistent with ADR-0095); releasing the hold shows it again. The share itself does not alter retention. **ANY `message_redaction` row hides the message from the partner, requested, pending or rejected as well as approved** (staff readers see a body only after approval; a partner gets the stricter rule because a request means someone already doubted the content). Retention destruction of a message removes it from the partner view; the destruction role may delete share rows (tenant offboarding), but `audit_event` rows survive.
9. **D9: no PHI out of band.** M36 sends the partner nothing. The invitation link and the code are shown once to the creator, who delivers them; the admin screen says link and code must travel separately. `partnerNotificationText()` is the single fixed string any future sender may use ("You have a new secure message. Sign in with your link to read it.") and takes no argument, so it cannot carry a name, a channel, a patient or a body. No email or SMS binding is built or wired (open item 5).
10. **D10: limits.** Redeem is budgeted per client address before any database work; **there is deliberately no per-organisation budget** (unauthenticated junk naming a tenant could otherwise exhaust it for real partners; the invitation is a 256-bit secret, so a distributed guess is not the threat). Verify has ONLY a per-client-address limiter plus the database cap of 5 attempts per share, which is the real control. The partner state and messages routes have a per-address budget (300 per minute) so a wrong bearer cannot be replayed for free. Reads are limited per share (60 per minute) and capped at 50 messages per page. The code has 5 attempts per share. A tenant may hold at most 10 live shares at once, and a creator at most 3 per rolling day (anti-sprawl).
11. **D11: flag and permission.** Tenant flag `messaging.external_partners` (absent = off). Off: creating a share is the uniform 404 and EVERY partner route is the uniform 404, so turning the flag off cuts all partner access at once, without touching rows. Listing and revoking shares need only the permission, never the flag (a share that exists stays revocable). New permission `messaging.share.manage`, granted by the migration to `compliance_officer`, `administrator`, `org_admin` only (existing tenants) and by the compliance_officer template (new tenants); no clinical, aide or other role holds it. A fresh step-up is required to create and to revoke (step-up key = permission key). Creation also requires a BAA reference (1 to 120 characters, a contract id or the like) the creator attests exists; the platform cannot verify it (open item 1).
12. **D12: uniform refusals.** The bearer is resolved BEFORE the verify body or the messages query is validated, so an unauthenticated caller never receives a 422 with detail; bad input after a live bearer is a 422. Unknown share, other tenant, other conversation, expired, revoked, flag off, wrong stage, bad token: the same 404 body. Distinguishing "expired" from "unknown" would be an oracle for share ids and a hint to the attacker; the partner view learns "access ended" from the 404 on the state route alone.
13. **D13: session life and rotation.** A partner session dies, with the uniform 404, after **30 idle minutes** (`EXTERNAL_SESSION_IDLE_MINUTES`; `last_seen_at`, touched by the very statement that resolves the bearer, on the database clock, no cache) or **12 hours in total** (`EXTERNAL_SESSION_MAX_HOURS`; `expires_at`, CHECKed `<= created_at + 12 h` and never past the share), whichever comes first. Past either, the partner redeems a NEW invitation, which staff reissue (D14); the old bearer cannot be revived. The idle bound is also in the read-trail guard. **Activation rotates the bearer:** `verify` replaces the enrolment token hash in the same update that moves the stage to `active` (the guard allows a `token_hash` change only together with that transition) and returns the new token once, so an enrolment token captured earlier is useless afterwards. A touch of `last_seen_at` alone writes no audit row (the audit function skips it). The names and numbers are constants in `packages/types` and pinned against the SQL by a test.
14. **D14: reissue.** `POST /external-shares/:id/reissue` (permission `messaging.share.manage`, a fresh step-up, the tenant flag, an outbox) replaces the invitation and the verification code of a LIVE share: it bumps `invite_generation` (every session of an older generation is dead in the same statement that checks liveness, with no session row touched), clears `invite_redeemed_at`, restarts `code_attempts` at zero and returns the new token and a NEW 8-digit code once. **Like create, it requires the manager to be able to view the channel by membership (the plain predicate, no break-glass); a manager who cannot gets the SAME 404 as for an unknown share and nothing changes** (new credentials re-open a conversation, so a share listed to an administrator must not let them read a private channel). Revoke needs no such check: it only removes access. The guard trigger makes reissue the ONLY path that resets the counter or clears the redemption: it demands the acting user as `reissued_by`, new secrets for both, one generation at a time, a live share, and a zero start, and any other update of those columns is refused. Audited by the share's audit trigger (generation and actor, never a hash or a code) and a trail-only `m36.external_share.reissued` event (ids only). Expiry is NOT extended by a reissue. The staff screen asks for a confirmation first (one click ends the partner's access), and so does Revoke.
15. **D15: minimum necessary.** Creating a share requires `acknowledgedMinimumNecessary: true` in the API (422 otherwise) and the staff dialog's checkbox: the creator confirms that an outside party will read the conversation and that patient names in free text are NOT filtered. It is stored on the share (`minimum_necessary_acknowledged`, CHECKed true, frozen) and audited with the insert.
16. **D16: free-text identifiers are withheld, names are not detected.** Names cannot be filtered reliably and this ADR does not pretend to. As defence in depth, the plaintext the partner reader already decrypts is run through a pure matcher (`packages/types/src/external-share-identifiers.ts`, fixture-tested with positive and negative tables) and a message that matches is **withheld, never masked**. **Normalisation first:** NFKC (fullwidth letters and digits become ASCII), every format character deleted (zero-width space and joiner, soft hyphen, bidi marks), every other Unicode decimal digit (Arabic-Indic, Devanagari, ...) mapped to ASCII. **A collapsed second view** deletes whitespace between digits and the dashes of a chain of three or more digit groups, so `1 2 3 4 5 6`, `123-456-789` and `12 34 56` count as a run of six digits; a two-part dosing range (`250-1000 mg`) is not joined. **Covered:** SSN-like numbers; any run of 6 or more digits in either view (record, account, member numbers, phones and 9-digit SSNs without separators); the labels MRN, DOB, DOS, SSN, ACCT and the account/policy/member/patient-id forms, also glued to what follows (`MRN123`, `DOB:4/12/50`, not inside words such as dose or dobutamine); numeric, ISO and named dates with years 1800 to 2099; US phone numbers; email addresses (checked with a bounded, linear scan, not a backtracking regex; an 8000-character hostile body is tested to finish fast); street addresses (street, avenue, road, boulevard, drive, lane, highway, terrace). **Omission, not a placeholder:** a placeholder row would confirm that a sensitive-looking message exists and force cursor handling; the cost is that a reply can appear without its parent, which the partner cannot see and we accept. The matcher runs on the server on the already-decrypted body, never logs it, and the read-trail row records only how many messages were withheld (`withheld_count`). **FALSE-POSITIVE COST (accepted, fail closed):** any six-digit number is withheld (a lot, order or accession number) and so is an UNLABELLED list of number groups separated only by spaces or dashes that totals six digits (`120 80 76 98`). Labelled vitals and labs (`BP 120/80, HR 88, INR 2.5, K 4.1, glucose 142 mg/dL`) pass, because a letter, slash, colon, comma or decimal point always sits between the numbers, so the collapsed view never joins them; the fixture table pins both sides. **This reduces, and does not eliminate, the exposure. NOT detected:** names and nicknames, relationships, diagnoses and drugs tied to a person, indirect identifiers ("the lady in room 214 whose daughter lives in Boise"), spelled-out digits, letter homoglyphs for digits, an identifier split by letters (`M R N`), seven-digit local numbers, two-part dates (10/23), five-digit numbers (rooms, lots, zip codes), dot-separated digit groups outside a phone pattern, and the short street suffixes st/dr/ct/way. A message it misses is shown as typed.
17. **D17: trusted proxy, fail closed.** The per-address budgets key on `request.ip`, which Fastify takes from the socket peer unless the peer is in `TRUSTED_PROXY_CIDRS`. Partner access is **available only when NODE_ENV is explicitly `development` or `test`, OR the list is present and VALID**; production, staging, `prod`, a typo and an unset NODE_ENV all need a valid list. **Validation** (`bootstrap/trusted-proxies.ts`, the one reading used by Fastify's `trustProxy`, the realtime client key and this gate): every entry must be an IPv4 or IPv6 literal, a CIDR with a prefix of 1 or more (never `/0`, in either family), or one of proxy-addr's keywords `loopback`, `linklocal`, `uniquelocal`; wildcards (`*`), booleans (`true`), bare numbers (a hop count, `1`), malformed addresses and prefixes (`/33`, `/abc`, `/-1`) and wrong-case keywords are rejected. **One bad entry poisons the whole list.** Today proxy-addr under Fastify THROWS at boot on most of these (`invalid range on address: 0.0.0.0/0`, `invalid IP address: garbage`) but accepts a bare `1`; now the parse returns `false` (trust nobody, the safe default) instead of crashing the boot or passing a hop count through. When the gate says unavailable, the **partner routes are not mounted** (they answer the framework 404), **one boot warning names the offending entries** (or says the variable is unset), and staff **create and reissue answer `PARTNER_ACCESS_UNAVAILABLE` (HTTP 503)** with the message "partner access needs trusted proxy configuration", so a share nobody can use is never made. List and revoke still work. Verified by inject: from an untrusted peer a spoofed `X-Forwarded-For` does not move the key; from a trusted peer the forwarded address does (an extra client-supplied hop does not win); a poisoned list trusts nobody. Documented in `deploy/production/TENANT_EDGE.md`. **Residual:** a syntactically valid but over-broad list that includes a hop the attacker controls (for example a large public CIDR) is still the operator's responsibility; only /0 is refused.
18. **D18: members see it.** The channel detail a member already reads (`GET /channels/:id`) carries `externalShare: {active: true, partnerKind, expiresAt}`, computed live from the share table on every read (revoked or expired: absent; two active shares: the one that ends last). **Members only:** the route's read predicate also admits break-glass readers, so the handler asks the plain membership predicate (`canViewChannel`, no grant) before computing the indicator and a non-member reader never receives it. **It says access has been GRANTED, not that it is in use:** a share the partner has not opened, or whose code is locked, still counts, because it can become usable the moment the partner redeems it; the web copy reads "An outside partner has been given read access to new messages in this conversation until <date>." The thread shows it as a persistent status line (`<output>`). It uses the SAME channel-detail request the presence hook already makes (`use-channel-detail.ts` shares an in-flight or fresh read), so opening a thread costs one request and the banner then re-reads once a minute, pausing while the tab is hidden and reading again when it returns. It clears on a 403 or 404, keeps what it shows through a network or server error, and never shows one channel's banner on another. **The banner follows the flag exactly as partner reads do:** with `messaging.external_partners` off every partner route is already the uniform 404, so no partner can read and the banner is absent too; turning the flag back on restores both. No system message is written into the channel (the module has no cheap writer).

### Amendment S6 to S8 (2026-10-23, still Proposed): the partner's page, the staff preview, and replies as a design only

19. **D19: the partner page lives in `apps/web`, as a chromeless route `/partner/share`, and is NOT the staff app's session.** *Where, and why not elsewhere.* `apps/web` already serves anonymous, link-opened pages outside the shell (`/auditor/redeem`, `/invite/:token`, `chrome: false`), on the tenant host, behind the same nginx and CSP, with the kit and the axe/DOM test harness; the partner API is same-origin with it, so `connect-src 'self'` already covers it and no CORS or second origin appears. `apps/portal` is rejected: it is the patient/family consumer PWA under `/portal/` with its own session guard and patient context, and putting an organisation's outside vendor there would blur two identity populations that must stay separate. A new app is rejected: a third build, nginx location, CSP and release pipeline for one page, and the pull to give it its own auth. *No second auth system and no staff session:* the page does not use the shared API client at all. `partner-api.ts` is a plain `fetch` with `credentials: "omit"` (the staff cookie can never ride along), `cache: "no-store"`, `referrerPolicy: "no-referrer"`, `redirect: "error"`, no CSRF or idempotency middleware and none of the client's global 401/503 watcher (which would bounce a partner to the staff sign-in or the maintenance page). The principal is still only the D5 bearer. *Link shape:* `/partner/share#o=<organisationId>&t=<token>`. Both values are in the **fragment** (never sent to a server, so absent from access logs, proxies and `Referer`), nothing is in the query, and no share id, partner name, channel or patient is ever in a link; the page reads the fragment once and rewrites the address bar and history entry to the bare path. **Nothing is redeemed until the partner presses "Open the conversation"**, so a link scanner that merely loads the page cannot burn the single-use token (it can still redeem it if it executes the page and clicks, which limit (k) already names). Reaching the route with the flag off still renders the page; every partner API then answers the uniform 404, so the partner sees the neutral state and learns nothing about the flag.
20. **D20: the flow, the one neutral state, and what the page never touches.** Steps: link (button) → redeem → code (8 digits, `autocomplete="one-time-code"`, numeric keypad) → verify → read. **Every refusal the server maps to the uniform 404 (also a 401/403) shows ONE state**, with one fixed text ("This link is not available. It may have ended, been replaced, or never worked. If you were expecting access, ask the person who sent it to you."): revoked, expired, code-locked, flag off, wrong organisation, unknown, wrong stage, a malformed or missing fragment, and a revoke or expiry that lands mid-read. It never says which, so the page adds no oracle to the 404 contract (D12); a malformed link makes no network call at all. The only partner-visible distinctions are those that need a live bearer or are about the caller: *"That code is not correct"* (HTTP 422, only reachable with a live enrolment bearer; **it never states attempts remaining**, so the cap of 5 is not advertised), *"Too many attempts. Wait a minute"* (429, per client address) and *"We could not reach the service"* (network or 5xx, the form is kept). **No PHI or secret in any ambient place:** the document title is the constant `Secure message` (and is restored on leaving); the page adds `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` and `robots noindex, nofollow` while mounted; the address bar carries no identifier after load; nothing is written to `localStorage`, `sessionStorage`, IndexedDB or a cookie; the page calls no `console` method and every failure is a closed kind mapped to fixed copy, never response text; messages are plain React text (no markup is parsed, no URL is auto-linked, `white-space: pre-wrap`). A test asserts the title, storage, console, URL and the request flags.
21. **D21: the bearer lives in memory only (the ADR's bearer decision, applied).** D5 chose an `Authorization` bearer so nothing is ambient and no CSRF defence is needed; the page therefore keeps the session token in a React ref: not state, not storage, not a cookie. An HttpOnly cookie was considered and rejected for this slice: it would be ambient (CSRF on `verify`), need server `Set-Cookie`/`SameSite`/path work and a second revocation surface, and the ADR's "nothing cached, one statement" argument is simplest with a header. **Cost, stated plainly:** a reload, a crash or closing the tab ends the session, and because the invitation is single use the partner then needs a staff **reissue** (D14). That is acceptable for a first, flag-off slice and is open item 8.
22. **D22: staff see what the partner will see, as a SYNTHETIC preview, before and after creating.** `PartnerPreview` renders the SAME `PartnerConversationView` component the partner page renders (so the first-page shape cannot drift), with two fixed example messages labelled "Example", under the plain-language rules of D2 and D16 (shown, never shown, withheld not masked, not detected). It appears in the create dialog **above the minimum-necessary checkbox**, so the acknowledgement is informed, and again in the one-time panel beside the link. *Synthetic and not live-filtered, because:* a "preview as the partner" of real content needs a staff-facing route that runs the partner reader (a second, content-bearing read path with its own audit and its own opportunity to leak), shows the sharer the very content the filter exists to second-guess, and cannot exist before the share does (the dialog is pre-create). The cost is that a preview cannot catch a particular message; the copy says the example text is not their messages. **Delivery stays manual and the UI says so:** the panel states that ChartWarden does not send the link or the code, no approved covered channel exists, and that staff must hand the link over by a channel they trust and the code by phone. The panel now shows one **Invitation link** instead of a raw organisation id and token (the link is the only form the partner page accepts). No email or SMS is built (D9, open item 5).
23. **D23: session limits are enforced in the page too, and every partner response is uncacheable.** Polling a conversation touches the server's `last_seen_at` (D13), so a tab left open would keep a session alive for the full 12 hours. The page therefore counts only the partner's own input (pointer, key, touch, wheel): after **30 minutes without input** (`EXTERNAL_SESSION_IDLE_MINUTES`) or **12 hours** since redeem (`EXTERNAL_SESSION_MAX_HOURS`) it drops the bearer and every message from memory and shows the idle text ("This session ended after 30 minutes of inactivity and the messages were cleared from this page"; the page knows its own clock, so this is not an oracle) or, for the absolute limit, the neutral state. The conversation is re-read every 15 seconds **while the tab is visible** (also on becoming visible) and by a keyboard-reachable **Refresh** button; a failed refresh keeps what is shown with an alert; the first 404 ends the session. A re-read follows the cursor from the start for at most 4 pages (200 messages) and replaces the list (a withheld row at a page boundary still advances the cursor; live-tested), costing at most 16 reads a minute against the 60 per-share budget. **Server side:** the live suite found that a thrown 404/422/429 on a partner route carried no `Cache-Control` (only handler successes did), and a 404 is heuristically cacheable; an `onSend` hook now sets `Cache-Control: no-store` on every `/api/v1/messaging/partner/*` response, with `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` and `nosniff` (already global) asserted on successes and refusals. The SPA shell is a static, PHI-free asset and keeps its existing nginx headers (`strict-origin-when-cross-origin` for the document; the page and every fetch override it with `no-referrer`); no nginx change was needed or made.
24. **D24: accessibility.** Labelled code field (visible label, not a placeholder), 44px (2.75rem) targets, one `h1` per step that takes focus when the step changes, errors in `role="alert"`, the conversation in a `role="log"` region with `aria-live="polite"` and an accessible name, timestamps in `<time>`, no colour-only state, focus-visible from the kit. axe (without colour contrast, which jsdom cannot compute) is clean on the link, code and reading steps and on the staff preview.
25. **D25: partner replies (`messaging.external_partner_replies`, off) are a PROPOSED DESIGN, NOT BUILT.** *Why not built:* a reply is a message by an author who is not a `user`; the table allows that shape (`author_kind` is an enum with a `(author_kind = 'user') = (author_user_id IS NOT NULL)` check) but adding a kind touches every consumer that assumes a staff author (hydration, search tokenisation, export, redaction, mention and ack parsers, outbox consumers, retention classes, the M36 ack/urgent ratchets), and four decisions below (inbound identifier policy, notification, clinical-lead review of what a pharmacy may write into a record, compliance concurrence) are unmade. Shipping a half-safe write path into the record is worse than no replies. The design the team proposes, for sign-off:
    - **R1 flags and gates.** A SEPARATE tenant flag `messaging.external_partner_replies` (absent = off) that is only effective while `messaging.external_partners` is on; off, `POST .../partner/shares/:id/replies` is the uniform 404. The share carries `replies_allowed boolean not null default false`, set by staff at create and frozen (a read-only share can never be upgraded; create a new one), so a partner reply needs BOTH the tenant flag and the share's own opt-in. Needs the same bearer, active stage, and one-statement liveness (D4) as a read.
    - **R2 attribution.** New `message_author_kind` value `external_partner`; `author_user_id` stays NULL; new nullable `message.external_share_id uuid` (no foreign key: offboarding may delete share rows and the message must survive; org-scoped by RLS and the existing composite keys) and `message.external_author_label text` (a snapshot of the staff-chosen `partner_name`, so a later rename or share deletion cannot change who spoke). A CHECK ties them: `(author_kind = 'external_partner') = (external_share_id IS NOT NULL AND external_author_label IS NOT NULL)`. A guard trigger refuses an `external_partner` message unless its share is live on the database clock, belongs to the same channel and organisation and has `replies_allowed`, and refuses any update of those columns. This is the migration reserved as **1203**; it also needs an enum-add migration split (an enum value cannot be used in the transaction that adds it). No `user` row ever exists.
    - **R3 what a reply may contain.** Request body `{ text, nonce }` only (`additionalProperties: false`): no attachment, mention, acknowledgment request, urgent flag, clinical link, quote or reply-to, no markdown or rich body (`body_kind = 'plain'`). Size cap **2 000 characters** (staff cap is larger; a partner needs a sentence, not an essay). The server sets every other field. `nonce` (client-generated) plus a unique `(external_share_id, nonce)` makes a retried POST idempotent.
    - **R4 limits.** Per share **5 replies per minute and 30 per hour**, per client address on the same limiter as `verify`; the trusted-proxy fail-closed gate (D17) applies unchanged (replies are unavailable wherever partner routes are). A share is capped at **200 replies** in total; the 201st is a 429 that tells the partner to contact the organisation. Rate limiting is per share first, because address-only limits are evaded.
    - **R5 inbound identifier policy: REJECT, do not withhold.** The matcher (D16) runs on the inbound text. A match answers 422 `EXTERNAL_REPLY_NOT_SENT` ("This message was not sent. It appears to contain a personal number or detail; remove it and send again"), nothing is stored, and neither the text nor the matched kind is logged. *Why reject:* "withhold from staff" would store the partner's identifiers (a record of data of unknown provenance, against minimum necessary) and, worse, leave a partner believing a message was delivered that staff never see; a pharmacist's "do not give this patient X" vanishing silently is a patient-safety hazard. *Cost:* the 422 teaches an adversary the matcher; the per-share limits bound probing, and the matcher was never a security boundary. *Unmatched* PHI (names, indirect details) is stored as typed and is staff-visible only, with the badge; this is the same limit (j), now on the write side. Open sign-off.
    - **R6 how staff see it.** A reply renders to channel members only (the existing membership read paths; a non-member and a break-glass reader without membership get the existing rules, never a new bypass) with an unmistakable, non-colour badge **"External - <partner label>"** in the message header, the label text from the snapshot, no avatar, no link auto-detection, no mention rendering, and no reaction/reply/forward/pin/promote-to-chart/ack actions. Search indexing, global search, AI summarisation and any LLM context EXCLUDE external replies until separately reviewed (untrusted text from outside the organisation must not enter a model prompt unfiltered). Export includes them with the badge.
    - **R7 notifications.** A reply NEVER triggers a staff push, email or SMS containing its content. At most the fixed generic text of ADR-0073 ("New message in a shared conversation"), to members who already have the channel unmuted, and only one per channel per 15 minutes; mention, ack and urgent pipelines are not entered at all. Whether to notify at all is an open sign-off (alert fatigue and a partner who can ring a bell are the cost).
    - **R8 retention, hold, redaction.** The message follows the channel's retention class and legal hold (ADR-0086, ADR-0095) exactly as a staff message does; redaction (ADR-0055) works unchanged; deleting a share never deletes its replies, and an offboarded tenant's destruction role removes them with the channel.
    - **R9 revoke and expiry.** A reply's write checks share and session liveness in the same statement that inserts (`INSERT ... SELECT ... WHERE EXISTS (live share and session on the database clock)`), and the guard trigger re-checks inside the transaction; after a revoke a reply is the uniform 404 and the message is not written. A revoke that commits after the insert statement has taken its snapshot is ordered after that reply (the linearisation point is the insert); this is the same bound as a read in flight.
    - **R10 audit and events.** The reply insert is audited by the message audit trigger like any message (author kind and share id, never the text); an outbox event carries ids only. The partner page would gain a composer only when both flags and `replies_allowed` are on, with the same neutral state on any refusal and a character counter and polite status for the result.
    - **Residual risks to sign off:** an outside party can place text in the record staff act on (phishing "call this number" in a trusted channel, instructions that look clinical), a stolen bearer can speak as the partner, a reject-oracle (above), and the label is a name staff typed (impersonation of a different company is a staff error the badge cannot catch).

## Threat model

| Threat | Control |
|---|---|
| IDOR across shares (guess another share id) | Partner routes take the share id in the URL only to compare it with the session's own share; mismatch is the uniform 404. There is no list-shares route for a partner. |
| IDOR across tenants | The org comes from the redeem body for the invite and from the token prefix for the session; every query is under `withOrgContext` (FORCE RLS); the composite FKs make a cross-tenant link unrepresentable. |
| Enumeration of share ids / tokens | Ids and tokens are random (122 and 256 bits); refusals are uniform; redeem/verify are rate limited per IP and org; code attempts are capped per share. |
| Privilege creep into other conversations | The reader's only channel is `share.channel_id` read from the row in the same statement as the session; no channel id ever comes from a request; no `user` row exists, so no staff permission or ABAC predicate can match. |
| Expiry bypass | Expiry is judged by the database clock in the same statement as the read; no cache; sessions are bounded by the share. |
| Stolen session token | 256-bit bearer, hashed at rest, bound to a stage and a share; dies with the share; revocation is instant. A stolen token before the code step reaches nothing, and it is replaced when the code is proven (D13). Dead after 30 idle minutes or 12 hours (D13). Not device-bound (known limit c). |
| Brute-forcing the 8-digit code | 5 attempts per share in a CHECK-bounded counter, never reset; scrypt-hashed. |
| Replay of the invitation | Single use (atomic update); the token is useless after redemption. |
| Content in notifications | M36 sends none; the fixed text takes no input; events carry ids only. |
| Staff unaware a partner reads their channel | Share creation is audited and announced on a trail event; members see a live banner on the channel (D18), and the creator confirmed the minimum-necessary statement (D15). |
| A link scanner or prefetcher burns the invitation | Nothing is redeemed until the partner presses the button; the token rides the fragment (never fetched by a plain GET); limit (k) remains for a scanner that runs the page and clicks (D19). |
| The partner page leaks PHI or a secret through URL, title, storage, console, Referer or cache | Fixed title, fragment scrubbed on load, bearer in memory only, no console or storage use, `no-referrer` on the page and every fetch, `no-store` on every partner response including refusals (D20, D21, D23); asserted by DOM and live tests. |
| A refusal distinguishes revoked from never-existed | One neutral state for every 404/401/403 and for a malformed link (no call made); the wrong-code 422 exists only for a live bearer and states no attempts left (D20); live test compares refusal bodies byte for byte. |
| A tab left open keeps a session alive past its idle bound | The page counts only the partner's input and drops the bearer and messages after 30 minutes or 12 hours (D23); a modified client can still poll to 12 hours (limit t). |
| Staff approve a share without knowing what the partner sees | Synthetic preview above the acknowledgement and beside the link, from the same component the partner uses (D22); example text, not the real conversation (limit w). |
| An outside party writes into the record (replies, proposed) | Not built. Separate flag, per-share opt-in, reject-on-match, size/rate caps, plain text only, badge for staff, no notifications with content, excluded from search and AI (D25). |
| A compromised administrator shares widely | Permission limited to three roles, step-up, caps (10 live per tenant, 3 per creator-day), BAA reference, audit on every create, revoke and read. |

## Known limits

- (a) Patient names in free text are not detected; the exclusions are structural. Content the sender types about a patient is visible to the partner. The share is for non-clinical coordination conversations; the admin screen says so, and clinical-lead review is open.
- (b) The second factor is a spoken code, not a device-bound authenticator.
- (c) Session tokens are not device- or IP-bound.
- (d) A partner sees author display names of staff who posted after the share.
- (e) Edits after the share are visible as edited text; the pre-edit revision is never shown (revisions are not read).
- (f) The 7/30-day expiry, the 10/3 caps and the 60-per-minute read limit are defaults, not agreed values.
- (g) The BAA reference is an attestation, not a verified link to a contract record.
- (h) The per-address budgets mean something only when `TRUSTED_PROXY_CIDRS` is right. Without a valid list (and outside development/test) the feature is OFF (D17); a valid-looking list that includes a hop the attacker controls still lets a spoofed `X-Forwarded-For` pick its own bucket. Only the real proxy hops belong in the list.
- (i) Session life is bounded (D13) but not bound to a device or address: a stolen active token works for up to 30 idle minutes or 12 hours from anywhere, and reissue is the only recovery.
- (j) Free text is only partly filtered (D16): names, indirect identifiers and anything the matcher does not list reach the partner as typed, and a determined sender can evade pattern rules (spelled-out digits, homoglyphs, letters between digits). The staff acknowledgement (D15) exists because of this limit, not instead of it. The matcher also withholds harmless messages: any six-digit number and an unlabelled run of number groups that totals six digits.
- (k) The invitation can be consumed by a link scanner or prefetcher before the partner opens it (single use); the creator then revokes and creates a new share. The partner still needs the out-of-band code.
- (l) `code_hash` protects an 8-digit number: a leaked `message_external_share` row is brute-forceable offline (about 10^8 guesses). The 5-attempt cap does not help against a stolen row.
- (m) Sharing a discipline, location or on-call channel is NOT 42 CFR Part 2 gated: only `care_team` and `bereavement` are excluded, and patient names in free text are not detected, so a Part 2 discussion in an ordinary channel could reach a partner.
- (n) Lockout leaves a durable trail: every wrong attempt (and the fifth, `code_attempts = 5`) is an `audit_event` row written by the share's audit trigger, without the code or its hash; no separate event row is written.
- (o) The member banner is read from the channel detail, not pushed: it can lag a revoke by up to a minute on an open thread (and while the tab is hidden), and it is absent whenever the flag is off. Members who never open the channel are not told, and no message about it is written into the channel. It states that access was granted, which can overstate (the partner may never have opened the invitation).
- (p) Withheld messages are omitted, so a partner can see a reply without the message it answers (D16).
- (q) A reissue does not extend the share: the expiry stays frozen, so the partner's total access never exceeds the original window.
- (r) The page re-reads at most 200 messages (4 pages of 50, oldest first). A longer conversation shows its first 200 since the share and newer messages are not reachable from the page until the server offers a tail read. Shares last at most 30 days, but a busy channel can pass this.
- (s) The bearer is memory-only (D21): a reload, a crash or closing the tab ends the session and the partner needs a staff reissue. The invitation fragment is scrubbed from the address bar on load, so it cannot be re-opened either.
- (t) The 30-minute idle bound in the page is cooperative: it protects an honest tab left open. A modified client can poll the server and keep the server-side `last_seen_at` fresh up to the 12-hour bound.
- (u) Some mail systems and chat apps rewrite links, drop fragments or open them in an in-app browser; a link whose fragment is lost shows the neutral state and needs a reissue or another channel. The fragment is also in the partner's browser history entry until the page rewrites it on load.
- (v) The partner page is served by the staff SPA bundle: the partner downloads the app's JavaScript (no secrets in it) and the SPA's own start-up requests run with no session. This was reasoned and unit-tested, not exercised in a real browser against the real shell.
- (w) The staff preview is synthetic: it shows the shape and the rules, never a particular message, and its rule text can drift from the server's filter; a test pins the component, not the rules.
- (x) The page shows author display names of staff exactly as D2 limit (d) says, and a partner can still forward what they read; nothing here stops copying.

## Open human items

1. **Legal/BAA:** is a free-text reference enough, or must a partner directory with a stored BAA exist first? Which partner kinds (pharmacy, DME, other) may be shared at all?
2. **Replies:** may a partner post? D25 proposes the design (separate flag, per-share opt-in, a new `external_partner` author kind, reject-on-identifier-match, 2 000 characters, 5/min and 30/hour per share, plain text, badge for staff). Sign-offs needed: clinical lead on what a pharmacy may write into the record; compliance on storing partner-typed text and on reject vs withhold (R5); whether staff are notified at all (R7); exclusion from search and AI (R6); the schema change across every message consumer.
3. **Shareable marking:** may attachments or clinical links ever be shared, and who marks them?
4. **Partner authentication strength:** is the out-of-band code acceptable, or must partners enrol TOTP/passkey?
5. **Notification delivery:** who sends the invitation and the "you have a message" text, and through which vendor under which BAA?
6. **Member awareness:** the live banner is built (D18); is it enough, or must a system message also be written into the channel when a share starts and ends?
7. Compliance and clinical-lead concurrence on D1 to D12.
8. **Session continuity:** is a memory-only bearer (D21, reload = reissue) acceptable for partners, or should an HttpOnly, path-scoped cookie with its own CSRF and revocation design replace it?
9. **Hosting:** concurrence that the partner page belongs in `apps/web` (D19) and not in a dedicated app, plus an edge decision on whether `/partner/*` should carry its own `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` and `Cache-Control: no-store` at nginx (today the page and its fetches set both themselves).
10. **Tail read:** a server-side tail or "since" read so a long conversation shows its newest messages (limit r).
11. **Preview:** is a synthetic example enough for the minimum-necessary acknowledgement (D22), or must staff be able to see the real filtered output before creating?
12. **Delivery:** unchanged (item 5); until a BAA'd transport exists the staff copy states that staff hand the link and the code over themselves.

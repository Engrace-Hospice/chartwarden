# ADR-0096 — Messaging external partner share: one conversation, explicit, expiring, instantly revocable, read-only

- **Status:** **Proposed** (2026-10-23). Backend and the staff admin screen are built (see Implementation status), flag-off by default. Legal (BAA) review, clinical-lead sign-off on what may be shared, and compliance concurrence are NOT recorded; decisions D1 to D18 are the build team's defaults.
- **Date:** 2026-10-23
- **Phase:** Messaging Hub Phase 6, WP 6.6 (capability AW-10 "approved external partners").
- **Related:** [ADR-0087](ADR-0087-messaging-break-glass.md) and [ADR-0089](ADR-0089-messaging-export.md) (the grant, guard-trigger, step-up and flag patterns reused here), [ADR-0086](ADR-0086-messaging-retention-and-legal-hold.md), [ADR-0010](ADR-0010-tenancy-rls-invariants.md), the M2 auditor two-stage session (`apps/backend/src/modules/02-identity/auditor-enrolment.ts`, M2 spec 7.7), M31 portal engagement scope.

## Context

WP 6.6: "`external_share`: explicit, expiring, single-conversation scope for e.g. pharmacy/DME; partner identity via M31/M29 patterns; no PHI over out-of-band channels. Partner sees only shared conversations/resources; revocable instantly." Every other M36 reader is a workforce member (ADR-0010 tenancy plus channel membership). A partner is not a workforce member, has no `user` row, and must never gain one as a side effect (a user row would carry roles, a default organisation and the whole `messaging.read` surface). This is the largest new trust boundary in the module, so the default is as small as it can be: read one conversation, for a short time, through a surface that can only ever answer for that one share.

## Decision (proposed)

1. **D1: scope is exactly one conversation.** One `message_external_share` row names one `channel_id` (composite FK to `channel`). There is no multi-channel share, no patient scope, no "all my threads". Only `discipline`, `location` and `on_call` channels may be shared. `care_team` and `bereavement` (a patient subject, 42 CFR Part 2 exposure), `announcement` (a tenant-wide broadcast) and `dm` are refused with the same neutral 404 as an unknown channel. **Direct messages are deferred:** sharing a DM would hand a partner a conversation two people believed was private, so it needs every participant's consent, which the module has no way to record. **The creator must be able to view the channel by membership** (the same `canViewChannel` predicate the message routes use, WITHOUT the break-glass read grant): holding `messaging.share.manage` alone never reaches a conversation, and a non-member gets the same 404 as for an unknown channel. The check is in the service and, for the channel kind, repeated at every partner read (a channel cannot change kind, but the read is self-contained).
2. **D2: live, but only what was posted after the share, minus clinical content.** The partner sees messages in that channel with `created_at >= shared_from` (the database clock at creation, frozen by a guard trigger) that are: not deleted, not redacted (an approved redaction, ADR-0055), `body_kind` plain/markdown/rich (never `system`), and carry NO attachment, NO clinical link, NO structured entity reference (a patient or staff mention), no chart-promotion request, no acknowledgment request, and are not urgent or addressed to one recipient (`urgency <> 'routine'` or an `urgent_recipient_user_id`). A reply or quote is shown only when the message it points at is itself visible. The history before the share is never visible. *Why live and not a snapshot:* a snapshot freezes the first PHI-bearing answer and forces a second copy of message bodies outside the cipher; live-from-now keeps one encrypted store, one redaction path, one retention path, and makes revoke and expiry true cut-offs. *Why "after the share":* the people who wrote earlier messages did not know a partner would read them. *Why the exclusions:* attachments and clinical links are the highest-PHI content and the least reviewable, and a patient mention is how a message becomes patient-specific. "Explicitly marked shareable" content (a per-message opt-in) is deferred: nothing in this slice lets an attachment or clinical content reach a partner (open item 3). The exclusions are by structure, never by reading text, so a patient named in free text is NOT detected (known limit a).
3. **D3: expiry.** The share carries `expires_at`, default 7 days, at most 30 (`CHECK expires_at <= shared_from + 30 days` and `> shared_from`). A share cannot be extended: expiry is frozen; to continue, create a new share (a new `shared_from`, so the partner does not see the gap as continuous history either). A partner session never outlives its share.
4. **D4: revocation is instant because nothing is cached.** Every partner request resolves the session and the share in ONE SQL statement on the database clock: `share.revoked_at IS NULL AND share.expires_at > now() AND session.expires_at > now()`. No in-process or Valkey grant cache, no JWT, no "check at login". Revoking sets `revoked_at` (guard: once, by the acting user, forward only); the next partner request returns the uniform 404. Revoke never depends on the tenant flag and needs only the manage permission plus a fresh step-up.
5. **D5: partner identity (M31/M29-style, no user account).** A *partner access principal*, deliberately not a Better Auth session and not a `user`:
   - **Invitation (stage 0).** Creating a share returns, once, a 256-bit one-time invite token (`base64url`, 43 chars) and an 8-digit verification code. Only `sha256(token)` and an scrypt hash of the code are stored. The token is bound to its organisation in the redeem body (`organizationId` + `token`, the auditor-redeem shape) so the lookup runs under that tenant's RLS: a token presented against another tenant matches nothing.
   - **Enrolment stage.** Redeeming the token (single use, atomic `invite_redeemed_at` update) mints a partner session token (256-bit, stored as `sha256`, bearer in the `Authorization` header, so it is not ambient and needs no CSRF defence) in stage `enrolment`. That session reaches only `POST /partner/verify` and `GET /partner/shares/:id` (state). Everything else answers the uniform 404.
   - **Second factor, then active.** `POST /partner/verify` takes the 8-digit code, which the staff member who created the share must give the partner through a DIFFERENT channel than the link (a phone call). Five wrong attempts lock the share (`code_attempts`, a CHECK): only a staff REISSUE (D14) resets the counter, with a new invitation and a NEW code. On success the session moves to `active` (guard: forward only) AND its bearer is replaced by a fresh one (D13), and only then can it read.
   - *Departure from the auditor pattern, stated plainly:* auditors prove TOTP because they are `user` rows with an authenticator; a partner has none, so the second factor is the out-of-band code. It is weaker than TOTP (a code spoken once, valid for the share's life). TOTP/passkey for partners is an open item (4).
6. **D6: what a partner can post — nothing in this slice.** `access` is a column fixed to `'read'` by CHECK. A reply would need a message author kind other than `user`/`system`, a send path that bypasses channel membership, and clinical-lead review of what a pharmacy may write into the record; it is deferred (open item 2). A read-only partner also cannot mark anything read, react, or learn who else is a member.
7. **D7: every partner read is audited, fail closed.** Each successful read inserts one `message_external_share_read` row (share, session, count of messages returned) in the same transaction, before any byte is returned; if the insert fails the caller gets no messages. Both tables, plus the share, carry the 1107-style PHI audit triggers (the share audit row omits the invite and code hashes and the partner free text). Share creation and revocation also publish trail-only outbox events (`m36.external_share.created|revoked`, ids and instants only).
8. **D8: legal hold, redaction, retention.** **A message under an ACTIVE legal hold is NOT shown to a partner** (judged on the hold's live criteria in the same statement as the read, as the ADR-0089 export does, consistent with ADR-0095); releasing the hold shows it again. The share itself does not alter retention. **ANY `message_redaction` row hides the message from the partner, requested, pending or rejected as well as approved** (staff readers see a body only after approval; a partner gets the stricter rule because a request means someone already doubted the content). Retention destruction of a message removes it from the partner view; the destruction role may delete share rows (tenant offboarding), but `audit_event` rows survive.
9. **D9: no PHI out of band.** M36 sends the partner nothing. The invitation link and the code are shown once to the creator, who delivers them; the admin screen says link and code must travel separately. `partnerNotificationText()` is the single fixed string any future sender may use ("You have a new secure message. Sign in with your link to read it.") and takes no argument, so it cannot carry a name, a channel, a patient or a body. No email or SMS binding is built or wired (open item 5).
10. **D10: limits.** Redeem is budgeted per client address before any database work; **there is deliberately no per-organisation budget** (unauthenticated junk naming a tenant could otherwise exhaust it for real partners; the invitation is a 256-bit secret, so a distributed guess is not the threat). Verify has ONLY a per-client-address limiter plus the database cap of 5 attempts per share, which is the real control. The partner state and messages routes have a per-address budget (300 per minute) so a wrong bearer cannot be replayed for free. Reads are limited per share (60 per minute) and capped at 50 messages per page. The code has 5 attempts per share. A tenant may hold at most 10 live shares at once, and a creator at most 3 per rolling day (anti-sprawl).
11. **D11: flag and permission.** Tenant flag `messaging.external_partners` (absent = off). Off: creating a share is the uniform 404 and EVERY partner route is the uniform 404, so turning the flag off cuts all partner access at once, without touching rows. Listing and revoking shares need only the permission, never the flag (a share that exists stays revocable). New permission `messaging.share.manage`, granted by the migration to `compliance_officer`, `administrator`, `org_admin` only (existing tenants) and by the compliance_officer template (new tenants); no clinical, aide or other role holds it. A fresh step-up is required to create and to revoke (step-up key = permission key). Creation also requires a BAA reference (1 to 120 characters, a contract id or the like) the creator attests exists; the platform cannot verify it (open item 1).
12. **D12: uniform refusals.** The bearer is resolved BEFORE the verify body or the messages query is validated, so an unauthenticated caller never receives a 422 with detail; bad input after a live bearer is a 422. Unknown share, other tenant, other conversation, expired, revoked, flag off, wrong stage, bad token: the same 404 body. Distinguishing "expired" from "unknown" would be an oracle for share ids and a hint to the attacker; the partner view learns "access ended" from the 404 on the state route alone.
13. **D13: session life and rotation.** A partner session dies, with the uniform 404, after **30 idle minutes** (`EXTERNAL_SESSION_IDLE_MINUTES`; `last_seen_at`, touched by the very statement that resolves the bearer, on the database clock, no cache) or **12 hours in total** (`EXTERNAL_SESSION_MAX_HOURS`; `expires_at`, CHECKed `<= created_at + 12 h` and never past the share), whichever comes first. Past either, the partner redeems a NEW invitation, which staff reissue (D14); the old bearer cannot be revived. The idle bound is also in the read-trail guard. **Activation rotates the bearer:** `verify` replaces the enrolment token hash in the same update that moves the stage to `active` (the guard allows a `token_hash` change only together with that transition) and returns the new token once, so an enrolment token captured earlier is useless afterwards. A touch of `last_seen_at` alone writes no audit row (the audit function skips it). The names and numbers are constants in `packages/types` and pinned against the SQL by a test.
14. **D14: reissue.** `POST /external-shares/:id/reissue` (permission `messaging.share.manage`, a fresh step-up, the tenant flag, an outbox) replaces the invitation and the verification code of a LIVE share: it bumps `invite_generation` (every session of an older generation is dead in the same statement that checks liveness, with no session row touched), clears `invite_redeemed_at`, restarts `code_attempts` at zero and returns the new token and a NEW 8-digit code once. The guard trigger makes this the ONLY path that resets the counter or clears the redemption: it demands the acting user as `reissued_by`, new secrets for both, one generation at a time, a live share, and a zero start, and any other update of those columns is refused. Audited by the share's audit trigger (generation and actor, never a hash or a code) and a trail-only `m36.external_share.reissued` event (ids only). Expiry is NOT extended by a reissue.
15. **D15: minimum necessary.** Creating a share requires `acknowledgedMinimumNecessary: true` in the API (422 otherwise) and the staff dialog's checkbox: the creator confirms that an outside party will read the conversation and that patient names in free text are NOT filtered. It is stored on the share (`minimum_necessary_acknowledged`, CHECKed true, frozen) and audited with the insert.
16. **D16: free-text identifiers are withheld, names are not detected.** Names cannot be filtered reliably and this ADR does not pretend to. As defence in depth, the plaintext the partner reader already decrypts is run through a pure matcher (`packages/types/src/external-share-identifiers.ts`, fixture-tested with positive and negative tables including clinical shorthand) and a message that matches is **withheld, never masked**: SSN-like numbers, any run of 6 or more digits (record, account, member numbers, phones without separators), the labels MRN / DOB / DOS / SSN / account / policy / member / patient id, numeric, ISO and named dates (dates of birth), US phone numbers, email addresses and street addresses (street, avenue, road, boulevard, drive, lane, highway, terrace). **Omission, not a placeholder:** a placeholder row would confirm that a sensitive-looking message exists and force cursor handling; the cost is that a reply can appear without its parent, which the partner cannot see and we accept. The matcher runs on the server on the already-decrypted body, never logs it, and the read-trail row records only how many messages were withheld (`withheld_count`). **This reduces, and does not eliminate, the exposure.** NOT detected: names and nicknames, relationships, diagnoses and drugs tied to a person, indirect identifiers ("the lady in room 214 whose daughter lives in Boise"), seven-digit local numbers, two-part dates (10/23), five-digit numbers (rooms, lots, zip codes), and the short street suffixes st/dr/ct/way. A message it misses is shown as typed.
17. **D17: trusted proxy, fail closed.** The per-address budgets key on `request.ip`, which Fastify takes from the socket peer unless the peer is in `TRUSTED_PROXY_CIDRS`. In production with that variable unset or empty every partner would share the proxy's address, so at the composition root (`external-share-proxy.ts`) the **partner routes are not mounted** (they answer the framework 404), one boot warning is logged, and staff **create and reissue answer `PARTNER_ACCESS_UNAVAILABLE` (HTTP 503)** with the message "partner access needs trusted proxy configuration", so a share nobody can use is never made. List and revoke still work. Outside production the socket peer is the client and the feature is available. Verified by inject: from an untrusted peer a spoofed `X-Forwarded-For` does not move the key; from a trusted peer the forwarded address does (and an extra client-supplied hop does not win). Documented in `deploy/production/TENANT_EDGE.md`.
18. **D18: members see it.** The channel detail a member already reads (`GET /channels/:id`, the same route and predicate; a non-member never reaches it) carries `externalShare: {active: true, partnerKind, expiresAt}`, computed live from the share table on every read (revoked or expired: absent; two active shares: the one that ends last). The web thread shows a persistent status line, "An outside partner can read new messages in this conversation until <date>.", re-read every minute. **The banner follows the flag exactly as partner reads do:** with `messaging.external_partners` off every partner route is already the uniform 404, so no partner can read and the banner is absent too; turning the flag back on restores both. No system message is written into the channel (the module has no cheap writer).

## Threat model

| Threat | Control |
|---|---|
| IDOR across shares (guess another share id) | Partner routes take the share id in the URL only to compare it with the session's own share; mismatch is the uniform 404. There is no list-shares route for a partner. |
| IDOR across tenants | The org comes from the redeem body for the invite and from the token prefix for the session; every query is under `withOrgContext` (FORCE RLS); the composite FKs make a cross-tenant link unrepresentable. |
| Enumeration of share ids / tokens | Ids and tokens are random (122 and 256 bits); refusals are uniform; redeem/verify are rate limited per IP and org; code attempts are capped per share. |
| Privilege creep into other conversations | The reader's only channel is `share.channel_id` read from the row in the same statement as the session; no channel id ever comes from a request; no `user` row exists, so no staff permission or ABAC predicate can match. |
| Expiry bypass | Expiry is judged by the database clock in the same statement as the read; no cache; sessions are bounded by the share. |
| Stolen session token | 256-bit bearer, hashed at rest, bound to a stage and a share; dies with the share; revocation is instant. A stolen token before the code step reaches nothing, and it is replaced when the code is proven (D13). Dead after 30 idle minutes or 12 hours (D13). Not device-bound (known limit c). |
| Brute-forcing the 8-digit code | 5 attempts per share in a CHECK-bounded counter, never reset; scrypt-hashed. |
| Replay of the invitation | Single use (atomic update); the token is useless after redemption. |
| Content in notifications | M36 sends none; the fixed text takes no input; events carry ids only. |
| Staff unaware a partner reads their channel | Share creation is audited and announced on a trail event; members see a live banner on the channel (D18), and the creator confirmed the minimum-necessary statement (D15). |
| A compromised administrator shares widely | Permission limited to three roles, step-up, caps (10 live per tenant, 3 per creator-day), BAA reference, audit on every create, revoke and read. |

## Known limits

- (a) Patient names in free text are not detected; the exclusions are structural. Content the sender types about a patient is visible to the partner. The share is for non-clinical coordination conversations; the admin screen says so, and clinical-lead review is open.
- (b) The second factor is a spoken code, not a device-bound authenticator.
- (c) Session tokens are not device- or IP-bound.
- (d) A partner sees author display names of staff who posted after the share.
- (e) Edits after the share are visible as edited text; the pre-edit revision is never shown (revisions are not read).
- (f) The 7/30-day expiry, the 10/3 caps and the 60-per-minute read limit are defaults, not agreed values.
- (g) The BAA reference is an attestation, not a verified link to a contract record.
- (h) The per-address budgets mean something only when `TRUSTED_PROXY_CIDRS` is right. In production without it the feature is OFF (D17); set too broadly (a hop the attacker controls), a spoofed `X-Forwarded-For` lets an attacker choose their own bucket. Only the real proxy hops belong in the list.
- (i) Session life is bounded (D13) but not bound to a device or address: a stolen active token works for up to 30 idle minutes or 12 hours from anywhere, and reissue is the only recovery.
- (j) Free text is only partly filtered (D16): names, indirect identifiers and anything the matcher does not list reach the partner as typed. The staff acknowledgement (D15) exists because of this limit, not instead of it. The matcher also withholds some harmless messages (any six-digit number, a numeric date).
- (k) The invitation can be consumed by a link scanner or prefetcher before the partner opens it (single use); the creator then revokes and creates a new share. The partner still needs the out-of-band code.
- (l) `code_hash` protects an 8-digit number: a leaked `message_external_share` row is brute-forceable offline (about 10^8 guesses). The 5-attempt cap does not help against a stolen row.
- (m) Sharing a discipline, location or on-call channel is NOT 42 CFR Part 2 gated: only `care_team` and `bereavement` are excluded, and patient names in free text are not detected, so a Part 2 discussion in an ordinary channel could reach a partner.
- (n) Lockout leaves a durable trail: every wrong attempt (and the fifth, `code_attempts = 5`) is an `audit_event` row written by the share's audit trigger, without the code or its hash; no separate event row is written.
- (o) The member banner is read from the channel detail, not pushed: it can lag a revoke by up to a minute on an open thread, and it is absent whenever the flag is off. Members who never open the channel are not told, and no message about it is written into the channel.
- (p) Withheld messages are omitted, so a partner can see a reply without the message it answers (D16).
- (q) A reissue does not extend the share: the expiry stays frozen, so the partner's total access never exceeds the original window.

## Open human items

1. **Legal/BAA:** is a free-text reference enough, or must a partner directory with a stored BAA exist first? Which partner kinds (pharmacy, DME, other) may be shared at all?
2. **Replies:** may a partner post? If so, as what author kind, into what, and with whose review?
3. **Shareable marking:** may attachments or clinical links ever be shared, and who marks them?
4. **Partner authentication strength:** is the out-of-band code acceptable, or must partners enrol TOTP/passkey?
5. **Notification delivery:** who sends the invitation and the "you have a message" text, and through which vendor under which BAA?
6. **Member awareness:** the live banner is built (D18); is it enough, or must a system message also be written into the channel when a share starts and ends?
7. Compliance and clinical-lead concurrence on D1 to D12.

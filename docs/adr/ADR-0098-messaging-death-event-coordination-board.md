# ADR-0098 — Messaging death-event coordination board: a fixed checklist after a patient's death, one accountable owner per item, an audit trail, coordination only

- **Status:** **Proposed** (2026-10-23). Built behind the tenant flag `messaging.death_board` (default off). Decisions D1 to D13 (D13 is the wave 4 stall sweep) are the build team's defaults. The clinical lead, the bereavement coordinator and compliance have not signed off on the template, the due offsets, the owner rule or the wording; see Open sign-offs.
- **Date:** 2026-10-23
- **Phase:** Messaging Hub Phase 6, WP 6.9 (building on WP 3.8).
- **Related:** [ADR-0065](ADR-0065-messaging-lifecycle-review-queue.md) (the lifecycle review queue and the two death events this reads), [ADR-0061](ADR-0061-messaging-communication-tasks.md) (tasks: the ownership semantics mirrored here), [ADR-0062](ADR-0062-messaging-clinical-handoffs.md) (per-item accountability and the database guard pattern), [ADR-0084](ADR-0084-messaging-workforce-offboarding.md) (WP 5.9 offboarding, reused for a leaver), [ADR-0073](ADR-0073-messaging-notification-content-policy.md) (no free-text PHI in notifications), [ADR-0010](ADR-0010-tenancy-rls-invariants.md).

## Context

WP 6.9: "Checklist thread for pronouncement and follow-up tasks, building on the lifecycle review queue (WP 3.8). Acceptance: each checklist item has one accountable owner and an audit trail." After a death the follow-up work (pronouncement, telling the family, equipment pickup, medication disposal, record and document tasks) is spread across people and modules, and today nothing in M36 shows who owns which step. WP 3.8 already reacts to the same event, but only to queue the dead patient's open messaging tasks for a supervisor; it creates no work.

## Decision (proposed)

1. **D1: a new, thin item table, not M36 communication tasks.** Three tables (migration 1201): `death_board` (one row per organisation and patient), `death_board_item` (one row per board and template key) and the append-only `death_board_item_event`. Tasks were considered first and rejected for four concrete reasons: (a) a task is anchored to a message (`message_id` NOT NULL with a composite FK) and a checklist item has no message; inventing one would plant a message in a patient's thread whose text is the item wording; (b) a task lives in a channel and a task create on an archived channel is `410 CHANNEL_ARCHIVED` since WP 3.8, and the care-team channel is archived by the death (GAP-104); (c) the bereavement channel's subject is "a bereavement-plan reference, not reliably the patient", and the task rule derives its patient from the channel subject, so a task there could be about the wrong patient or none; (d) the task rules (`patient_is_clinical`, the active-per-assignee index, close reasons, accept step) describe a conversation follow-up, not a checklist. What is reused is the **semantics**, copied deliberately: an owner column that is never empty, a `needs_owner` flag when the owner leaves and nobody can take the item (visible and alerted, as `communication_task.needs_owner`), a system actor on offboarding moves, the successor-then-flag sweep, and an append-only event table beside an audit trigger. WP 3.8's review queue is unchanged and still handles the dead patient's old tasks.
2. **D2: trigger.** A new consumer `m36.death-board` reads `m6.admission.discharged` and `m5.patient.deceased` and resolves the patient with the **same function** the lifecycle review consumer uses (`resolve` in `patient-lifecycle-review.ts`, now exported), so the two can never disagree about who died. Only a `death` cause opens a board; a transfer or an ordinary discharge opens nothing. It is bound at boot beside the review consumer (`apps/backend/src/index.ts`, grep `buildM36DeathBoardConsumer`): bound is not computing, so the live suite feeds the real consumer both events and asserts the rows. `declaredConsumers` is unchanged (`m36` already consumes both events). A malformed payload, an unresolved admission or a patient nobody can own throws (relay retry, then DLQ), never a silently unboarded death.
3. **D3: the template is a platform floor, version 1, fixed in `@chartwarden/types`.** Six plain items: pronouncement of death recorded (due 2 h), family or representative notified (4 h), medical equipment pickup arranged (3 d), medication disposal completed and witnessed (24 h), clinical record follow-up tasks completed (3 d, the M4 side) and required documents filed (3 d, the M22 side). The M4 and M22 items are checkboxes a person ticks: **no cross-module write and no cross-module read** (the board does not check that the record task is really done). Due times are fixed offsets from creation on the database clock. **Tenant additions are out of scope** (a tenant cannot add, remove or reword items): the wording is reviewed text that must never carry a name, and an editable template would be a free-text PHI store. The item key set is a database CHECK, so a template change is a migration and a version bump.
4. **D4: exactly one owner per item, and the creation-time rule.** `owner_user_id` is NOT NULL. At creation every item goes to the first eligible person of, in order: the patient's primary RN, the live care team (by user id), the people who last sat on the patient's care-team channel (soft-removed rows included, because M5 may already have ended the assignments when the event is handled). Eligible means an active messaging user whom the 42 CFR Part 2 gate admits for this patient. If nobody is eligible no board is written, supervisors get the "no owner" alert and the consumer throws: there is no state in which an open item has no owner. One person owning all six items at the start is a default, not a plan; the owner or a supervisor spreads them (D5). An item is born open, unflagged and unfinished (database trigger).
5. **D5: reassignment is audited and bounded.** The item's owner or a `messaging.review` holder (the existing supervisor key; no new permission) can reassign an open item to an **active** user the Part 2 gate admits for the patient (422 `DEATH_BOARD_OWNER_INVALID` otherwise). Each move writes an event row (actor, from, to, reason `manual` or `supervisor`) in the same transaction, and the audit trigger records the actor. Repeating a reassignment to the current owner is a no-op, EXCEPT on a flagged (`needs_owner`) item, where naming the current owner is refused with the same 422 (it would not be a move, and the database guard would otherwise raise a 500). A supervisor who is not the item's owner cannot name themselves as the new owner (same uniform 422; mirrors the S-10 self-approval control, so a supervisor cannot assign an item to themselves and then complete it); the owner naming themselves stays a no-op. A reassignment always lands on a person and clears `needs_owner` (database guard). **Offboarding (WP 5.9):** when a leaver is revoked or deactivated, `m2.membership.revoked` / `m2.user.deactivated` (the access-revocation consumer's `reassignOwnedItems`) and the manual offboarding run (chained into the task step) call `reassignForLeaver`: each open item of the leaver moves to the next eligible person by the D4 order (the leaver excluded), recorded as a system event with reason `offboarding`; when nobody is eligible the item keeps the leaver as owner, is flagged `needs_owner`, an event is written and supervisors are alerted. A flagged item cannot be completed until a supervisor reassigns it. This runs whether or not the flag is on (a security control), and each item is isolated so one failure does not strand the rest (the first failure is rethrown after the sweep so the dispatcher retries).
6. **D6: completion is explicit, the owner's, idempotent, on the database clock.** Only the current owner completes an item (403 `DEATH_BOARD_NOT_OWNER` for anyone else who can see it); the database guard enforces it (`completed_by_user_id` must equal `owner_user_id`, the owner cannot change in the same update, a flagged item cannot be completed). `completed_at` is set by the guard trigger from `now()`, whatever the client sends. A done item is final. The same owner completing again gets the same item and writes nothing. There is no "complete on behalf of": a supervisor reassigns to themselves first, which is visible in the history.
7. **D7: visibility is a membership predicate with a uniform 404.** A viewer sees a board only if the 42 CFR Part 2 gate admits them for the patient (**fail closed**: a throw is "no", for supervisors too) **and** they are a supervisor (`messaging.review`), or are the **current** owner of an item of that board, or are on the patient's live care team. Everyone else, a missing id, a malformed id, another tenant and a flag-off tenant get the same 404 body. A past owner (reassigned away, or an actor in the history only) has no standing and gets the uniform 404: involvement is current ownership, never history. The routes also require `messaging.read` and `patient.read`. Reads are audited against the disclosed patient (one event per distinct patient). The board never joins the bereavement channel's membership, because that channel's subject is not reliably the patient.
8. **D8: notifications carry no PHI (ADR-0073).** Two in-app alert types (`m36.death_board.assigned` to the new owner, `m36.death_board.needs_owner` to supervisors) with fixed text: no patient, name, item wording, cause or date in the text, the payload or the correlation id (a hash). The deep link is the screen path. Alerts are inbox entries; they page nobody and send no push or email. Redelivery is absorbed by the correlation id.
9. **D9: idempotency.** `death_board_one_per_patient` (unique on organisation and patient) and `death_board_item_one_per_key` make the two death events, a redelivery and a concurrent pair converge on one board of six items; the second writes nothing. A replayed consumer re-sends only deduplicated alerts, which heals a crash between commit and alert.
10. **D10: nothing is deleted or archived by the board.** It does not touch a channel, a membership, a message or a task. The care-team channel archive (GAP-104) and the WP 3.8 review rows are as before; the bereavement channel is kept (its retention class and `archives_at` are untouched); the live suite compares row counts and the bereavement channel's `is_archived` before and after. `chartwarden_app` has no DELETE on the three tables; `chartwarden_destruction` has it for tenant destruction only.
11. **D11: coordination only; no clinical documentation.** The board writes nothing to M4, M5, M12, M22 or any chart and reads nothing from them beyond the patient label. Completing "medication disposal" does not record a disposal; it records that a named person says the work is done. The chart remains the record of fact.
12. **D12: flag, permission, events, audit.** The tenant flag `messaging.death_board` (the platform flag reader, tenant row wins, absent = off, an unreadable flag reads as off, cached a few seconds) is checked **first** in every handler, before any id, query or body is parsed: off answers the uniform 404, and the list answers `200 {enabled:false,boards:[]}` so the web can say "not turned on". The consumer opens no board while the flag is off (no backfill; see limits). No new permission and no outbox event in this version. Audit: a bespoke PHI trigger on `death_board` and `death_board_item` scrubs `patient_id`; the event table (ids, enums, instants) uses the generic trigger.

13. **D13 (wave 4, migration 1202): a periodic stall sweep for owners who lose eligibility outside offboarding.** An open item whose owner is no longer an active messaging user, or is no longer admitted by the 42 CFR Part 2 gate for the patient, previously stalled silently (limit k). `messaging.death_board_stall_sweep` (hourly at :50, one repeatable BullMQ tick fanning out over active orgs exactly like the break-glass and handoff-expiry sweeps; flag-independent, because a board that exists must not stall on a flag) calls the org-explicit `flagStalledForOrg(orgId)` (a tick is not a request: it runs under `withOrgContext(systemOrgContext(orgId))`). Per organisation it reads open, unflagged items (keyset on id, `STALL_SCAN_PAGE` 500 per page, at most `STALL_SCAN_MAX` 2000 per tick), groups by patient, asks the active-user seam and the Part 2 gate once per patient and owner set, and flags at most `STALL_FLAG_MAX` 200 items per tick. A flagged item is `needs_owner` with **the owner column unchanged** (the existing guard semantics: the flag clears only by a reassignment onto a person; the Part 2-refused owner reaches nothing, the uniform 404, and a flagged owner cannot complete), plus a system `needs_owner` event with the new reason `owner_ineligible` (migration 1202 widens the reason CHECK), and the existing fixed-text supervisor alert (`m36.death_board.needs_owner`, "A follow-up checklist item has no available owner. A supervisor must assign it."): no patient, item or name wording. **Idempotent:** a flagged item leaves the scan, the flag is a locked, guarded transition (`FOR UPDATE`, re-checked open, unflagged and same owner), and the alert is deduplicated on a hash of the item id and version. **The alert is sent before the flag**, so a failed alert leaves the item unflagged and the next tick retries; the cost is a rare early alert if the item moved between the scan and the lock. **An outage is not a verdict:** an active-user check or Part 2 gate that throws leaves that owner unflagged this tick (the viewer-side gate still fails closed). Logs carry organisation and item ids and the error class only. A failure on any item is counted, the sweep finishes the rest, then throws so the tick fails visibly (BullMQ retry, DLQ alert). "Eligibility" is the creation and reassignment rule (active user and Part 2 admission); **leaving the care team alone does not stall an item**, because the reassign guard has never required care-team membership of an owner.

## Hazards

- **No board for a death while the flag was off.** The consumer does nothing then, and a later switch-on does not backfill. A tenant must switch the flag on before the first death it wants coordinated: there are **no retroactive boards**, operators must enable the flag before it is needed, and a later backfill of deaths recorded while the flag was off is an **open item** (not built).
- **Wrong or stale owner.** The primary RN may be the wrong person for "medication disposal" or "equipment pickup". The cost of a wrong default is one visible reassignment; the cost of a smart default that is wrong silently is worse, so the default is one rule.
- **A checkbox is not a check.** An item ticked "record tasks completed" proves nothing about the chart. The board must not be cited as evidence that a task was done.
- **Part 2.** A supervisor the gate refuses sees nothing; a patient whose flag cannot be read is invisible to everyone until it can. An owner who loses their release after assignment keeps a row they can no longer open (404) and cannot complete; a supervisor must reassign.
- **Offboarded owner with no successor** leaves a flagged item owned by a person who has left. It is loud (alert, state word "No owner", completion refused) but not self-healing.

## Known limits

- (a) Every item starts with the same owner (D4). (b) A new owner is entered by user id on the screen (no picker); the history route has no screen. (c) The list examines the 100 most recent boards (`DEATH_BOARD_SCAN_MAX`), in three queries plus one Part 2 verdict and one care-team read per distinct patient, then shows up to the limit (default 25, max 50). A board older than the newest 100 is invisible in the list to non-supervisors and reachable only by id; the routes carry a per-user / per-org rate limit (30 / 300 per minute, fail-open). (d) The care-team test is the live roster, so after M5 ends the assignments a former team member who never owned anything loses sight of the board (a supervisor and the owners keep it). (e) Due times are display only: no reminder, no escalation, no page when overdue (Phase 4 owns those). (f) No outbox event, so M33 analytics cannot see the board. (g) Manual offboarding runs the board sweep inside the task step and reports no separate count. (h) A revoked owner's already-fetched screen can still show the item until it refreshes. (i) The template is not tenant-configurable (D3). (k) **Superseded by D13 (wave 4):** a stalled item is flagged and supervisors are told within about an hour; remaining limits of that sweep: it runs hourly (up to an hour of silence), looks at the 2000 lowest-id open unflagged items per organisation per tick and flags 200 per tick (a larger backlog is cleared over several ticks, and items beyond the scan cap would wait until earlier ones are completed or flagged; a truncated scan logs a warning), it flags only on a positive "no" from the seams, and an owner who regains eligibility keeps the flag until a supervisor reassigns (the flag clears only by a reassignment). (l) Part 2 consent evaluations for death-board reads and owner checks are logged under their own caller route `death_board visibility/ownership`, not the care-team channel label. (j) A death recorded without a resolvable owner needs the DLQ to be watched.
- (d) Stall sweep ceiling: each tick scans open unflagged items from the LOWEST id upward and stops at `STALL_SCAN_MAX` (2000) per organization. With more than 2000 open unflagged items, the later ones are never examined (the truncation warning fires only when a further row really exists). A rotating cursor is a follow-up, not built.

## Wave 4 additions

Migration `1202_m36_ack_broadcast_withdraw_deadline_death_board_stall.sql` (shared with ADR-0097 D13, D14) widens `death_board_item_event_reason_shape` with `owner_ineligible` (also in `DEATH_BOARD_REASON_CODES`, the Drizzle CHECK and the typed route schema). New: `flagStalledForOrg`, the store's `openUnflaggedItems`, `death-board-stall-worker.ts`, queue `messaging.death_board_stall_sweep` with its parameters and `deathBoardStallSweep` schedule in `@chartwarden/messaging`, boot registration beside the break-glass sweep (needs `DATABASE_PLATFORM_URL`; without it the sweep is off and boot logs a warning), unit, tick and live tests (`test:m36:death-board:live`, section 5).

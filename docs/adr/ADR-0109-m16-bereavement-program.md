# ADR-0109 — M16 bereavement program: a universal 13-month floor for every risk tier, assessment tasks outside the outreach ledger, an internal-only physician letter, and program activities (mailings, groups, memorials, library) inside M16

- **Status:** **Proposed** (2026-11-05). The product decisions below were approved by the product owner on 2026-11-05 and are final; this ADR stays Proposed until the first PR of the work is reviewed.
- **Date:** 2026-11-05
- **Phase:** post-launch bereavement depth (extends the built M16 cadence, risk scorer and closure; adds the program-level activities).
- **Related:** [ADR-0005](ADR-0005-manifest-single-source-of-truth.md) (manifest is the source of truth; seams and outbox add no `dependsOn` edge, D1), the Module 16 spec (as-built addendum, section 15), the Module 36 alert catalog (`PLATFORM_DEFAULT_ALERT_RULES`), the Module 22 render-request contract, the M16 bereavement program blueprint of 2026-11-05 (the full table, route and screen inventory; it lives with the PR description, not in the committed corpus).
- **Amends:** the Module 16 spec cadence floor (D4 and section 7.4): the spec's floor is tier-shaped and its low tier is five touchpoints. This ADR wins on conflict until the spec is re-locked; the spec carries an as-built addendum (section 15) instead of a version bump.

## Context

Facts read from the repo on 2026-11-05:

- The M16 schedule is tier-shaped (`packages/bereavement/src/cadence/schedule-floor.ts`): the low tier is a condolence letter, 1-, 3- and 6-month calls and the anniversary letter. The owner wants the full Medicare-aligned bereavement timeline to reach every family; a floor that depends on a rubric score would let a mis-scored family lose required contact.
- Risk is scored by a deterministic 8-factor rubric owned by M16. The scorer is ours; it is not a licensed third-party instrument and must not be presented as one.
- `buildDrizzleM16WriteService` and `buildM16WorkerDeps` are composed in `apps/backend/src/index.ts` without an `alerts` port, so every M16 alert effect is skipped silently. M16's `AlertPort.emit({kind,payload})` does not match M36's `AlertEmitter.emit(orgId,{eventType,payload,severity,correlationId})`, and no `m16.*` rule exists in `PLATFORM_DEFAULT_ALERT_RULES`.
- `BEREAVEMENT_OVERSIGHT_ROLES` names `bereavement_program_coordinator`; migration 1234, `packages/idg/src/policy.ts`, the web discipline map and `DISCIPLINE_FOR_ROLE_KEY` name `bereavement_coordinator`. No role template or seed materializes either key (tenants create roles); the only database fact is migration 1234, which grants to `bereavement_coordinator`.
- `bereavement_outreach` rows do not persist the cadence mark the spec (section 7.4) names, so a schedule cannot be topped up idempotently; a tier change never regenerates the schedule; the family closeout letter (`CLOSEOUT_CADENCE_ITEM`) is spec-only.

## Decisions

**D1 - Everything stays inside M16.** No new module. New tables (mailings, support groups, memorial services, library metadata, assessment tasks, physician letter) are M16-owned PHI tables written by M16. Cross-module reach is through seams and the outbox only, so no `dependsOn` edge is added (ADR-0005 D1). Rejected: a separate "bereavement program" module (every table would still join M16's plan and contact rows).

**D2 - The universal floor.** The Medicare-aligned timeline is the floor for every tier; tiers only add. (`condolence_letter` moves from d7 to d2, `one_month` d30 becomes `initial_outreach` d28, and the 9-month call, the anniversary call and the closeout letter join the low tier.) Days are counted from the UTC-midnight death date. Low: `condolence_letter` d2 (sympathy letter), `initial_outreach` d28, `three_month` d90, `six_month` d180, `nine_month` d270 (mark `nine_month_high_risk_only`, relabeled "Month 9", legacy name kept), `anniversary` d365 (letter, "card"), `anniversary_call` d365, `closeout_letter` d390 (separable from the anniversary pair). Moderate adds `week_2` d14; high adds `week_1` d7 and `two_month` d60; complicated adds `immediate_screen` d2 and turns `week_1` into an in-person visit. Counts are 8 / 9 / 11 / 12. `one_month` is a deprecated alias that sits in no floor (kept so old rows and consumers still resolve it). Rejected: a tier-shaped floor (a family scored low by the rubric would lose Medicare-timeline contact).

**D3 - Assessment checkpoints are tasks, not outreach rows.** The initial assessment (d28) and the midpoint reassessment (d180) are `bereavement_assessment_task` rows. An outreach row would count in the M30 CoP numerator for a contact nobody made. The cadence module exposes the task dates as pure data (`assessment-floor`); the table, the hourly miss-deadline pass and the `risk_assessment_overdue` alert follow in a later change.

**D4 - A tier change tops the schedule up, forward only.** `planScheduleTopUp(deathDate, contact, existingMarks, fromIso)` is pure: it returns the floor items for the new tier that have no row yet and are still in the future, and it never cancels or moves an existing row. The hook in `appendScore` runs it on an active plan whose `ruleTier` differs from the prior tier; the regenerate-schedule route uses marks the same way. Each outreach row persists its mark (`bereavement_outreach.cadence_mark`); a row from before that column is read through `inferLegacyMark` (a pure heuristic over kind and due offset). In-flight plans are not rewritten: the new floor reaches them only through regenerate, resume, rescind or a tier change. Rejected: a backfill migration that rewrites in-flight schedules (it would change dates a counselor has already planned around).

**D5 - Our own 8-factor instrument, never named after a third-party instrument.** No user-facing label, string, document or API field names a licensed bereavement-risk instrument. A test greps the sources for it. Pre-death, an MSW, chaplain or bereavement counselor on the care team (or oversight) may record a draft of the eight factors (tri-state, default unknown, no free text) per admission; it is writable only while no plan exists. The substance-use factor is forced `unknown` unless the existing bereavement disclosure gate clears it. At the initial score the draft is carried in with a merge rule where **true wins**: carried-over evidence never silently lowers a computed risk. The pending `bereavement_idg_risk_flag` is marked assessed when the initial assessment task completes.

**D6 - The physician letter goes to an internal hospice clinician only.** Owner decision: a condolence letter is created `pending_review` when the plan becomes eligible, addressed to the attending clinician recorded on the care team (`CareTeamProvider.attendingUserId`), or to an internal user the reviewer picks. No M40 community-physician lookup and no M6 seam change. A human reviews and approves (gated by `org_scope_bereavement_oversight`); approval emits `m16.bereavement.document_requested`, which M22 renders (template `bereavement_physician_condolence_letter`). A decline carries a redacted reason of at least 20 characters. The letter is due death + 2 days.

**D7 - Alerts are real, and three new kinds exist.** A real `AlertPort` adapter over the M36 `AlertEmitter` seam is passed at both composition sites (the write service and the cron workers) and at both death-intake consumers. M16 alert kinds map to an M36 event type, a severity and a PHI-free correlation id (`m16:<kind>:<subject ids>`, so a redelivery does not page twice). Routing is explicit and rule-backed: only a kind with a seeded platform rule has a route, and a kind with no route is a no-op that never reaches the emitter (M36 would persist a zero-recipient alert for it). New kinds: `death_intake` (emitted by the intake consumers when they open a plan; counselor auto-assignment does not exist, so oversight is told), `physician_letter_pending_review`, `risk_assessment_overdue`. Each gets a `PLATFORM_DEFAULT_ALERT_RULES` row with the `role_set` resolver over the bereavement oversight roles. M16's seven earlier kinds (`outreach_missed`, `contact_unreachable`, `anniversary_due_in_14_days`, ...) have never had a seeded rule and stay unrouted until a rule and a route are added together.

**D8 - One coordinator role key: `bereavement_coordinator`.** `BEREAVEMENT_OVERSIGHT_ROLES` (and the M39 mirror of it, and the tests that pin it) name `bereavement_coordinator`, the only key a migration references. A tenant that created a role under the other spelling gains nothing; no role template materializes either key.

**D9 - Discharge summary PDF for auditors (internal).** A typed payload (offered versus delivered per contact and mark, tasks done and missed, unreachable contacts, opt-out scopes, tier trajectory, closure reason; no notes, goals, factors or substance-use data) is emitted at every closure writer and rendered by M22 (`bereavement_discharge_summary_internal`); the id is stamped on `bereavement_plan_closure.discharge_summary_document_id`. The document is internal: volunteers and the portal never read it.

**D10 - Mailings (PR 2).** A quarterly agency newsletter and tenant-configured holiday mailings, with no default wording shipped (generation refuses empty content). A batch PDF first (a print vendor later); email when preferred and stored. `no_mailings` is a new opt-out kind: the contact stays `active` and calls continue (the allowed-kind set is narrowed so `resolveOutreachKind` re-modalizes to phone). It does not raise `contact_opted_out`, so M36 keeps the family channel. Activity rows (a mailing, a group session, a memorial attendance) log a contact but satisfy no cadence item and do not count in M30. Patient-linked families only.

**D11 - Support groups, memorial services, grief library (PR 3).** Recurring and drop-in groups, in person and virtual, program families only; a staff or volunteer facilitator (a volunteer must pass the 418.78 gates and may run, not manage). Memorial services for deaths within a configurable window (default 12 months) with staff and portal RSVP. The library is M16 metadata over M22 documents, portal-only. All three are specified in the blueprint and recorded in the as-built addendum as they land.

**D12 - Migrations are additive, in a stated order.** `no_mailings` (own file, an enum value), then the cadence columns (`cadence_mark`, `care_goals_cipher`, `discharge_summary_document_id`), then one expand migration per new table family; the single contracting change is relaxing `outreach_id`/`kind` NOT NULL on the contact log (PR 2). Every new table carries the three tenancy columns, RLS enabled and forced on `app.org_id`, an `audit_phi_write_<table>` trigger and FK indexes. New value sets are `text` plus CHECK (no new Postgres enums). Ordinals are re-checked against `main` and every open branch at each PR; the blueprint's 1330/1331 were taken by the tenant-onboarding branches, so the first two files are `1340` (`no_mailings`) and `1341` (cadence columns), and later M16 migrations continue from 1342.

## Deferred (not built)

The decedent-birthday mailing (M16 holds no date of birth; needs a new M5 bulk seam), a print vendor, multi-language content, household de-duplication, address validation, a batch-PDF purge job (compliance decides retention), M36 alerts for draft mailing runs, a portal "my groups" view, waitlists, video transcoding, guest names, and the `channel_specific` / `pause_temporarily` opt-out semantics (every opt-out kind currently acts as a full opt-out; ticketed).

## Consequences

- A low-risk family now receives eight touchpoints instead of a tier-shaped subset; counselor load rises accordingly. The tiers still add contact, they no longer gate the Medicare timeline.
- A tier change adds work for the future and never removes it. A family that moves from high to low keeps the extra rows it was already given.
- Binding the adapter changes nothing for M16's earlier alert kinds (they have no rule, so they stay unrouted); only the three new kinds deliver. Giving the earlier kinds a rule and a route is a separate, product-owned decision about who is paged.
- The cadence floor, the top-up planner and the alert mapping are pure and unit-tested; the DB-side proof (RLS, routes, workers) is in the live suites named in the blueprint.
- This ADR does not change `dependsOn`, add a module, or touch the architecture documents. `compliance/controls.yaml` gets a row for the new PHI surfaces in the PR that adds them (human approval).

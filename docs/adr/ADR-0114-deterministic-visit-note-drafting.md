# ADR-0114 — Visit notes are drafted deterministically from structured records, never copied forward, confirmed section by section, and amended rather than overwritten

- **Status:** **Accepted** (2026-11-19, the owner). The owner decided this in a design session on 2026-11-18, after a competitor comparison, and accepted it on 2026-11-19. Slice 1 is built behind the per-tenant switch, default off; a tenant is switched on only after the corpus clinical review is approved (`CORPUS_CLINICAL_REVIEW`) and the minimum mobile build per VN-D19 is out.
- **Date:** 2026-11-18
- **Related:** [ADR-0007](ADR-0007-m8-m9-m10-read-direction.md) and [ADR-0043](ADR-0043-m8-completeness-seam-inversion.md) (M8 reaches other modules by seam), [ADR-0012](ADR-0012-ai-governance-contract.md) (AI governance), [ADR-0072](ADR-0072-separate-react-native-expo-mobile-app.md) (separate mobile app), [ADR-0094](ADR-0094-messaging-voice-notes-and-dictation.md) (dictation), architecture §10.5, and [ADR-0113](ADR-0113-visit-note-closure-clock.md) (note-closure clock). The full design is `docs/implementation/04-visit-note-drafting-redesign.md`, whose decision IDs are `VN-D1`…`VN-D30` (the M8 spec has its own D1-D13).
- **Amends:** nothing. It does not change Layer 2; it keeps it a stub. Decision 4 extends the M8 addendum path (M8 spec D2) without changing it.

## Context

Facts read from the repo on 2026-11-18:

- A scheduled visit seeds a draft that carries ids only. Nothing from the care plan, scale readings, vitals, medications or the last visit reaches the note, although the M8 spec promises vitals and scales in the narrative.
- Layer 2 (LLM enhancement) is a deny-stub, and AI is blocked platform-wide (`ai.full` off, M32 inference seams are deny-stubs).
- Gate check 7 (Layer 2 decisions) was `deferred`, and so was gate check 9 (attestation confirmed). Check 9 is in fact enforced where the sign request is parsed: `SignEncounterSchema` requires `attestationConfirmed: true` (`routes/encounters-typebox.ts`, pinned by a test), so slice 1 reclassified it as enforced elsewhere rather than building it. What nothing carried was the clinician's confirmation of what a draft says: the attestation covers the note as a whole, not each drafted section.
- CMS requires the physician's certification narrative to show the patient's individual clinical circumstances and not standard language used for all patients. The top denial reason at CGS is "does not support a terminal prognosis" (53% of denied claims in one CGS quarter).
- A competitor markets, as vendor claims, a visit note drafted from the care plan and the clinician's key observations by a non-generative engine, and a nurse's public comment praises it (unverified). The same competitor also markets separate AI tools, rolling out one at a time (vendor-claimed).

## Decision

**Decision 1 (VN-D4) — The draft is compiled deterministically.** The visit-note draft is built from structured records (linked care-plan goals and interventions, scale readings with their change since the last visit, vitals, medication and order changes) and the clinician's own selections. No LLM and no generated free text is in the draft path. Layer 2 stays a stub; AI suggestions are a separate later decision with their own ADR.

**Decision 2 (VN-D6) — No text copy-forward.** Prior-note text is never carried into a new note. "Changes since last visit" is computed from structured data, and anything carried over needs per-item re-attestation.

**Decision 3 (VN-D7, with the binding clause of VN-D12) — Confirmation is per section and bound to content.** The clinician confirms, edits or deselects each drafted section; signing is blocked until every drafted section is resolved. A confirmation binds to the draft snapshot version, the phrase-corpus version and a hash of the section's rendered content, so a confirmation never covers content the clinician did not see. Source ids per section are kept in the audit trail only; the signed text reads as the clinician's own narrative.

**Decision 4 (VN-D30) — A signed note is amended, never overwritten.** A signed visit note stays modifiable only through an amendment on the existing addendum path. The signed text is preserved. Each amendment is attributed, dated, step-up authenticated and re-confirmed per changed section, and it re-triggers cosign when the author needed one.

## Considered options

- **LLM-first draft.** Rejected: AI is blocked platform-wide, surveyors cannot reconstruct what the clinician chose from what a model wrote, and the trials we found show small or no time savings (one randomised trial: about 9.5% for one scribe, not significant for another) and staff-reported hallucinations (47% of staff in one trial).
- **Copy-forward from the last note.** Rejected: OIG warned that it may facilitate inflated or duplicated claims. A structured "what changed" view gives the speed without that exposure.
- **Per-sentence confirmation.** Rejected: it would raise the click count and defeat the time-to-sign goal. The section is the unit.
- **In-place edit of a signed note.** Rejected: it destroys the record of what was signed, and the M8 spec locks signed encounters as immutable with changes through addenda.
- **Tenant-editable phrase library.** Deferred, not decided here: the platform corpus is curated and versioned in v1 because template sprawl is the known failure of smart-phrase systems.

## Consequences

- Slice 1 builds a per-author draft computed at open, as of the visit time, and adds a new "every drafted section resolved" check (gate check 13); check 9 stays where it is enforced today (see Context). As-of time needs new as-of variants of the care-plan, scale and medication seams.
- Amendments must layer on a frozen authoritative text while source-derived sections render per viewer (Part 2). Modelling that on the M8 addendum state machine is the first task of slice 1.
- The mobile app and any offline path inherit Decision 3: offline confirmations are re-validated at sync and never silently accepted against newer data.
- Rolling this out is per tenant, default off, because `main` auto-deploys.
- The clinical review of the phrase corpus is of one release (VN-D34, slice S2): an approval counts only for the corpus version it names, and the switch cannot be enabled on an approval for another release. Slice S2 adds the recertification visit's own wording as a new release, so S1's approval does not carry over to it.
- Slice S2b adds the RN admission visit's four sections (VN-D35 to VN-D38) as another release, `narrative-vocab@2026.12.1`, again unreviewed. It states only what is on record and reads through existing seams except two additive changes, an as-of variant of M5's caregiver roster and an opt-in `historical` argument on M39's evaluator (a note is signed after the visit, so "was it on file then" must not depend on the record's status today); a draft source's owning module keeps its own read key, asked by template (VN-D37).
- Slice S5 adds the face-to-face visit by a physician or nurse practitioner and the IDG follow-up of every discipline (VN-D52 to VN-D57) as `narrative-vocab@2026.12.4`: no new phrase, existing ones tagged for the new pairs, still unreviewed. A physician's or nurse practitioner's face-to-face note drafts the recorded vitals and the orders in effect at the visit time beside S2's decline evidence, and nothing the clinician judges (no reason for visit, assessment, plan, interventions or attestation); the IDG follow-up drafts as the discipline's own visit does, and its own `idg_followup` section stays the clinician's words because nothing links the visit to the decision that prompted it. The face-to-face draft now also asks `prescribing.read`. The slice also fixes a defect in every draft: a draft computation now waits for all its reads before it answers, so a seam that fails at once can no longer leave a store read running on a connection that has gone back to the pool, and a read abandoned at its time bound is cut off at its next query by a transaction lease that ends with the unit of work. No migration and no new seam.
- Slice S4 adds the social-work and chaplain visits (VN-D45 to VN-D51) as `narrative-vocab@2026.12.3`: six new phrases in one draft-only category, still unreviewed. A social worker's note drafts `interventions` and `plan`, a chaplain's drafts `plan`, from the discipline's own M15 plan as sealed in the signed version in effect at the visit time (M15 supersedes only at discharge, so the latest signed version at or before the visit is the one in effect; the system-built discharge version, made from unsigned working rows, is never read), never from the M7 care plan and never a psychosocial, spiritual or family section. Open goals and active interventions only; no status, progress or outcome is stated, and a goal is attributed to the patient, family or agent only where the record says so and a clinician has not revised it. Part 2 is per viewer through M15's own rule, failing closed; the draft routes ask `discipline_plan.read` (not kind-scoped, not a default grant of these roles: recorded in the register). No cosign, no link and no migration: a confirmation applies to the whole section.
- Slice S3 adds the LPN visits and the aide visit (VN-D39 to VN-D44) as `narrative-vocab@2026.12.2`: no new phrase, existing S1 phrases tagged for them, still unreviewed. An LPN drafts the vitals, interventions and plan, never the PPS or ESAS-r sections; an aide drafts the activities of daily living from the recorded ADLs and the care-plan interventions. A draft reads only the sources its template drafts from and asks the read keys of exactly those, by template, in the service. Cosign is unchanged, and a cosigner without Part 2 disclosure cosigns the redacted view (VN-D13).

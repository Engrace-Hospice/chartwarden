# ADR-0110 — Messaging response analytics: aggregate-only figures over M33, small cells withheld, no individual view

- **Status:** **Proposed** (2026-10-23). Built (see Implementation status). The minimum cohort of 5 and the whole question of who may see which figure are the build team's defaults; HR and compliance concurrence is not recorded and is required before the threshold is treated as settled or any individual-level view is even considered.
- **Date:** 2026-10-23
- **Phase:** Messaging Hub Phase 6, WP 6.2 (capability "usage and response reports").
- **Related:** [ADR-0078](ADR-0078-messaging-supervisor-reassign.md) (the supervisor queue is the live, per-request view; this is the retrospective, aggregate one), [ADR-0069](ADR-0069-messaging-acknowledgment-request-aggregate.md) (the request and event tables), [ADR-0010](ADR-0010-tenancy-rls-invariants.md), the M33 spec (`docs/modules/CHARTWARDEN_MODULE_33_REPORTING_SPEC_v0_2.md`, "consumes every module through seams"), `docs/messaging-hub/04-phases-4-to-6.md` (WP 6.2), `10-ui-spec-phases-4-to-6.md` (6.2).

## Context

WP 6.2 asks for acknowledgment latency, escalation rate, handoff acceptance time and per-discipline response, "via M33", with these guardrails: aggregates only, no content, access-controlled, and not used for individual discipline without HR/compliance review. The risk is not the arithmetic, it is that a response-time table broken down finely enough is a league table of named nurses. Five requests answered by one clinician is that clinician's record whatever the column is called.

## Decision (proposed)

1. **D1: where it lives.** M36 owns the tables, so M36 owns the numbers: it publishes a seam `MessagingResponseAnalyticsReader` (interface in `@chartwarden/types`, registered in `registerMessagingSeams`, fail-closed deny-stub in the M1 registry, `phi: false`). M33 serves the HTTP route `GET /api/v1/messaging/analytics/response` over that seam (`routes/messaging-response-analytics.routes.ts`), exactly the M33 posture of consuming other modules through seams and never through their tables. M33 reads no M36 table, M36 imports nothing from M33. No new table, no new outbox event, no consumer binding, no manifest change.
2. **D2: live query, not a materialized view (v1).** The figures are computed per request from `message_acknowledgment_request`, `message_acknowledgment_event` and `clinical_handoff` inside the caller's tenant context (FORCE RLS plus an explicit `organization_id` filter), over a window of at most 92 days (default 30). A materialized view in `report_mv` was considered and rejected for v1: the cohort rule counts *distinct people*, which is not additive across the daily buckets an MV would hold, and medians are not either. A pre-aggregated MV is the follow-up if the live query becomes slow (the indexes on `organization_id, requested_at`-style access are the first thing to measure). The existing `chartwarden_m36_ack_*` Prometheus gauges are reused for nothing here: they are process-wide, cross-tenant, 24 hour, not durable and not tenant-sliced, so they cannot answer a tenant's question and must not be mixed into a tenant's report. The existing `m36.ack.*` outbox events carry the same facts as the event table (this reads the table, which is the system of record, instead of replaying events).
3. **D3: what is measured.** Acknowledgment latency (median of `acknowledged_at - requested_at`), the share acknowledged at or before the deadline, the escalation rate (a request with at least one `escalated` event), handoff acceptance time (median `accepted_at - offered_at`) with offered and accepted counts, and a breakdown by ack class or by *discipline*. Cancelled requests are excluded.
4. **D4: discipline is a group of roles, never a person.** A request's discipline is the alphabetically first *clinical-staff* role key its assignee holds now (deterministic; a person with two clinical roles counts in one group, so a person is never double counted into a larger cohort). It is read through the identity schema (the sanctioned `membership` / `role` / `membership_role` join) into an in-memory map used only to name a group; the map and the assignee ids never leave the reader.
5. **D5: aggregates only, by construction.** The response contract has no user, patient, message or request field, and a test asserts the body carries no uuid and no field name containing user, person, patient, message, body, email or name. There is no per-person breakdown, no drill-down to requests, no export, and deliberately no route or permission for one. Team and shift breakdowns (10-ui-spec) are not built: team needs a source of truth for team membership that M36 does not own, and shift needs the tenant's timezone and shift definitions; both are later slices and neither would be a per-person view.
6. **D6: small-cell suppression (provisional).** A figure is shown only when at least `ANALYTICS_MIN_COHORT = 5` *distinct people* stand behind it (people, not requests). Rules: (a) the whole report is `insufficient_data` when fewer than 5 distinct assignees stand behind the organisation's requests in the window; (b) a breakdown group below 5 people is left out and only *counted* in `suppressedGroups` (never named, never sized); (c) complementary suppression: when exactly one group would be hidden, the smallest visible group (fewest requests) is hidden too, so "organisation total minus the shown groups" cannot isolate one small group; (d) the handoff figures are null when fewer than 5 distinct recipients stand behind them; (e) a hidden group's median is never merged into an "other" bucket. **The value 5 is provisional**: it follows the common small-cell convention and the 10-ui-spec text, it is not a compliance decision. Lowering it needs HR/compliance sign-off; raising it does not. Known residue: with two or more hidden groups the difference between the total and the shown groups is their *sum*, which is accepted for a provisional floor and recorded here, not hidden.
7. **D7: access.** New permission `messaging.analytics.read` (migration 1170, category `compliance`, ABAC none), granted to `compliance_officer`, `administrator` and `org_admin` (existing tenants by backfill; mirror in `packages/auth` contributed catalog and its test). No "manage" key: configuration is the flag. Not granted to supervisors or clinicians by default; the tenant decides, and the route returns the same aggregate to anyone who holds the key.
8. **D8: flag.** Per-tenant ops flag `messaging.response_analytics` through the existing `feature_flag` mechanism (absent = off; no table or migration for it; set by the platform admin API, step-up gated, not by this change). Off is a uniform 404 for a permitted user; the screen renders "This feature is not turned on for your organization." The flag is read on every call, so a kill switch lands at once.
9. **D9: audit.** The read declares `auditRead: { none: ... }`: it touches no patient and no person, only counts. A future change that adds any row-level or per-person field must change this to a real read audit with a resource id (see CLAUDE.md on `resourceIdParam`) and needs a new ADR and the HR/compliance review.
10. **D10: never.** Individual-level or per-person response views, rankings, scorecards or exports; use in performance management; message content or patient identity in any figure; any machine-learning scoring; cross-tenant benchmarking.

## Implementation status

Built on the WP 6.2 branch: `packages/types/src/messaging-analytics.ts`, `apps/backend/src/modules/36-messaging/analytics/` (reader, pure cohort floor with unit tests), M33 route and TypeBox schema (`routes/messaging-response-analytics*.ts`), seam entry and deny-stub, migration `1170_m36_response_analytics_permission.sql`, permission catalog entry, OpenAPI enrichment and regenerated client, web screen `/messaging/analytics` (`response-analytics.tsx`, hook, wording, CSS, DOM test with axe), live suite `test:m36:response-analytics:live` (and its `ci.yml` job) as the real `chartwarden_app` role.

## Not done / open

- HR and compliance review of the threshold and of the permission's default holders (the clinical lead and privacy officer should also confirm that a per-discipline median over 5 people is acceptable to staff representatives).
- Team and shift breakdowns; a materialized-view path for ranges beyond 92 days; a CSV/PDF export of the aggregate (an export is a new egress surface and needs its own decision).
- A per-tenant minimum cohort *higher* than 5 (tighten-only) is possible later; there is no setting today.
- The platform admin step that sets `messaging.response_analytics` for a real tenant; the demo seed (`db:seed:messaging-flags`) does not switch it on.

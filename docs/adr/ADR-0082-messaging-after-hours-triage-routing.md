# ADR-0082 — After-hours triage routing (WP 4.13): an after-hours item opens an M19 `triage_call` through a guarded seam, the care-team thread shows a fixed-text result, and acknowledgment stays the 4.2 aggregate

- **Status:** **Proposed** (2026-10-19). Nothing in this ADR is built. If accepted it is built flag-off and fail-closed behind a new tenant flag `messaging.afterhours_triage` (absent = off). **OPEN: E1 (no M19 owner is named), E2 (who may be addressed by role and who may address a role), E3 (this ADR route), E4 (fallback recipient when the on-call lookup fails) and E5 (an escalated assignee who is not in the channel), all in `docs/messaging-hub/11-decision-sheet.md` section E, plus the M19 change in "What changes in M19".** No owner or approval is claimed. On `main` the real tier-1 router is unbound by default ([ADR-0057](ADR-0057-escalation-ownership-m19-m36.md) item 12), so until M19 binds it every after-hours routing attempt would be refused as "roster not connected".

## Context

Checked in the code, not assumed:

- **M19 already owns the after-hours clinical record.** `triage_call` (`packages/db/src/schema/oncall/triage-calls.ts`) is "THE after-hours clinical record": nine states, with the open states in `TRIAGE_CALL_OPEN_STATES` (`packages/types/src/oncall.ts`). The transition table is `TRIAGE_CALL_EDGES` in `apps/backend/src/modules/19-oncall/lifecycle/triage-call.ts`. A call has a nullable `patient_id` (a call exists before the caller is identified), a caller kind (`patient`, `caregiver`, `external_clinician`, `portal_session`, `system_inbound`, `bereaved_family`), an inbound channel (`phone`, `sms`, `email`, `portal_message`, `inbound_visit_failure`, `inbound_evv_anomaly`, `inbound_alert_failure`) and a `source` (`manual`, `no_show`, `late_checkout`, `geofence_anomaly`, `m36_delivery_failure`, `paper_origin`).
- **A human opens a call through one route, and the sender of a chat message cannot use it.** `POST /api/v1/triage-calls` (`routes/triage-calls.routes.ts`) needs `triage.open` and the ABAC predicate `oncall_eligible` (an active covering shift, or a supervisor / org-admin / medical-director role, or M17's tonight roster; `abac-predicates.ts`). Its `source` is closed to `manual` (`OpenableSource` in `routes/m19-typebox.ts`) on purpose, so a caller cannot back-date or mislabel a call. The covering shift is resolved server-side, because `on_call_shift_id` is NOT NULL; no covering shift is a 409 that names the gap.
- **Machine-opened calls already exist, and one is M36's.** The D10 consumers (`consumers/inbound-trigger.ts`, `m17-no-show.ts`, `m18-late-checkout.ts`, `m36-delivery-failed.ts`) open a call from an outbox event with `caller_kind = system_inbound`, dedupe on `source_outbox_event_id` (`triage_call_source_dedupe_uq`), coalesce onto an open machine-opened call for the same patient, and, when nothing covers the instant, publish `m19.oncall_shift.coverage_gap` instead of a call. `m36-delivery-failed.ts` opens a patient-less operational call (`source = m36_delivery_failure`, channel `inbound_alert_failure`). That file notes that no producer publishes `m36.notification.delivery_failed` yet, so the consumer is wired but not exercised. This is the precedent for an M36-originated call; there is no precedent for an M36-originated call that carries a patient.
- **M19 SLAs exist and are M19's.** `jobs/triage-sla-policy.ts` and `packages/types/src/oncall.ts`: callback target default 30 minutes (`TRIAGE_CALLBACK_SLA_DEFAULT_MINUTES`, tenant `callback_sla_per_severity`), awaiting-callback grace 60 minutes (tighten-only), per-tier escalation defaults 15 / 30 / 15 / 60 / 15 minutes (`TRIAGE_ESCALATION_SLA_DEFAULT_MINUTES`), with a mandatory escalation capped at the tightest tier default. Decision D6 (`brief-m19-owner.md`, `brief-clinical-lead-signoff.md`): triage SLAs are untouched and the stricter clock wins where both apply.
- **M19 events M36 could read exist, mostly id-only.** `apps/backend/src/modules/19-oncall/events-by-m19.ts` catalogues `m19.triage_call.opened`, `.disposition_selected`, `.documented`, `.closed`, `.escalation_recorded`, `.callback_sla_breached`, `.escalation_sla_breached` and `m19.oncall_shift.coverage_gap`, all with `declaredConsumers: []` for the triage-call events. `disposition_selected` carries `dispositionData: Type.Unknown()`, so a consumer must not copy it. M36's existing consumer of the SLA and coverage-gap events is `outbox-consumers/oncall-alerts.ts` (alerts only, no thread message).
- **The seam is tier 1 and `rn` only.** The real router (`19-oncall/seams/escalation-router.ts`) answers tier 1 from the covering shift of the patient's provider entity and answers `unavailable` for tier 2 and above. `ESCALATION_DISCIPLINE_BY_ROLE` maps only `rn` to `clinical_rn`; `physician` is `no_route_configured` today, although ADR-0081 lists `physician` as an addressable role. It is `unbound by default` and answers only for organizations in `ESCALATION_ROUTER_ORGS` (`brief-m19-owner.md`).
- **The 4.2 aggregate is per message, per patient, per care-team channel.** `AckRow` / `NewAckRequest` (`apps/backend/src/modules/36-messaging/ack-requests/ack-types.ts`) require `messageId`, `channelId`, `patientId`, one `assignedUserId`, and optionally `addressedRole` (ADR-0081, migration 1129). Acknowledgment is one explicit action by the assigned, still-qualified user; opening or reading never writes (ADR-0069).
- **The escalation walk skips next-tier users who cannot view the channel** (ADR-0070 interim; decision E5). An after-hours on-call who is not on the patient's care team is therefore skipped today.
- **There is no family or caregiver messaging into M36.** Family messaging without an app is WP 6.7, proposed and unbuilt (`10-ui-spec-phases-4-to-6.md` §9). "Family calls" in the 4.13 row can therefore only reach M19 through M19's own intake (phone or portal, M31), not through a chat message.

The failure to design against is hazard H-02 (wrong recipient or nobody), and its twin here: an after-hours item that exists in one module while the other module and the people in it believe it is handled.

## Decision (proposed)

1. **M19 owns the triage record; M36 never creates one by writing M19 tables.** An after-hours item becomes a `triage_call` only inside M19, through a new narrow seam in `packages/types` (ADR-0005; no import edge from M36 to M19). Working name `AfterHoursTriageOpener`. M36 calls it; M19 implements it. M19's lifecycle, SLAs, shift resolution and escalation rungs are not copied, wrapped or reimplemented in M36. The seam is deny-stub by default, like `EscalationRouter`.
2. **Two entry paths, kept separate.**
   - **Path A, a staff message after hours (M36 to M19).** A clinician who holds the role-send permission (ADR-0081, `messaging.oncall.send`) addresses the on-call role. The sender confirms the named person as in ADR-0081. If the tenant's after-hours determination says it is after hours (see question Q6; **not defined anywhere on `main`**), M36 additionally asks the opener seam to open one call for that message and patient. The message and its ack request are exactly the ADR-0081 urgent send; the call is the clinical record, the request is the accountability record.
   - **Path B, a family or staff call that arrives in M19's own intake (M19 to M36).** The call is opened by M19's existing means (`POST /api/v1/triage-calls` by an eligible clinician, or a D10 consumer). M36 only surfaces the result in the care-team thread (decision 5). Nothing in M36 is on the intake path, so a family caller never addresses a role through M36.
3. **Who is addressed.** Path A addresses the person the sender confirmed from the tier-1 resolution (ADR-0081 decisions 3 and 4), through the same guarded router (`guardEscalationRouter`). Role map: `rn` only until M19 maps another role (`physician` is unmapped today). A coverage gap, `roster_not_connected`, "no ladder" and a router failure are refusals with the ADR-0081 telephone-procedure wording; **nothing is sent, no call is opened, and there is no fallback recipient** (proposed answer to E4; the alternative `tenant_oncall_policy.emergency_contact_e164` named in `brief-m19-owner.md` is an open decision, not built here). A router exception propagates and is never turned into a gap or a person.
4. **How the call is opened (M19 side, needs the M19 owner).** The seam implementation reuses the D10 machine-open pattern, not the human route: `caller_kind` and channel chosen from existing values or new ones (Q3), a dedupe key derived from the M36 message id so a retry never opens a second call, the covering shift resolved by M19, and the existing patient-grain coalesce so a second item about a patient with an open machine-opened call joins it instead of paging the same nurse twice. No covering shift publishes `m19.oncall_shift.coverage_gap` (existing behaviour, which already alerts `org_admin` and `clinical_supervisor` through `oncall-alerts.ts`), and the M36 send is refused or the message is sent without a call as decided in Q4. A new `triage_call_source` value is likely (the closed enum has no M36 chat value); that is an M19 schema change and an open question, not a decision.
5. **Surfacing the result in the care-team thread.** M36 consumes M19's id-only events by name (as `oncall-alerts.ts` does) and posts at most one fixed-text system line per stage into the linked care-team thread: call opened, disposition recorded (the disposition enum word only, mapped to fixed wording), documented, closed. It never copies `chief_complaint_text`, caller quote, transcript, answers, `dispositionData`, caller phone or protocol content (the `triage_call` columns M19 itself denylists, §3.6 in `triage-calls.ts`). The line names the call by reference id only. Whether disposition words in a thread are acceptable is a compliance question (Q8). The thread never becomes the triage record (the hub's design rule).
6. **Acknowledgment stays the 4.2 aggregate, and the two clocks never merge.** The on-call clinician acknowledges the message's request with the existing explicit action; that is the only way the request leaves `waiting`. Opening, working or closing the `triage_call` does not acknowledge the request, and acknowledging the request does not advance the call. The ack deadline (important 60/120, urgent 15/30, Critical 5/10, provisional) and the triage SLAs both run; **the stricter clock wins** and neither is edited (D6). M36's timers and walk (ADR-0070) are unchanged, including the interim that skips a next-tier user who cannot view the channel.
7. **Linking the two records.** One M36-owned, id-only link (message id, request id, `triage_call_id`, organization) written in the same transaction as the send, unique per message so a retry converges. Shape (new table versus a column on the ack request) is open (Q7). The link is how the consumer in decision 5 finds the thread, and how M36 ignores `m19.triage_call.*` events for calls it did not originate unless Path B correlation is accepted (Q5).
8. **Flag, permission, fail-closed.** New tenant flag `messaging.afterhours_triage` (absent = off). It also requires `messaging.role_send`, `messaging.ack_requests`, `messaging.urgent_clinical` and `messaging.sbar` (the ADR-0081 chain), checked in the service before any router or seam call, so off is the uniform 404. No new permission is proposed for Path A (it reuses `messaging.oncall.send`, granted to no tenant role by default); the M19 system-open path is a D10-style machine action with no actor permission. Flag off: no call is opened by M36 and no thread line is posted; M19 behaves exactly as today. Any seam failure, an unbound opener, or a refused call is a visible refusal or an explicit "sent without a triage call" state as decided in Q4; it is never a silent drop and never a guessed recipient.

## What changes in M19 (needs the named owner, E1)

Proposed, none of it done: (a) implement the opener seam (system-open with a message-derived dedupe key, patient attached when the caller is an identified patient of the org, org check and patient-in-org check as the router does); (b) decide the `triage_call_source` and channel values (Q3), which is an enum and CHECK change in M19's schema; (c) if Path B correlation is wanted, declare M36 a consumer of the triage-call events (`declaredConsumers` is empty today). **Unchanged:** the nine-state machine and `TRIAGE_CALL_EDGES`, `triage.open` and `oncall_eligible` on the human route, the closed `OpenableSource`, `callback-sla`, `escalation-sla` and `awaiting-callback-timeout` crons, their defaults and the tenant tighten-only rules, the mandatory-escalation ceiling, the D10 dedupe and coalesce, the coverage-gap watchdog. Triage SLAs are untouched; where an M36 deadline and an M19 SLA both apply the stricter wins ([`brief-m19-owner.md`](../messaging-hub/brief-m19-owner.md), D6).

## PHI, tenancy and RLS

- Tenant comes from the request context (Path A) or the outbox envelope (Path B consumer), never from a payload or body field, as in `oncall-alerts.ts` and `promotion-outcomes.ts`. The router and opener assert the caller's org and that the patient belongs to it; a cross-org or unknown patient is the same uniform refusal.
- The link row is tenant-scoped under the three-column rule with FORCEd RLS on `organization_id` ([ADR-0010](ADR-0010-tenancy-rls-invariants.md)); `arch:tenancy` and the manifest `.ts` and `.cjs` mirror apply if a table is added (Q7).
- Ids, enums and instants only cross the seam and the events; message text, SBAR and any clinical free text stay in the message and are never sent to M19, and nothing M19 denylists comes back. Logs carry the organization, the role and a status word, never a patient, name, text or recipient.
- Out-of-band notices keep the ADR-0073 / WP 4.7 rule: no patient identifiers in a push, SMS or email.
- Live suites for this work prove RLS as the real `wardenx_app` NOBYPASSRLS role on a private database (repo rule), and use the real catalog-validating publisher, not `NOOP_OUTBOX`.

## Hazards (listed here; not added to the register in `docs/messaging-hub/05-clinical-workflows-and-safety.md`)

The register's H-01 to H-10 are fixed by that document; this ADR does not renumber or extend it. Proposed new hazards for the clinical lead to accept into the register, or reject:

- **A. Two records, one believed handled.** The call is worked in M19 while the request stays `waiting` (or the reverse). Mitigation: decision 6 keeps both clocks live, the unacknowledged queue (WP 4.9) still shows the request, and no state is mirrored silently.
- **B. Off-team on-call skipped (E5).** The after-hours on-call is usually not on the care team, so the existing walk skips them. Mitigation proposed: the on-call works the call in M19, and the thread line is informational; the request ack for an off-team user stays blocked until E5 is decided.
- **C. Wrong "after hours".** The after-hours determination (Q6) is wrong in either direction (a daytime item opened as a call, or a night item left as chat). Mitigation: the sender sees whether a triage call will be opened before sending, and the determination is M19's schedule truth, not an M36 clock.
- **D. Disposition shown in a thread to people who should not see it.** Mitigation: fixed wording from the enum only, restricted-channel handling follows ADR-0078 and Part 2 review (Q8).
- **E. Duplicate calls on retry or redelivery (H-09 family).** Mitigation: message-derived dedupe key plus M19's patient-grain coalesce.
- **F. Messaging as the emergency path (H-03).** The permanent "not the emergency path" notice stays on the composer; routing an item into a triage call is not an emergency dispatch.

## Alternatives rejected

- **M36 writes `triage_call` rows directly.** Violates M19 ownership and ADR-0011 (no direct cross-module table access) and would bypass the shift resolution and the coverage-gap behaviour.
- **Let the sender call `POST /api/v1/triage-calls`.** The route is gated on `oncall_eligible`, which a chat sender is not, and widening it would change who can open a clinical record (M19's tamper-resistance reasoning for `OpenableSource`).
- **Merge the ack deadline and the triage SLA into one clock.** Contradicts D6 (SLAs untouched, stricter wins) and would let one module's tenant override loosen the other's floor.
- **Automatic fallback to tier 2, a manager, the care-team primary or a telephone number when the lookup fails.** The router has no tier 2 and no owner has defined a ladder; a widened page is a silent change of who is paged (H-02). Left to E4.
- **Close the triage call when the request is acknowledged, or the reverse.** Acknowledging a message is not a clinical disposition; coupling them lets a read-and-tap end a call.
- **Copy the call's content or disposition detail into the thread.** Makes chat a second, unscrubbed clinical record.
- **Family messaging through M36 now.** There is no such channel (WP 6.7 is unbuilt); building one to feed 4.13 is a new outbound surface needing its own ADR and a privacy review.

## Plan of engineering slices (all flag-off, once this ADR is accepted and E1 is answered)

1. **S1, types and deny-stub.** `AfterHoursTriageOpener` interface and a fail-closed deny-stub in `packages/types` and the M36 registry (the WP 4.3 pattern); an in-memory fake for tests only; guard wrapper that refuses a malformed or test-source answer. No behaviour change.
2. **S2, M19 opener implementation (M19 owner).** System-open with a message-derived dedupe key, org and patient-in-org checks, coverage-gap publication, source and channel values from Q3, a migration if the enum changes (next free ordinal checked at build time), M19 live suite for dedupe, coalesce and no-coverage.
3. **S3, M36 link and send path.** The id-only link and the flagged extension of the ADR-0081 role send (same transaction as the message and request), refusals per decision 3, the flag, and a live suite on the real catalog-validating publisher.
4. **S4, thread surfacing.** The consumer of the M19 triage-call events, fixed-text lines from a closed map, tenant from the envelope, redelivery converges, malformed event throws to DLQ; live suite that the line lands and carries no content.
5. **S5, web.** Composer indicator "this will also open an after-hours triage call", card line, text-and-icon states, wording for clinical-lead approval; axe and keyboard checks.
6. **S6, acceptance.** Live: an after-hours item reaches the on-call clinician and is acknowledged through the 4.2 aggregate (the 4.13 acceptance in `04-phases-4-to-6.md`), across a shift boundary and DST through the real router, with the stricter clock proven. Blocked on E1, E5 and a bound router.

## Consequences (if accepted)

- One new seam, one M36 consumer, one link, one flag; at most one migration in M36 (the link) and one in M19 (the source enum), each checked against `arch:migration-numbering`. No change to the ack aggregate's state machine or to M19's triage lifecycle and SLAs.
- Until a router is bound for a tenant, the feature answers "roster not connected" and refuses, which is the honest behaviour.

## Known ceilings

- Tier 1 and `rn` only, as the router stands; the sender confirms one person.
- The check-then-send window of ADR-0081 decision 4 applies, plus one more: the call is opened after the person is confirmed, so the shift could change in between. The call's own `on_call_shift_id` is M19's resolution at open time, not the sender's.
- The thread line is informational and is only as complete as the events M19 publishes today; `dispositionData` is deliberately not read.
- Live acceptance needs the real router bound for a test organization (`ESCALATION_ROUTER_ORGS`).

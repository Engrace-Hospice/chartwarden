# ADR-0070 — Escalation timers: the database decides what is due, a delayed BullMQ job per (request, tier, window) and a per-minute sweeper only wake it, and the escalation is ONE guarded write; with no router bound nothing is invented, the request stays open and visible

- **Status:** **Accepted** (2026-10-15; decisions closed in the WP 4.4 blueprint). **Every per-tier window, the placeholder role key, the "one assignee per tier" reading and the record cadence below are PROVISIONAL: no clinical-lead sign-off is recorded** (`docs/messaging-hub/brief-clinical-lead-signoff.md`, section 6). The escalation ACTION ships behind the ops flag `messaging.escalation_timers`, default off.
- **Amended by [ADR-0085](ADR-0085-messaging-audited-thread-read-for-escalated-assignee.md) (proposed, not accepted) for accepted limit (i) only:** with its flag on, the walk would choose a qualified next-tier user who cannot view the channel and grant them that one thread; until it is accepted and built, limit (i) stands as written.
- **Date:** 2026-10-15
- **Phase:** Messaging Hub Phase 4 (WP 4.4, Durable timers and backup routing).
- **Implements:** [ADR-0057](ADR-0057-escalation-ownership-m19-m36.md) decision 4 ("timers stay in M36") and the fall-back note of item 11 (record the resolved tier and assignee on the request; never re-derive ownership from a clock).
- **Extends:** [ADR-0069](ADR-0069-messaging-acknowledgment-request-aggregate.md) (the `escalated` status, written only here; the update-guard trigger and the transition table, unchanged), [ADR-0056](ADR-0056-clinical-acknowledgment-vs-read-receipts.md) (D4 per-tier windows).
- **Followed by:** [ADR-0071](ADR-0071-messaging-delivery-failure-handling.md) (WP 4.5: early escalation on a failed route and `failed_unreachable`, which this ADR never writes).
- **Related:** [ADR-0068](ADR-0068-messaging-urgency-model.md), [ADR-0010](ADR-0010-tenancy-rls-invariants.md), [ADR-0011](ADR-0011-ownership-inversion-rls-bypass.md).

## Context

WP 4.2 stores a request's `deadline_at` and nothing acts on it; WP 4.3 ships the `EscalationRouter` seam and a fail-closed deny-stub (no M19 owner is named). WP 4.4 (`docs/messaging-hub/04-phases-4-to-6.md`): "BullMQ job per (request_id, tier) with deduplication; on deadline mark overdue, route to the next tier, new delivery; restart-safe; DLQ audited. Done when: kill worker mid-timer gives exactly one escalation after recovery; duplicate job gives no duplicate incident."

Facts that shape the design:

- A queue is not a source of truth. Redis can be flushed, a job can be lost between a commit and an add, a worker can die holding a job, a job can be delivered twice. "Exactly one escalation" therefore cannot live in the queue.
- Today every `resolveTier` answers `unavailable` (the deny-stub), so **cannot route is the production behaviour**, not an edge case. A timer that does nothing in that case, or invents a recipient, is the failure ADR-0057 exists to prevent.
- ADR-0069 froze the state machine in the database. `escalated` and a tier-up (`escalated -> escalated`, tier strictly increasing) already exist; two things were missing for a usable escalation: the tier's clinician could not become the assignee (so could never acknowledge), and the next window could not start after the first (the maximum is relative to `window_started_at`, which only a reassign could move).

## Decision

1. **The database is the source of truth for "what is due"; BullMQ is the wake-up.** A request is due when it is `pending` or `escalated` and `deadline_at` has passed. Two things call the same function, `processDue(org, request)`: a delayed job armed per window, and a sweeper. Neither decides anything.
2. **One delayed job per (request, tier, window deadline), deterministic job id** `ack-esc-<request>-<tier>-<deadline ms>` (no colon: BullMQ rejects it). The id carries the window's deadline on top of the plan's `(request, tier)` because a reassign restarts tier 0 with a new deadline: the old job (old id) finds a different window and does nothing, the new window arms its own id. A duplicate add of the same window is a no-op in BullMQ. Payload: organization, request, tier, deadline instant (ids only; a payload that is not exactly that is **unrecoverable**: no retry, straight to the DLQ). Queue `messaging.ack_escalation_timer`, five attempts, exponential backoff 2 s to 60 s, concurrency 5.
3. **Arming is an outbox consumer** (`m36.ack-timer-arming`) on `m36.ack.requested`, `.reassigned` and `.escalated`. It re-reads the request's CURRENT window from the database and adds that job (so a stale or replayed event arms the right job); a Redis failure is rethrown and the outbox retries. It ignores the flag.
4. **A per-minute sweeper** (`messaging.ack_escalation_sweep`, repeatable trigger under a fixed scheduler id) fans out over active organizations and runs `sweep(org)`: up to 500 due requests, oldest deadline first, each in its own transaction, a per-request failure isolated and counted, any failure fails the tick (visible in the failed set). It is the safety net for a flushed or lost queue (a lost Redis delays an escalation by at most one tick) and the retry loop while no router can answer.
5. **The escalation is ONE guarded write.** `processDue`: flag check; read the request; if not due (or not the window the job was armed for) return `not_due`; ask the router (outside the transaction: a read) and pick the recipient; then in ONE transaction lock the request `FOR UPDATE`, re-check that it is still due **at the same version** (else `stale`), `UPDATE ... WHERE version = <read version>` to `escalated` with the new tier, the new assignee and a new window, insert the history row `escalated` (actor null = the system, `from`/`to` status, previous assignee, tier) and publish the id-only `m36.ack.escalated` event. Commit.
6. **Exactly-once argument.** Two executors (a job, a retry, a second worker, the sweeper) can both decide to escalate. The first to lock the row commits a new version, tier and deadline; the second, once it holds the lock, re-reads and finds the version moved, so it writes nothing (`stale`) or, if it started later, finds the request not due (`not_due`). The conditional `UPDATE ... WHERE version` is the arbiter even if the lock were ever bypassed. A crash before commit leaves nothing and the job or the sweeper redoes it; a crash after commit and before the job is acknowledged redelivers a job whose window no longer matches. An acknowledgment racing the deadline is decided by the same lock in both orders: acknowledged first, the escalation sees a new version and does nothing; escalated first, the old assignee can no longer acknowledge (not the assignee) and the tier's clinician can. The live suite proves each (8-way concurrency, duplicate delivery, ack before / during / after, a flushed Redis). The worker-killed case proves that a STALLED job is recovered by BullMQ and runs once; it does not itself claim a crash between the write and the acknowledgment: "exactly once after a partial write" rests on the single-transaction write and on the concurrency and duplicate-delivery cases (a committed escalation can never be repeated, an uncommitted one leaves nothing).
7. **Who is asked and who is chosen.** The router is asked with the request's own patient, a fixed placeholder role key (`ACK_ESCALATION_REQUIRED_ROLE = "rn"`: the request stores no `required_role`, ADR-0069), `tier = current_tier + 1`, and `asOf` = the clock's instant as ISO with `Z`. The seam is reached through `guardEscalationRouter`, the org-checking wrapper ADR-0057 asked for, called inside the tenant's system request scope (a timer is not a request): it validates `nextTier` (null, or a whole number above the tier asked about and not above 99; a malformed one is turned into `unavailable`, recorded and visible, never thrown into a retry loop) and refuses a call whose organization or patient is not an id, an answer for another tier or instant, a test source (`in_memory_fake`) and a malformed recipient list. The first recipient who is not the sender, not the assignee who did not act, and passes `stillQualified` (active, keys, Part 2 gate, sees the channel) becomes the assignee; another tenant's user is therefore never chosen (the database also refuses a non-member assignee). A tier with a gap, or whose people are all unusable, is skipped to the router's `nextTier` (ADR-0057 item 5). Lookup errors propagate and the job retries; nothing is decided on a blip.
8. **Cannot route is a visible state, not a silence.** `unavailable` (any reason but `tier_not_configured`), a gap or unusable people at the end of the ladder: the request is **not changed**. The timer writes a history row `route_unavailable` (and the id-only `m36.ack.route_unavailable` event) with a per-request **backoff of 1 h, 2 h, 4 h ... capped at 24 h** (the n-th record waits `min(1 h * 2^(n-1), 24 h)`); `tier_not_configured`, and a final tier (`nextTier` null) whose people are all unusable (the sender, the assignee who did not act, not qualified), write `ladder_exhausted` the same way. **The backoff is decided in the sweep's query** (an anti-join on the newest record of the current window), so backed-off requests never consume the 500-request batch and cannot starve a newer routable one. **After 8 records per window no more rows, events or alerts are written**, but the router is still probed once a day (inside a 5-minute window after each 24 h boundary from the last record), so a router bound later can still escalate the request. The supervisor audience is told of an exhausted ladder on the **first `ladder_exhausted` record** only (counted per event type with `typeOccurrence`, so an earlier `route_unavailable` does not use it up; the shared `occurrence` drives only the backoff and the cap); later records go to the sender and assignee alone. The sender and the current assignee (and, for `ladder_exhausted`, the supervisor audience through a seeded role rule) get a generic in-app alert, fixed text. The router is asked again on that schedule, so the moment a router is bound the request escalates (within the backoff, at most a day); the backoff and the cap keep the history and the inbox from filling. Reads of an overdue request carry `escalation_note` (`route_unavailable` / `ladder_exhausted`, derived from the history of the CURRENT window with one batched query per read) so the card can say plainly that it is still open. **`failed_unreachable` is never written here**: it is WP 4.5's and means delivery failure.
9. **Migration 1114, no new table.** (a) Two event types, `route_unavailable` and `ladder_exhausted` (`ALTER TYPE ... ADD VALUE`, nothing uses them in the same transaction). (b) The update guard of 1107 is replaced in full: `escalated` is written only with an empty `app.user_id` (a person's session is refused), an escalation may move the assignee to an active member of the organization and start the next window within five minutes of the database's `now()` (like a reassign). The guard also refuses an escalation whose recipient is the sender or whose deadline is not within (now(), now() + 60 minutes]. The transition table, the immutable columns, the version rule and every other actor rule are unchanged. **Like 1107's it is SECURITY DEFINER with a pinned `search_path`**; the database refuses only a non-member / inactive assignee and the sender, while "qualified and on the right tier" is the application's check. The pin test now reads the 1114 text and still compares it with `ACK_TRANSITIONS`. The window is started by the DATABASE clock (`now()` in the update) so the guard's "starts now" rule holds whatever the application clock says. In short: the database state decides what is due; the "past its deadline" comparison uses the application clock; only the new window's start and the trigger use the database's `now()`.
10. **Per-tier windows (ADR-0056 D4), provisional.** Tier n gets: important 30, 15, 7, 5 minutes (half the previous, floor 5; the last repeats), urgent 7 then 5, critical 3 then 2. Tier 0 is the class default (60 / 15 / 5). Every value is within the class's platform maximum (a test pins it). M19's `tierAckWindowMinutes` is not in the contract (ADR-0057).
11. **Flag `messaging.escalation_timers`, default off, absent = off, read through the same `FeatureFlagReaderPort` path as `messaging.ack_requests`.** It gates the ACTION: with it off `processDue` returns before reading the request, the sweeper returns immediately, and a due request is left exactly as it is, still open and shown as overdue. **Why gating the action and not arming is safe:** arming only adds a delayed job that carries ids; the job's only effect is to call `processDue`, which re-reads the database and obeys the flag. So a job armed while the flag was off does nothing, and a flag switched on later takes effect on the next tick without re-arming anything. Gating arming instead would lose timers for requests created while the flag was off and need a back-fill. **While the flag is off nobody is told that a request is overdue** (the card still shows it as overdue to whoever opens it; there is no alert). That is by design while the flags are off and must be revisited before the flag is enabled for a tenant. **Enabling the flag escalates every request that is already overdue at the next tick**: switch it on only for a tenant without old open requests, or after settling them. `messaging.urgent_clinical`, `messaging.ack_requests` and `messaging.escalation_timers` stay off for every tenant (there is no migration, seed or default that turns any on).
12. **DLQ, audited.** A timer job that exhausts its retries (or is unrecoverable) is copied to the declared queue `messaging.ack_escalation_timer.dlq` AND recorded as an `m36.dlq_job` audit row in the job's tenant (`buildDlqAuditRecord`: queue, job id, a SHA-256 of the payload, instant, the error CLASS, `disposition: pending`; never the payload or the message), and the shared DLQ-promotion alert fires. A failure to write either is reported, never swallowed. A job in the DLQ loses nothing: the sweeper still finds the request. A job BullMQ fails inside Redis for stalling more often than allowed (`stalled more than allowable limit`, which the Worker itself does not report) is caught through a QueueEvents listener and lands the same way. A poison payload is one attempt and cannot occupy the queue.
13. **Events and consumers, all wired.** Catalog (`events-by-m36.ts`): `m36.ack.escalated` (adds `previousAssignedUserId`, `tier`, `deadlineAt`), `m36.ack.route_unavailable`, `m36.ack.ladder_exhausted` (add `tier`), all outbox-only with declared consumer `m36`. Consumers bound in `apps/backend/src/index.ts`: `m36.ack-timer-arming` (requested, reassigned, escalated) and `m36.ack-escalation-notifications` (the three new events). The notification consumer follows the WP 4.2 stale-event rule: the new assignee is alerted only if the request is still `escalated`, still assigned to them, at the event's version, visible and qualified; the not-routed alerts only if the request is still open at the event's version; a lookup error is rethrown. Three seeded in-app-only alert rules. Out-of-band delivery is WP 4.6 and is not built.
14. **A reassign restarts the window** (ADR-0069) and arms the new window's job; the old job is not cancelled, it simply finds another window. **Delete cancels** through the existing cancel; the stale job finds a cancelled request. Acknowledged, resolved, cancelled and `failed_unreachable` requests are never due.
15. **Web.** The card (`ack-wording.ts`, `ack-request-card.tsx`) shows an escalated request ("Escalated to you because it was not taken in time" / "...to the next responsible person..."), treats an overdue escalated request as overdue, and, when the server sends `escalation_note`, says plainly that nobody has taken responsibility, the deadline passed, it could not be passed on (or there is no one further) and it is still open. The hook announces, once and politely (the always-mounted polite region, never an alert), a re-read that shows a request escalated to the viewer or passed on to the next person; the next unchanged read clears it. No new screen.

## Data model

- Migration `packages/db/migrations/1114_m36_ack_escalation_timers.sql`: two enum values and the replaced update guard. No table, grant or policy. Code mirrors: `packages/db/src/enums.ts`, `packages/types/src/messaging-ack.ts` (`ACK_EVENT_TYPES`, `ACK_ESCALATION_TIMERS_FLAG_KEY`, `ACK_ESCALATION_REQUIRED_ROLE`, `ACK_ESCALATION_NOTES`).
- Queues in `packages/messaging/src/jobs/queues.ts`: `messaging.ack_escalation_timer` (+ `.dlq`) and `messaging.ack_escalation_sweep` (+ `.dlq`), owned by `@wardenx/messaging` like every messaging queue (`arch:queue-names`).

## Alternatives considered

- **Trust the queue (job = the escalation).** Fails the first Redis flush and the first duplicate. Rejected.
- **A pure sweeper, no jobs.** Correct but wakes up to a minute late for every request, and the plan names a per-(request, tier) job. Kept both: the job gives timeliness, the sweeper gives recovery.
- **Key the job on (request, tier) only.** A reassign returns to tier 0 and would collide with the finished tier-0 job. The window deadline in the id fixes it.
- **Gate arming with the flag.** Loses timers created while off. Rejected (decision 11).
- **Write `failed_unreachable` when no tier answers.** That state means every delivery route failed (WP 4.5); conflating it hides a configuration gap as a delivery failure. Rejected; the request stays open and is told apart by its history row and `escalation_note`.
- **A column for the retry schedule or the resolved recipients.** The request already stores tier and assignee, and the history stores the rest; a new column would need guard changes for no gain. The hourly cadence is derived from the history.
- **Notify every user of the tier.** The aggregate has one assignee ("one qualified clinician takes responsibility"). Only the first usable user is the assignee and is alerted; the others are not told. Listed as an accepted limit.

## Consequences

- Today (deny-stub) an unacknowledged request does not move: it stays with its assignee, a `route_unavailable` row is written once an hour, the sender and assignee are told in-app, and the card says it is still open. When an M19 router is bound the same request escalates on the next tick.
- A lost or flushed Redis costs at most one sweep interval (60 s) of lateness.
- The escalation, its history row and its event commit together or not at all.
- Accepted limits: (a) only the first qualified user of a tier is assigned and alerted (the provisional one-assignee-per-tier reading), and a person who sits on two tiers can be routed again at a later tier; (b) the role key is a placeholder (`rn`) until the M19 owner and the clinical lead name the role-to-ladder map; (c) per-tier windows, the backoff (1 h to 24 h), the cap of 8 records and the daily 5-minute probe window are provisional (at the cap a request is probed only inside that window each day, so a router bound later escalates it within a day; a worker down for the whole window skips that day); (d) the sweep asks the router once per tick for each request that is due and not backed off, bounded by the 500-request batch; (e) the history stamps use the application clock while the window uses the database clock (equal in production; the live suite moves the first to make requests due); (f) enabling the flag escalates every already-overdue request at the next tick; (g) a stale job is not cancelled when a request is reassigned or deleted, it exits as a no-op; (h) no out-of-band delivery (WP 4.6), no out-of-band fallback recipient (`failed_unreachable` is WP 4.5, ADR-0071); (i) **the walk does not choose a next-tier user who cannot view the request's channel (interim built; the access decision is open).** Observed over HTTP on `main` 9604f58ad (router bound): the walk qualified a next-tier user with `makeStillQualified` only (active, the messaging keys and `messaging.urgent.receive`, the Part 2 gate), which checks neither care-team nor channel membership, unlike the first send (ADR-0068 decision 6). A user outside the channel was chosen (`escalated`, tier 1, previous assignee replaced) but the escalation notification consumer skipped them (`recipient_cannot_view_channel`, no alert), every escalation route answered 404 for them (`visibleRequest()` checks channel visibility first), the previous assignee could no longer act, and `failed_unreachable` stayed 0, so nothing flagged the stall. **Interim, built:** the shared decider (`createTierDecider`, used by the deadline timer, the sweeper and the WP 4.5 delivery-failure handler) now also asks `canViewChannel` (the predicate the ack service and the notification consumer use, wired with `throwOnSeamUnavailable` so a consent-seam outage throws and retries instead of reading as "cannot view") after `stillQualified`, for each candidate that is not the sender or the current assignee. A candidate who cannot view is skipped exactly like an unqualified one: to the next user of the tier, then to the router's `nextTier`; at the end of the ladder the walk records `ladder_exhausted`, the request stays with its current assignee (who can still act) and the supervisors are alerted as in decision 7. In the delivery-failure handler the same outcome is `failed_unreachable`, assignee unchanged. `makeStillQualified` is unchanged for the acknowledge/resolve transitions and the urgent consumer, and the first-send rules are unchanged. **Not solved:** an after-hours on-call who is not on the care team or in the channel is skipped until E5 is decided, so for a patient whose on-call is always off-team the ladder ends in `ladder_exhausted` (a loud supervisor alert, not an escalation to that person). Whether an escalated assignee may read that one thread (audited, time-limited access) remains OPEN for the clinical lead, compliance and security (decision sheet E5); the interim is an engineering proposal and this ADR stays Proposed. Known edges of the interim: the view check runs before the guarded write, so a membership or consent change in those milliseconds can still route to someone who has just lost access (the old stall, not new exposure); `ladder_exhausted` does not say whether the cause was no coverage, nobody qualified or nobody able to view; a prolonged consent-seam outage makes the walk retry rather than decide.

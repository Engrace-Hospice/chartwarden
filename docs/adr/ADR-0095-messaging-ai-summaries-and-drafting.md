# ADR-0095 — Messaging AI thread summaries and reply drafting (M32 data boundary)

- **Status:** **Proposed** (2026-10-23). The M36 side is **built, flag off** (2026-10-24): routes, input minimisation, output validation, web panel, M32 port adapter, capability rows, read-only gate and live proof. It still needs clinical-lead and compliance sign-off (hazard H-08) before any tenant turns it on, and it **serves nothing**: the two M32 capabilities are `PROPOSED` with no backend, BAA or ZDR chosen, so every call answers "AI unavailable". One migration (1220, a permission); no table.
- **Date:** 2026-10-23
- **Phase:** Messaging Hub Phase 6, WP 6.5.
- **Related:** [ADR-0089](ADR-0089-messaging-export.md) and [ADR-0091](ADR-0091-messaging-smart-work-inbox.md) (ADR style), [ADR-0086](ADR-0086-messaging-retention-and-legal-hold.md) (legal hold), [ADR-0055](ADR-0055-message-revisions-and-redaction.md) (redaction), `docs/architecture/CHARTWARDEN_AI_GOVERNANCE_v0.2.md` sections 4, 5, 7.3-7.5, 8.4-8.6, 9, `docs/modules/CHARTWARDEN_MODULE_32_AI_PLATFORM_SPEC_v0_2.md`, `docs/messaging-hub/04-phases-4-to-6.md` row 6.5, `docs/messaging-hub/10-ui-spec-phases-4-to-6.md` section 6.5, `docs/messaging-hub/brief-clinical-lead-signoff.md`.

## Context

WP 6.5 wants a thread summary with source-message citations and uncertainty, and draft reply proposals. Message text is PHI, written by many people, and untrusted. Facts verified in the repo:

- M32 already has the invocation pipeline (`apps/backend/src/modules/32-ai-platform/invocation/service.ts`). Its gates, in order: `ai_full_flag`, `lifecycle`, `kill_switch`, `tenant_opt_in`, `clinician_opt_out`, `token_cap`, `phi_minimization`, `routing`. Every call writes an `ai_invocation` row (7-year partitioned ledger, prompt/response/chart-context encrypted, `zdr_confirmed`, `hitl_outcome`). `recordHitlOutcome` is the one post-hoc write. The WP 6.5 docs call this log `ai_interaction`; **the table is `ai_invocation`** and this ADR uses it (no second table).
- `AiInvokeRequest` takes `patientId`, `actorMembershipId`, `consumerContextRef`, `prompt`, `chartContext`, and optional `responseSchema`. It has no write capability: the pipeline returns text.
- The chart-context validator (`chart-context-rules.ts`) is an allowlist per capability and **denies every capability that has no ruleset**. Before this work no messaging capability existed, so every M36 call was refused. That default is kept: the two messaging capabilities now have a ruleset (`channelId`, `messageCount`) but are registered `PROPOSED` with `defaultBackendKey: null`, so `sync-ai-capability-catalog` does not seed them and the pipeline answers as for an unknown capability.
- Governance requires a BAA, Zero Data Retention for PHI traffic (section 9.2), tenant opt-in with BAA resolution (`tenant-opt-in.ts`), and HITL for High-risk capabilities. Backend seed rows leave `baa_reference` and `zdr_status` as `null`; "only a deployment knows them".
- M36 has legal hold (`governance/under-hold.ts`, migration 1138), approved redactions that mask bodies (`message-redaction-mask.ts`, WP 5.6) and a 42 CFR Part 2 channel gate (`part2-channel-gate.ts`).

## Decision

### 1. Authorization and input boundary (nothing from outside the thread)

1. One entry point, `POST /api/v1/messaging/channels/:id/summaries` (and `.../draft-replies`), in M36. M36 loads the input; M32 never queries messaging tables. The route is gated on a new `messaging.ai.use` permission AND the caller being a current member of the channel (the `channel_visibility` predicate at the route and the same `canViewChannel` the thread read uses, evaluated again in the service, including the Part 2 consent gate). **Built decision: the break-glass read variant is NOT used** (`channel_visibility_read` would let a break-glass grantee send a conversation they are not a member of to a model, a further disclosure the grant does not cover); a grantee gets the ordinary not-a-member answer (open decision 10). Optional body `thread_root_message_id` summarises one reply thread instead of the channel's recent messages. Companion routes: `POST .../draft-replies` (also requires `messaging.send` and a non-archived channel), `POST .../ai-outcomes` and `GET /api/v1/messaging/ai/status` (`{enabled}`: flag AND permission; the web hides the panel when false). Membership or ABAC lost between request and model call re-checks and fails closed (one re-check immediately before dispatch).
2. **Input is exactly the messages that caller could read in that thread now**: the output of the existing hydrated thread read (so redaction masking and deleted-message filtering come for free), bounded by a window (default last 50 messages or 14 days, whichever is smaller, constants in one file). No other thread, no chart data, no patient demographics, no attachments, no links, no polls, no mentions' profiles.
3. **Excluded, not just masked**: messages with an approved redaction, deleted messages, and messages under an active legal hold are NOT sent to the model (a hold means "preserve", and a held body may also be subject to a pending dispute; sending it to a vendor is a new disclosure we do not need). They appear to the model as a placeholder `[message withheld]` so citations and ordering stay truthful, and the UI shows "N messages not included". A channel whose patient carries the `sud` flag is refused unless M39 says the caller's release covers AI processing; v1 refuses all Part 2 channels (open decision 4): built as any care-team channel whose patient carries the `sud` flag, or whose flags cannot be read, answers the generic `AI_NOT_AVAILABLE_FOR_CHANNEL` (422) without saying why, and the model is never called.
4. **Minimum necessary** (governance 9.3): sender is replaced with a role token (`[RN]`, `[SW]`, `[MD]`, `[FAMILY]`) and a per-thread alias (`Sender A`); the patient is `[PATIENT]` (never name, MRN, DOB, address, phone); timestamps become relative ("2h before the last message"). The model needs content, not identity. Redaction is deterministic code (M36 side), not a prompt instruction. `chartContext` passed to M32 carries only `{ channelId, messageCount }`; an allowlist ruleset for `messaging_summarize` and `messaging_draft_reply` in M32 (`chart-context-rules.ts`) admits those keys and nothing else. Built detail: names of the patient (first, last, full, MRN) and of every person in the thread become `[PATIENT]` or that person's `Sender A [RN]`; phone, e-mail, URL, SSN, dates, street addresses and 6+ digit numbers become fixed tokens; bodies are cut at 1,200 characters and collapsed to one line so a body cannot forge a second message line; the delimiter strings are neutralised. A system-authored message, a message with no readable text (an attachment only) and an unreadable rich body are withheld like a redacted one. The caller's own display name is never sent; the real names are put back into the answer the caller reads, after the model, from the ids M36 itself sent.
5. `consumerContextRef` names the channel and the message-id range, so the ledger row can prove what the call covered. `patientId` is passed when the channel is patient-scoped, so section 3.2.8's PHI indicator and the disclosure accounting fire.

### 2. Summary output: citations and uncertainty, rendered deterministically

1. The model is asked for structured JSON via `responseSchema`: `{ points: [{ text, sourceMessageRefs: [string], confidence: "high"|"medium"|"low" }], openQuestions: [string], insufficientContext: boolean }`. The prompt gives each message an opaque ref `m1..mN` (not the real id). Validation failure: retry once, then show "AI unavailable" (governance 8.6).
2. **Citations cannot point outside the thread.** The model never emits real ids. M36 maps `mK` back to a message id from the exact list it sent; a ref not in that list (invented, or the ref of a withheld message) is dropped and the point is demoted to `low`; a point left with zero valid refs is discarded (built; the earlier "shown with no source" wording contradicted the discard rule and was dropped). The UI renders each citation as a link built by the server from the mapped id (`#message-<id>` inside the same channel view); no model-provided URL, markdown or HTML is ever rendered (output is plain text, escaped). A per-point check that the cited message is still readable by the caller is repeated at render time.
3. **Uncertainty is visible**: every point carries its confidence label; `insufficientContext` or fewer than 3 included messages renders "Too little to summarise"; withheld-message count is shown; the panel header is "AI summary - draft - not verified" (UI spec 6.5). The summary never states a clinical conclusion, diagnosis, dose or disposition; the prompt and an output check (denylist of order/dose/diagnosis phrasing) route such output to refuse-with-explanation (governance 7.5).

### 3. Draft replies: composer only, a human sends

1. A draft is returned to the browser and placed in the composer by an explicit "Copy to draft" click. It is never written to `messaging_message`, never to the drafts store, never auto-posted, never queued. The human edits and presses the normal send, which runs the normal send path (permissions, Part 2 gate, rate limits) as that human. `recordHitlOutcome` records `accepted`, `edited` or `rejected`.
2. A sent message that began as an AI draft carries no hidden marker in the clinical record (governance 7.3.1: AI provenance is audit only); the link is the `ai_invocation` row's hitl outcome.
3. Drafts may be offered only for channels where the caller could post anyway. Drafts never include attachments, mentions, or orders.

### 4. The AI cannot send, order, or write a chart record (structural)

1. M36's AI port is `MessagingAiPort { summarize(request); draftReply(request); recordOutcome(input) }`, declared in `packages/types/src/messaging-ai.ts` (built; so neither module gains a dependency edge on the other and both manifests are unchanged). M32 implements it (`32-ai-platform/seams/messaging-ai.ts`, a thin adapter over the one governed pipeline) and the boot passes it to M36 lazily. It returns TEXT; M36 parses and validates it. `recordOutcome` is the one human-decision write, guarded in the ledger update to the actor's own row, a messaging capability, this channel, and a still-open outcome (`pending` or `accepted`, so "accepted" may be refined to "edited" once). It has no method, parameter or injected dependency that can send a message, create a task, order, or chart entry. M36's AI folder (`36-messaging/ai/`) imports `@chartwarden/types` and type-only `@chartwarden/messaging`, plus the thread read through an injected method set (`canViewChannel`, `listMessages`, `listThread`), and nothing from sending, orders (M11/M12), encounters (M8), tasks or drafts.
2. **Gate (built)** `arch:ai-messaging-readonly` (`scripts/check-ai-messaging-readonly.ts`, in `arch:fitness`): (a) static: every file under `36-messaging/ai/` has an import allowlist (dependency-cruiser rule or AST scan) that forbids the send-path module, any `insert`/`update`/`delete` Drizzle call, `withOrgContext` write helpers, and seam registries for M7/M8/M11/M12/M22 writers; (b) the M32 capability rows for these two keys must have `requires_hitl = true` and no tool/function-calling feature in `supported_features`; (c) the live suite `m36-ai.live.test.ts` (`test:m36:ai:live`) runs summarise and draft calls with an injection payload over the real M32 pipeline and asserts the message, draft, scheduled-message, task, clinical-link and outbox tables are byte-identical and exactly one `ai_invocation` row per call. Built difference: (b) checks the registry rows (high risk, tenant opt-in and clinical review required, `PROPOSED`) and that the port contract and adapter declare no tool / function-calling / write field; `supported_features` belongs to the backend row, and no backend is chosen.
3. The model gets no tools. No function calling, no browsing.

### 5. Ledger, flag, environment

1. **Every call is logged** by the M32 pipeline in `ai_invocation` (including refusals where a backend can be named). M36 adds nothing parallel, except an M36 audit event (built as `m36.ai.summary_viewed`, the catalog's `m36.<resource>.<verb>` form; channel id, invocation id, caller, kind, counts; no text) so the thread's own audit trail shows the access. The WP 6.5 acceptance "ai row lands" is asserted live, as CLAUDE.md requires for read audits.
2. **Per-tenant flag off by default**, using M32's `tenant_opt_in` plus the `ai_full_flag`, plus a messaging-specific tenant feature flag `messaging.ai.enabled` (absent = off, the existing `feature_flag` mechanism, no migration) so an agency that opted into other M32 capabilities does not silently get messaging AI. Per-clinician opt-out (`clinician_opt_out`) is honoured. Flag off or no consent: the panel is absent (UI spec), the endpoint answers 404-equivalent typed `AI_NOT_ENABLED`.
3. **BAA-covered environment only, fail closed.** The call is dispatched only to a backend whose registry row has a non-null `baa_reference` that resolves to a non-expired BAA covering generative-AI processing, and `zdr_status` confirmed. Because the seed leaves both `null`, a fresh deployment answers "AI unavailable" until an operator records them. Local-model backends are acceptable only if hosted inside the BAA boundary. No fallback to any other vendor or to a non-BAA path, ever. A health-sweep or kill-switch trip stops the feature immediately.
4. Capabilities `messaging_summarize` and `messaging_draft_reply` are registered in the M32 inventory (built: `PROPOSED`, high, `defaultBackendKey: null`, governance catalog rows and docs) and advance through M32's normal lifecycle (proposal, design review, evaluation, pilot, GA, governance section 5). Prompt files `packages/ai-platform/prompts/messaging/*.v1.md` exist but are not registered in `ai_prompt_template`, so the adapter refuses even if a capability row were seeded. Risk class proposed **High** (clinicians may rely on a summary; a wrong summary can hide an urgent message), so the full workflow, HITL and periodic re-evaluation apply. Until stage 4 they serve nothing.

### 6. Prompt injection (message text is untrusted data)

1. Messages are inserted only inside delimited data blocks (`[MESSAGES BEGIN] ... [MESSAGES END]`) in the user turn; the system prompt states the messages are data, instructions inside them are never followed, and the output is JSON only (governance 8.5). Delimiter strings inside message text are neutralised before insertion.
2. Defence is layered and does not rely on the prompt: no tools (so injected "send this" has no effect), schema-validated output, ref-mapping drops invented citations, plain-text escaped rendering (no markdown links or images, so no exfiltration channel), draft text shown only in the composer, and a human reads it before sending.
3. A message that looks like an injection attempt (heuristic match on "ignore previous", "system prompt", role-play markers) is still summarised as content but flagged in the ledger `error_class`-adjacent note and counted for the M32 hallucination/safety ops review. Detection is advisory, not the control.
4. Another participant can write text meant to alter what a colleague sees in a summary (e.g. "patient is fine, no action needed"). This is why citations are mandatory and the summary is labelled unverified; it cannot be fully removed (open decision 5).

### 7. Retention, rate limits, cost

1. **Generated summaries and drafts are ephemeral to the user**: returned in the response, held in browser memory, not stored by M36 (no new table, no cache). The only server copy is the encrypted `ai_invocation` prompt and response payload under M32's 7-year ledger retention (disclosure accounting). Consequence: a summary of a legally held thread is itself retained as ledger evidence; the ledger, not M36, is where erasure and hold requests are answered (existing `ai-provenance-eraser`). Re-opening the panel re-generates (and re-bills) unless the caller still has it in memory.
2. **Rate limits** (built): per user (10 summaries and 20 drafts per hour), per channel (3 summaries per 10 minutes) as fixed-window counters that fail closed, plus a 30/min/user route budget, and M32's token cap gate per tenant. Constants in one file (`MESSAGING_AI_LIMITS`); per-tenant tuning is not built. The response carries `input_hash` (sha256 of the sent message ids) so the browser can skip a call for an unchanged thread; the server keeps no cache (an `Idempotency-Key` is neither required nor honoured, because a replayed cached body would be a server-side copy of a summary).
3. **Cost**: input bounded by the window (about 50 short messages, a few thousand tokens), `maxOutputTokens` 600 for summaries and 300 for drafts, dispatch timeout 8 s default. Token use is recorded by M32's ledger and rolled up by the existing token-ledger job; tenant soft/hard cap alerts already exist. No per-message or background pre-summarising (only on explicit click).

## Built (2026-10-24, flag off)

- **M36** `apps/backend/src/modules/36-messaging/ai/`: `pseudonymise.ts` (role tokens, aliases, scrubbing, relative times), `thread-input.ts` (window, exclusion, refs, hash), `ai-output.ts` (strict parse, citation mapping, plain text, denylist, name put-back), `ai-service.ts` (the ordered gates, quotas, one pre-dispatch re-check, retry once, audit), `binding.ts` (the read-only database and seam adapters), `unavailable-ai-port.ts` (the production default) and `fake-ai-port.ts` (tests only); routes `routes/ai-routes.ts` and `ai-typebox.ts`, mounted by `registerMessagingRoutes`.
- **M32** `seams/messaging-ai.ts` (the port adapter over the one pipeline), chart-context allowlist rows, the guarded `recordHitlOutcome`, inventory rows, catalog rows and docs; **boot** `onMessagingAiPort` binds it only when a model backend is configured and the prompt registry exists.
- **Contract** `packages/types/src/messaging-ai.ts`; permission `messaging.ai.use` (migration 1220, administrator and org_admin only); audit action `m36.ai.summary_viewed`; **web** `ai-summary-panel.tsx`, `use-ai-assist.ts`, header button.
- **Tests**: unit (input, output, service, adapter over the real pipeline with in-memory stores, gate, web panel with axe) and the live suite `m36-ai.live.test.ts` plus the tenant-IDOR suite rows. No external model is ever called; the only model in any test is an in-process scripted backend.

Differences from the proposal above, all deliberate: the port lives in `@chartwarden/types` (no M36 to M32 manifest edge); a break-glass grantee does not get AI; the Part 2 refusal is generic; the capabilities carry no backend (no vendor decision was made); "accepted" can be refined to "edited" once; the outcome is recorded as `rejected` automatically when the answer is discarded (invalid twice, or a dose / order / diagnosis was refused).

## Not done / out of scope

No vendor, region, model, BAA or ZDR is chosen; no `ai_prompt_template` row or backend row exists, so nothing is dispatched anywhere. Per-tenant tuning of the limits; a hold-aware or consent-aware policy (the held / redacted / Part 2 rules are the conservative ones); evaluation datasets and the M32 stage-3 evidence; the clinical lead's wording review. Summaries of audio, attachments, or multiple threads; auto-triage, auto-categorisation or sentiment; AI that proposes tasks or orders (a separate, much higher-risk decision); translation (ADR-0093 forbids runtime machine translation); dictation (WP 6.3 ADR).

## Open decisions (compliance / clinical lead sign-off)

1. Risk class and whether summaries may be offered at all for the clinical care-team channels versus admin-only channels first.
2. Whether legal-hold messages should be excluded (this ADR) or included with a hold-aware policy; counsel to decide. Same for redaction-pending messages.
3. Role-token pseudonymisation versus sending real sender names; whether "relative time" harms quality enough to revisit.
4. Part 2: refuse all SUD-flagged channels (this ADR) or allow with a consent that explicitly covers AI processing.
5. Residual risk that a colleague's text misleads a summary; required wording of the "not verified" label and whether summaries may be used to skip reading unread urgent messages (acknowledgment requests must never be satisfied by a summary).
6. 7-year retention of encrypted prompts that contain message text; whether messaging prompts need a shorter class than other capabilities.
7. Which vendor, region and ZDR terms; whether local models are required for some tenants.
8. Default limits (window, per-user, per-channel) and the tenant price/cap.
9. Whether drafts are in scope for GA or only summaries first (drafts add authorship-of-record questions).
10. Whether a break-glass grantee may ever use AI on the conversation they were granted (built: no).
11. Whether the denylist should refuse or merely flag an answer that restates a dose a colleague wrote.
12. Who holds `messaging.ai.use` (built: administrator and org_admin only; every clinical role needs the clinical lead's decision).

## Unverified

- The capability rows, ruleset, permission and flag now exist (see Built). Still unbuilt: any prompt template row, backend row, evaluation or stage-4 approval.
- Backend `baa_reference` and `zdr_status` are `null` in the seed; the BAA with the intended vendor is recorded in governance 9.2 as "in progress" and may be out of date.
- Resolved: the thread read exposes `legalHold` per message when asked (`showLegalHold`), and the AI binding asks for it only to EXCLUDE those messages. A hold with no selector covers the whole tenant, so while one is active nothing can be summarised.
- The "edited" outcome is inferred in the browser (the copied text changed before the box was cleared); a message sent unchanged stays "accepted". It is a signal, not proof.
- The dose / order / diagnosis denylist is regular expressions: it also refuses an answer that merely restates a dose a colleague wrote, and it cannot catch every phrasing. It is a second wall, not the control.
- That `responseSchema` structured output is honoured by the chosen backend, and the summary quality and hallucination rate, are unmeasured; M32 stage-3 evaluation is the evidence.
- Whether the `ai_invocation` encrypted payload can be erased on patient erasure while keeping the accounting row was not re-checked here (`erasers/ai-provenance-eraser.ts` is the starting point).
- Rate and cost figures above are starting guesses, not measured.
